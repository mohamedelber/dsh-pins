What Is a European Laundry?

A European laundry, or European style laundry, is a compact laundry set inside a cupboard, usually behind bifold, sliding or hinged doors. It holds the same things as a laundry room, a washing machine, often a dryer, sometimes a small tub, with shelves above, but takes up only one stretch of wall in a hallway, kitchen or bathroom. In the US the same idea is often called a laundry closet; our laundry closet ideas show how it works there.

How to Plan a European Laundry in 6 Steps

  1. Choose the spot. A hallway, kitchen or bathroom wall near existing water, drainage and power is cheapest to set up.
  2. Choose your appliances first. Decide on side-by-side, stacked or a combo machine before you design the cupboard around them.
  3. Check the dryer type. A vented dryer must be ducted outside; a heat pump or condenser dryer avoids that.
  4. Measure width, height and depth. Allow for hoses, the power point and doors that open fully.
  5. Plan the storage. Overhead cupboards, a hanging rail and hamper drawers keep the benchtop clear.
  6. Book licensed trades. New taps, drains and power points are jobs for a licensed plumber and electrician.

Layout Ideas

The layout decides how wide the cupboard has to be, so these European laundry ideas start there. Add doors and storage once the layout is fixed.

1. Washer and Dryer Side by Side Under a Benchtop

Placing the two machines side by side under one benchtop, like the hallway laundry at the top of this guide, gives you a folding surface and room for overhead cupboards. It needs the widest cupboard, about 1,200 to 1,300 mm for two standard machines, but it is the easiest layout to use every day. Check the benchtop height clears the top of your tallest machine.

2. Stack the Dryer on the Washer

Stacking the dryer on top of the washer with a stacking kit cuts the width to about 700 mm, so a laundry can fit into a narrow linen cupboard. Use the kit made for your machines so the dryer sits securely, and keep the dryer controls within easy reach. A narrow column of shelves beside the stack holds towels and detergent.

3. One Washer-Dryer Combo

If there is room for only one machine, a washer-dryer combo washes and dries in the same drum, so a single 600 mm space is enough. It suits small apartments and rentals. Combos dry smaller loads than a separate dryer, so they work best for small households.

4. A European Laundry in the Kitchen

Hiding the laundry behind a tall kitchen door uses the same water and drainage as the kitchen sink. Matching the laundry doors to the kitchen cabinetry makes it disappear, and a stacked pair takes the space of one tall pantry unit.

5. A Hallway or Bathroom Cupboard

A hallway cupboard keeps the noise away from living areas, and a bathroom cupboard puts the machines right next to the dirty washing. If you are working with a top-loading washer, remember the lid needs clearance to open, so it cannot sit under a fixed benchtop; our top loading washer guide explains how much space to leave.

Door Ideas

Doors make a European laundry. They need to open wide enough to load the machines and close neatly over them.

6. Bifold Doors

Bifold doors fold back to the sides and open the full width of the cupboard while taking up less floor in front than one wide hinged door. They suit hallways, where a single wide door would block the walkway.

7. Sliding Doors on a Top Track

Sliding doors need no space to swing at all, which makes them ideal for a laundry in a living area or a narrow hallway. The trade-off is that only half the cupboard is open at a time, so place the machines and the tub behind different doors.

8. Handleless Doors That Match the Walls

Floor-to-ceiling handleless doors painted the same colour as the walls, or a soft contrast like sage, hide the laundry completely. A slim vent grille at the top of the doors lets warm, damp air escape, which helps with any dryer.

Storage Ideas

A cupboard laundry has little room, so the best storage-focused European laundry ideas go up the wall and into drawers.

9. Overhead Cupboards and a Hanging Rail

Overhead cupboards above the benchtop hold detergent refills, cleaning products and spare towels, and a hanging rail underneath lets shirts dry or wait on hangers. Keep laundry pods and chemicals on the highest shelf, out of reach of children.

10. Pull-Out Hamper Drawers

Wide pull-out drawers with fabric hampers sort darks and lights out of sight. They work beside a single machine or under the tub. Our laundry cabinet ideas show more ways to plan the joinery.

11. A Slim Pull-Out for Detergent or an Ironing Board

A narrow pull-out, 150 to 200 mm wide, beside the machines holds detergent bottles, or a fold-out ironing board that slides away. It uses the gap that is often left over when the cupboard is wider than the appliances.

12. Open Shelves Beside a Stack

With stacked machines, a column of open shelves beside them holds folded towels, baskets and the peg bag. Use baskets on the lower shelves so the cupboard looks tidy when the doors are open. Our laundry organisation guide explains where each item should go.

Tub, Benchtop and Practical Details

13. A Compact Laundry Tub

A compact stainless steel tub set into the benchtop is handy for soaking, hand washing and rinsing muddy shoes. It adds about 400 to 500 mm to the width of a side-by-side layout. A wall-mounted mixer tap or one on the benchtop keeps the bench easy to wipe.

14. A Benchtop for Folding

Even a short benchtop over a single machine gives you somewhere to fold. Choose a moisture-resistant surface such as laminate, compact laminate or stone, and seal timber well if you love the look of oak.

15. Ventilation and the Right Dryer

A closed cupboard traps heat and moisture. Choose a heat pump or condenser dryer, or have a vented dryer ducted to the outside, as Energy Rating explains the National Construction Code requires. Add a vent grille or leave the doors open while machines run. Fire and Rescue NSW's home fire safety checklist also advises cleaning the dryer's lint filter every time you use it.

16. Waterproofing, Power and a Leak Tray

Laundries are wet areas under the National Construction Code, which covers waterproofing in wet areas such as bathrooms and laundries, so plan the floor and walls with your builder or a licensed waterproofer. A washing machine leak tray under the machine adds protection in a timber-floored hallway, and new taps, drains and power points must be installed by licensed trades.

Common European Laundry Mistakes

  • Designing the cupboard before choosing the machines. Appliance sizes vary, so buy or measure them first.
  • Forgetting the depth. Hoses, taps and the power point need room behind the machines.
  • A vented dryer in a closed cupboard. Duct it outside or choose a heat pump or condenser dryer.
  • Doors that cannot open fully. Check doors clear the machine doors and the hallway.
  • No storage. Without overhead cupboards, detergent ends up on the floor.
  • DIY plumbing or electrics. Use licensed trades for new water, drainage and power.

European Laundry FAQ

What is a European laundry?

A European laundry is a compact laundry hidden in a cupboard, usually with a washing machine, a dryer, shelves and sometimes a small tub, behind bifold, sliding or hinged doors. It takes up one wall instead of a whole room.

How deep does a European laundry need to be?

Allow about 650 to 700 mm of depth for most front loaders, so there is room for the hoses, taps and power point behind the machine and for the doors to close in front. Check your appliances' installation guides, because depths vary between models.

How big is a European laundry?

A side-by-side washer and dryer usually needs about 1,200 to 1,300 mm of width, or about 1,700 mm with a compact tub. Stacked machines need about 700 mm of width but about 1,800 mm of height. A single washer-dryer combo fits in about 600 mm.

Can you put a dryer in a European laundry?

Yes. Heat pump and condenser dryers suit a cupboard because they collect or drain the water instead of blowing damp air out. A vented dryer needs to be ducted to the outside, and any dryer needs its lint filter cleaned after every load.

The best European laundry ideas start with the appliances and the space, then add doors that suit the room and storage that goes up instead of out.
