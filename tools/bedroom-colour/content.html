The best bedroom colour ideas start with the light, not the paint chart. In a north-facing bedroom, warm shades such as plaster pink, terracotta and warm white stop the room feeling cold; in a south-facing one, cooler greens and blues stay fresh. Soft sage green is the safest all-rounder, and Farrow & Ball's Patrick O'Donnell says green is the most natural colour family for the bedroom. Below are 14 bedroom colour ideas from paint-brand colour experts and designers, with named shades, plus how to test them before you commit.

Start With Which Way Your Bedroom Faces

Dulux puts it simply: the natural light a room receives has a huge effect on colour. Its advice is warm pinks and yellows for north-facing rooms and cooler shades for south-facing ones. Tash Bradley, Lick's director of interior design, explains that north-facing rooms receive cooler, bluish light, which can make colors appear darker. For east and west-facing rooms, Dulux Heritage says it's all about the time of day and when you'll be using the space. Most of us use a bedroom early in the morning and at night, so judge colours at those times, under your own lamps.

A paint's light reflectance value (LRV) tells you how much light it bounces back, on a scale from 0 to 100. A high LRV helps in a dim room, but Bradley adds that light needs to be present for the paint to reflect it. That is why designers often go darker, not paler, in a small, gloomy bedroom.

Greens

1. Soft Sage Green

Sage is the colour most bedroom colour ideas come back to. Dulux named Soulful Sage its Colour of the Year 2027, and creative director Marianne Shillingford says its soft, silvery quality brings a sense of lightness. O'Donnell suggests Farrow & Ball Lichen for a relaxing bedroom across both walls and woodwork, and Little Greene's Ruth Mottershead calls Little Greene Sage Green a calming and sophisticated shade of green with a very soothing effect. On a budget, Crown's Mellow Sage is a restorative pale sage green paint. In her renovated Somerset home, Farrow & Ball colour curator Joa Studholme painted the walls and woodwork of one bedroom in Sap Green. Pair sage with white linen, oak and a rust-coloured throw, as in the illustration above.

2. Deep Forest or Olive Green

Darker greens make a bedroom feel enclosed and calm rather than bright. Chelsea Clark of Lust Home says dark, rich greens bring depth to a room; Ideal Home suggests Dulux Everglade Forest, and Crown's Woodland Wanderer is a rich, deep and calming green. For olive, designer Julia Baum says Farrow & Ball Bancha has a deep feel that creates a sense of calm in any space. Green also works in a box room bedroom, where a dark shade blurs the corners.

Blues

3. Soft, Changeable Blue

Valspar's Lucy Steele says blue is one of the colours most associated with calm. Pale blues shift with the light: when SheerLuxe visited Studholme's home in 2021, it found Farrow & Ball Light Blue in her triple-aspect second bedroom was positively blue in strong morning light but greyer by evening. Farrow & Ball Lulworth Blue No. 89 and Mellow Flow, one of Dulux's three 2026 Colours of the Year, which Ideal Home calls the perfect muted blue that calms and soothes, are softer options. In a south-facing room, Dulux also suggests the pale Nordic Sky.

4. Navy and Inky Blue

Designer Alice Bettington says darker blues are a wonderful choice for bedrooms as they evoke the night sky. O'Donnell says Farrow & Ball Hague Blue works a treat in north-facing rooms because of its green undertone. Mottershead recommends Little Greene Royal Navy, saying deep shades create depth and intimacy, and Crown's Midnight Navy is the high-street version. Navy looks best painted everywhere, which is idea 13.

Pinks, Whites and Warm Neutrals

5. Plaster Pink

O'Donnell's advice is direct: pink is a colour for those poorly lit north-facing bedrooms. His pick, Farrow & Ball Setting Plaster, was the colour of Studholme's small third bedroom when SheerLuxe visited in 2021. Charlotte Cosby, Farrow & Ball's head of creative, calls it perfect for enveloping north-facing spaces. Little Greene's earthier Split Pink and Lick Pink 02 are close alternatives; Bradley says Pink 02 offers exactly what a bedroom should feel like. Graham & Brown's Colour of the Year 2027, Rose Earth, is a warm, earthy pink shade with subtle brown undertones.

6. Warm White

White is not one colour. Mottershead says warm whites work particularly well in north-facing rooms, while cool whites can work well in south-facing rooms. She adds that a tinge of pink can also make whites feel warmer, naming Little Greene China Clay and Rolling Fog – Pale. Crown's Fresh Coconut is a delicate neutral, warm off white. Designer Katie Miller says Farrow & Ball Shaded White always looks different throughout the day, so sample it first.

7. Taupe, Stone and Mushroom

If you are moving away from grey, warm neutrals are the obvious replacement. Ideal Home reports that warm, characterful colours are driving sales in 2026 at Farrow & Ball, with Wimborne White its best seller and the brown Mouse's Back also topping the charts. Designer Adam Knight chose the darker Oxford Stone for a bedroom, saying it promotes rest, comfort, and calm. Little Greene's Masquerade is a lighter warm neutral Mottershead suggests for bedrooms.

Deep and Earthy Colours

8. Terracotta and Clay

Cosby says Farrow & Ball Red Earth is the perfect shade for embracing a warmer color palette in a north-facing living room, and the same logic applies to a bedroom. James Sirett of Fired Earth says terracotta has a reassuring earthiness, but Annie Sloan warns that deep terracotta can look muddy in a dark north-facing room, so test a sample there. Terracotta also works lower down the wall (idea 14).

9. Aubergine and Plum

Farrow & Ball says in bedrooms, Brinjal feels dramatic yet grounding; Brinjal No. 222 is a deep, wine-coloured purple. Mylands suggests its Plum Tree No. 283 behind the bed, saying it's comforting and reassuring, and the paler, grey-lilac Lavender Garden No. 30, which Mylands says makes a wonderfully serene ambience in a bedroom, if a dark purple feels too much.

10. Deep Red

Red sounds stimulating, but a dark, brown-based red can feel enclosing. Mottershead suggests Little Greene Baked Cherry for all-over bedroom schemes, creating that cosy, cocooning quality. Keep it to walls you see from bed, not the wall you face when you wake, if you find it too strong.

11. Soft Black

O'Donnell says Farrow & Ball Railings can be a surprisingly successful option in a naturally poorly lit or small room. Andy Greenall of Paint & Paper Library says black helps to create a calm, intimate ambience. Warm it up with wood, brass and soft white bedding.

12. Ochre Yellow, Used Carefully

Yellow divides opinion. Mottershead suggests Little Greene Bassoon, an ochre, to help us feel uplifted, happy and energised. Colour expert Karen Haller, who worked with bed maker Rest Assured on its 2019 research, says to avoid yellow in the bedroom. If you love it, choose a muted ochre over a bright lemon, and try it on a headboard wall first.

Ways to Use Colour in a Bedroom

13. Colour Drench the Whole Room

Colour drenching means you take one colour and paint everything in that room in the same one colour: walls, ceiling, skirting, doors and window frames. Bradley says the result is that you don't notice the edges of the room. Studholme takes Farrow & Ball Stirabout over the walls and ceiling of her own main bedroom. Use a flat or low-sheen eggshell on the woodwork so it reads as one colour. Designer Lizzie Green says drenching is hugely effective in making a space feel bigger.

14. Two-Tone Walls

Painting the lower part of the wall in a deeper colour grounds the bed and makes the ceiling feel higher. One Dulux example shown by Ideal Home paints roughly the bottom two-thirds of the wall in Mellow Flow and the top third off-white. Keep the line straight with low-tack masking tape, and set it just above the headboard or at picture-rail height. Panelling does the same job: our wainscoting calculator and board and batten calculator work out the spacing.

To pull a bedroom colour scheme together, use the 60-30-10 rule. Ben Thomson of Promain describes it as allocating 60% of a room's colour to the dominant hue, 30% to a secondary colour and 10% to an accent, which Valspar's Sarah Lloyd says goes into soft furnishings such as throws, pillows, artwork and accessories. Interior designer Emma Deterding stresses there are no hard and fast rules.

How to Test and Paint

  • Sample big. Farrow & Ball says to paint two coats of sample pot on card, the bigger the better, and check your samples over the course of a day or two. For a bedroom, close the curtains and look at them under a lamp too. The Paint Shed suggests ideally A5 or bigger and moving the card around the room.
  • Pick a finish. Matt or flat emulsion is more forgiving of uneven walls than a sheen finish. Farrow & Ball Estate Emulsion has a 2% sheen and is wipeable but not washable; Modern Emulsion (7%) is tougher. Designer Claire Garner says my go-to paint finish for walls and ceilings is always matt.
  • Buy enough. Dulux Matt covers up to 14m2/litre per coat, and most colour changes need two coats. So a 5-litre tin gives two coats on up to about 35 m² at best-case coverage, enough for the roughly 31 m² of wall in a 3.5 m square bedroom with a 2.4 m ceiling once a door and window are taken off. Measure yours.
  • Ventilate. UK rules limit matt interior wall paint to 30 g/l of VOCs (solvents) when ready to use. The HSE's advice is simple: if indoors open doors and windows. Paint early in the day so the room can air before you sleep in it.

What Is the Best Bedroom Colour for Sleep?

There is no proven sleep colour. The most quoted figures come from commercial surveys, not trials: an undated Travelodge survey of 2,000 British homes (reported in 2017) found 58% of Britons with a blue wall colour reported waking up feeling happy, and Rest Assured's 2019 research put soft green as the most restful. Treat them as opinion polls. NHS advice for better sleep is to make sure your bedroom is dark and quiet and to avoid screens before bed, because the bright light makes you more awake. So choose a colour you find calming, add blackout lining and warm bedside lamps, and the paint will do its part. Our small bedroom storage ideas help with the clutter that keeps many of us awake.

If You Only Do One Thing

Buy three sample pots before you buy any paint: one warm, one cool and one deep shade from the ideas above. Paint each on a large card, look at them first thing in the morning and again at night under your lamps, and choose the one you still like on day two.
