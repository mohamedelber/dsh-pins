# Desk Height Calculator: Sources and Facts

Researched 2026-10-20. Every quote below was read on the live page through WebFetch. Most were checked a second time with an exact-match test.
[2+] means 2 or more sources agree. [1] means only one source says it.
Some pages could not be read (see "Gaps" at the end).

---

## 1. Standard desk height, and why one height does not fit everyone

- **The usual US desk is 28-30 in (71-76 cm) tall** [2+]
  - Cornell University Ergonomics Web, "Ergonomic Guidelines for arranging a Computer Workstation - 10 steps for users". https://ergo.human.cornell.edu/ergoguide.html
    > "between 28"-30" above the floor (suitable for most adults)"
  - Autonomous, "Standard Desk Height Chart by Your Height in Inches". https://www.autonomous.ai/ourblog/complete-guide-to-ergonomic-desk-height
    > "The standard desk height is 29 to 30 inches, and it fits almost nobody."
  - FlexiSpot UK, desk height buying guide. https://flexispot.co.uk/blog/how-to-choose-the-ideal-desk-height-buying-guide
    > "a standard desk height falls within the range of 28 to 30 inches (71-76cm)"

- **UK/EU standard: BS EN 527-1:2011 sets desk sizes and is still current** [1]
  - BSI Knowledge. https://knowledge.bsigroup.com/products/office-furniture-work-tables-and-desks-dimensions
    > "This European Standard specifies dimensions of work tables and desks for office tasks"
    - The page lists the status as "Current". It does NOT show the height numbers, because the standard is paywalled.
  - Coolblue's summary of the Dutch version (NEN 527). This is a retailer, not the standard itself. https://www.coolblue.nl/en/advice/what-is-a-nen-527-and-npr-1813-standard.html
    > Fixed sit desk: "Height from 74cm up to 76cm"
    > Adjustable sit desk: "Height from 65cm up to 85cm"
    > Adjustable sit-stand desk: "Height from 65cm up to 125cm"
  - Advice: say "about 72-76 cm" in UK copy. Do not quote an exact EN 527 number unless you can see the standard.

- **Why one height does not fit everyone** [2+]
  - Ergoweb, "29 inch desk height revisited". https://ergoweb.com/29-inch-desk-height-revisited/
    > "29 inches actually represents a one-size-fits-all, significant compromise."
    > "95th percentile seated elbow height USA males"
    > "It's easier and cheaper to manufacture static workstations."
  - Autonomous (same page as above):
    > "your correct desk height is your seated elbow height, which runs from about 22 inches at 5'0" to 29 inches at 6'4"."
  - OSHA Computer Workstations eTool, Desks. https://www.osha.gov/etools/computer-workstations/components/desks
    > "Desk surfaces that are too high or too low may lead to awkward postures,"

## 2. The sitting rule: elbows, forearms, feet, thighs

- **Keyboard and desk at (or just below) elbow height** [2+]
  - HSE (UK), "Working safely with display screen equipment" (good posture). https://www.hse.gov.uk/msd/dse/good-posture.htm
    > "Keyboard just below elbow height"
  - Mayo Clinic, "Office ergonomics: Your how-to guide". https://www.mayoclinic.org/healthy-lifestyle/adult-health/in-depth/office-ergonomics/art-20046169
    > "your hands at or slightly below the level of your elbows."
  - UCSF Ergonomics Program, "Work Surface and Monitor Heights" (hosted by UC Office of the President). https://ucop.edu/safety-and-loss-prevention/_files/ergonomics/work-surface-and-monitor-heights.pdf
    > "Position your input devices at elbow height"
  - Autonomous Desk Height Calculator (UK page). https://www.autonomous.ai/en-GB/desk-height-calculator
    > "Your desk should sit at your elbow height, so your forearms rest parallel to the floor with your shoulders relaxed."

- **Elbows at about 90 degrees or a bit more open** [2+]
  - OSHA eTool, Good Working Positions. https://www.osha.gov/etools/computer-workstations/positions
    > "Elbows stay in close to the body and are bent between 90 and 120 degrees."
  - Cornell (ergoguide page):
    > "at or greater than 90 degrees to avoid nerve compression at the elbow."
  - Newcastle Hospitals NHS Trust, 1-Minute Desk Check leaflet. https://newcastle-hospitals.nhs.uk/wp-content/uploads/2021/11/2024-Newcastle-OHS-1-Minute-Desk-Check-leaflet_PRINT_ONLY.pdf
    > "Is the chair high enough that your elbows are at 90 degrees or more when typing?"

- **Forearms roughly parallel to the floor (horizontal)** [2+]
  - OSHA (positions page):
    > "Hands, wrists, and forearms are straight, in-line and roughly parallel to the floor."
  - HSE, INDG36 "Working with display screen equipment". https://hse.gov.uk/pubns/indg36.PDF
    > "Forearms should be approximately horizontal and the user's eyes should be the same height as the top of the screen."

- **Feet flat on the floor, or on a footrest** [2+]
  - Mayo Clinic:
    > "Adjust the height of the chair so that your feet rest flat on the floor."
  - OSHA eTool, Chairs. https://www.osha.gov/etools/computer-workstations/components/chairs
    > "The seat should be comfortable and allow your feet to rest flat on the floor or footrest."
  - Cornell (ergoguide page):
    > "Also check that the feet can be placed flat on the floor or on a footrest."
  - HSE INDG36:
    > "A footrest may be helpful, particularly for smaller users."

- **Thighs about parallel to the floor** [2+]
  - Mayo Clinic:
    > "Or use a footrest so your thighs are parallel to the floor."
  - OSHA (positions page):
    > "Thighs and hips are supported and generally parallel to the floor."

- **If the desk is the wrong height: raise the chair and add a footrest** [2+]
  - OSHA (desks page):
    > "If the work surface cannot be lowered, raise the chair to accommodate the user."
  - Mayo Clinic: if the desk is too low, "put sturdy boards or blocks under the desk legs to raise it." (This was quoted as a fragment. It was not exact-match checked.)

- **Leg room under the desk** [1]
  - OSHA (desks page):
    > "Clearance for the legs, under the desktop, should generally be between 20-28 inches (50-72 cm) high."

- **How to measure your own desk height** [2+]
  - UCSF Mission Hall, "Finding the Right Desk Height". https://missionhall.ucsf.edu/sites/g/files/tkssra981/f/FindingRightDeskHeightMH.pdf
    > "Select the closest desk height option that matches your elbow height."
  - Inch Calculator, Desk Height Calculator. https://www.inchcalculator.com/desk-height-calculator/
    > "align the height of the desk with your elbows while seated"

- **Formula tied to body height**: OSHA, Cornell, HSE and Mayo give NO formula. They all use your own elbow height.
  - Calculators base their numbers on body-size survey data, for example the Uplift Desk Ergonomic Calculator. https://checkout.upliftdesk.com/ergonomic-calculator/
    > "These measurements use an average of male and female body dimensions."
    > "Data is based on the 1988 Anthropometric Survey of United States Army Personnel."
  - Advice: present any height-to-desk table as an estimate. Tell readers to check it against their own elbow height.

- **Published table: seated desk and chair by person height** [1]
  - Inch Calculator, "Suggested Ergonomic Desk Heights" table. https://www.inchcalculator.com/desk-height-calculator/
  - These values come from the page's table, as WebFetch reported them (sample rows only):

    | Person height | Seated desk | Seated chair | Standing desk |
    |---|---|---|---|
    | 5'0" | 22"-24" | 13"-15" | 36"-38" |
    | 5'4" | 23"-25" | 15"-17" | 38"-40" |
    | 5'8" | 25"-27" | 16"-18" | 41"-43" |
    | 6'0" | 26"-28" | 17"-19" | 43"-45" |
    | 6'4" | 28"-30" | 19"-21" | 46"-48" |

  - These numbers roughly match Autonomous: "about 22 inches at 5'0" to 29 inches at 6'4"" [2+ for the overall range].
  - Autonomous calculator, UK page:
    > "For sitting, most adults land between 24 and 30 inches (61 to 76 cm)."

## 3. Standing desk height

- **Standing desk goes at standing elbow height (about 90 degrees)** [2+]
  - Bellevue College, "Standing Desk Setup" (content from StartStanding.org). https://www.bellevuecollege.edu/wp-content/uploads/sites/395/2020/06/Standing-Desk-Setup.pdf
    > "Table height approximately at standing elbow height."
  - University of Pennsylvania EHRS, Adjustable Height Workstation Fact Sheet. https://ehrs.upenn.edu/sites/default/files/2018-12/Standing%20fact%20sheet.pdf
    > "the table height should be adjusted so that shoulders are relaxed and elbows remain at approximately 90 degrees"
  - Autonomous UK page (the "elbow height" quote in section 2 covers standing too).

- **Typical standing range is about 36-48 in (91-122 cm). Most adults land at 38-47 in.** [2+]
  - Autonomous calculator, UK page:
    > "For standing, between 38 and 46 inches (97 to 117 cm)."
  - Autonomous chart article:
    > "Sitting desk height is typically 28 to 30 inches; standing desk height is typically 38 to 47 inches."
  - Inch Calculator table (above): 36"-38" at 5'0" up to 46"-48" at 6'4".

- **Standing table by person height (height includes shoes)** [1]
  - Autonomous, "Standard Desk Height Chart by Your Height in Inches". https://www.autonomous.ai/ourblog/complete-guide-to-ergonomic-desk-height
  - WebFetch reported these values. The 5'4" and 6'0" rows were checked twice.

- **Add shoe height** [2+]
  - Uplift calculator:
    > "Always add your shoe height to your height"
  - Autonomous: its chart is labelled height "with shoes".

## 4. Chair seat height and keyboard tray

- **Seat height should adjust within about 15-22 in (ANSI/HFES 100-2007)** [2+]
  - University of California, Task Chair Requirements. https://uhs.berkeley.edu/sites/default/files/uc_task_chair_requirements_1.pdf
    > "Seat height is adjustable at least 4.5 inches within the range of 15 - 22 inches from the floor"
  - Knoll, Remix Ergo Checklist. https://www.knoll.com/media/86/639/Remix-Ergo-Checklist.pdf
    > "Must have seat pan height adjustment of at least 4.5" in the range between 15-22" above the floor."
  - Colorado Mesa University, ergonomics purchasing for office seating. https://www.coloradomesa.edu/human-resources/documents/wellness-and-safety/ergonomics-purchasing-office-seating.pdf
    > "Within the height range of 15 to 22 inches from the floor"
  - NOTE: I could not verify a "16-21 in BIFMA" seat range. The verified range comes from ANSI/HFES 100, not BIFMA. BIFMA X5.1 covers chair safety and strength:
    - IISE webcast, "Office Institutional Seating Standards". https://www.iise.org/uploadedFiles/Webcasts/Public/Office_Institutional_Seating_Standards.pdf
      > "ANSI/BIFMA X5.1 and X5.4 cover occupants up to the 95th percentile male for safety and performance."

- **Chair fit check** [2+]
  - OSHA (chairs page):
    > "The chair height is appropriate when the entire sole of the foot can rest on the floor"
  - HSE good posture:
    > "Gap of 2-3 cm between front of seat bottom and back of knee"

- **Keyboard tray** [2+]
  - Cornell (ergoguide page):
    > "An ideal keyboard arrangement is to place this on a height adjustable negative-tilt tray."
  - Cornell, "Sitting and Standing at Work". https://ergo.human.cornell.edu/CUESitStand.html
    > "Sit using a height-adjustable, downward titling keyboard tray for the best work posture,"
    - "titling" is the spelling on the page.

## 5. Monitor height and distance

- **Top of the screen at or slightly below eye level** [2+]
  - OSHA eTool, Monitors. https://www.osha.gov/etools/computer-workstations/components/monitors
    > "The top of the monitor should be at or slightly below eye level."
  - Mayo Clinic:
    > "The top of the screen should be at or slightly below eye level."
  - HSE good posture (UK):
    > "Top of screen level with eyes, about an arm's length away"
  - Cornell (ergoguide page):
    > "a user's eyes should be in line with a point on the screen about 2-3" below the top of the monitor casing"
  - Mayo Clinic, for bifocal wearers: "lower the monitor an additional 1 to 2 inches (about 2 to 5 centimeters)." [1]

- **Distance about an arm's length, at least 20 in (50 cm)** [2+]
  - Mayo Clinic:
    > "Place the computer monitor straight in front of you, directly behind your keyboard, about an arm's length away from your face"
    > "The monitor should be no closer to you than 20 inches (about 50 centimeters)."
  - Cornell (ergoguide page):
    > "which usually is around an arms length"
  - Newcastle Hospitals NHS leaflet:
    > "Screen distance approximately arm's length"
  - OSHA (monitors page):
    > "Generally, the preferred viewing distance is between 20 and 40 inches (50 and 100 cm)"
  - UCSF:
    > "Monitor distance (18-30”) should allow comfortable viewing without forward leaning."
  - Advice: write "about an arm's length, at least 20 in / 50 cm". The upper limit differs by source: 30 in for UCSF, 40 in for OSHA.

## 6. Kids' desks (optional)

- **Children need a raised seat and a footrest** [1]
  - Cornell, children's computer ergonomics. https://ergo.human.cornell.edu/cuchildcomp.html
    > "Young children need a chair that raises them to the correct height for computer use"
    > "provides a stable platform for their feet"
  - I found no reliable table of kids' desk height by age. Use the same elbow-height rule.
  - BIFMA X6.1 school seat sizes (IISE webcast, see section 4):
    > "Size A: Seat Height < 13.9 inches / User Weight 75 lbs. (age 6)"
    - This is a chair-size standard, not a desk guide.

## 7. Sitting and standing: change posture often

- **Cornell's 20-8-2 rule** [2+]
  - Cornell, "Sitting and Standing at Work". https://ergo.human.cornell.edu/CUESitStand.html
    > "then every 20 minutes stand for 8 minutes AND MOVE for 2 minutes."
  - UPenn fact sheet:
    > "8 minutes of standing, and 2 minutes of movement during 30 minutes of work time"

- **Standing still all day is not the answer. Movement matters.** [2+]
  - Cornell (ergoguide page):
    > "But standing in a static posture is even more tiring than sitting in a static posture, so movement is important."
  - UPenn fact sheet:
    > "It is important to change position during the day."

- **Short, frequent breaks** [2+]
  - HSE INDG36:
    > "Short, frequent breaks are better than longer, infrequent ones."
  - Cornell (ergoguide page):
    > "every 30 to 60 minutes you should take a brief rest break."

## Gaps and warnings

- **NHS (nhs.uk "how to sit correctly")**: the page could not be read (fetch error, then the permission request timed out). I used the Newcastle Hospitals NHS Trust leaflet instead.
- **OSHA keyboards page** (https://www.osha.gov/etools/computer-workstations/components/keyboards): blocked (permission request timed out). It was not used.
- **BIFMA "16-21 in" seat range**: not verified. Use 15-22 in (ANSI/HFES 100) instead.
- **EN 527 exact heights**: the standard is paywalled. The only figures found come from a retailer summary (Coolblue).
- **Height tables** (Autonomous, Inch Calculator): these are retailer and calculator estimates, not official guidance. Their values were read by an automated fetch tool. Spot-check them in a browser before publishing.
- **Sources disagree on screen height**: HSE (UK) says the top of the screen should be level with the eyes. OSHA and Mayo say at or slightly below eye level.
