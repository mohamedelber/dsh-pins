Choosing between a chest freezer and an upright freezer? A chest freezer wins on price and running costs, and an upright wins on floor space and organization. ENERGY STAR says a chest model is usually more energy efficient because the door opens from the top, and Consumer Reports says chest freezers cost less to buy. An upright fits a smaller footprint and keeps food on shelves where you can see it.

  • Pick a chest freezer if you buy in bulk, have floor space in a basement or garage, and want the lowest running cost.
  • Pick an upright freezer if floor space is tight, you hate digging for food, or bending down is hard.
  • Either works as a small second freezer for a household of two.

Chest Freezers: Best for Bulk Buying and Low Bills

Best for: large families, hunters and bulk shoppers, and anyone with space in a basement or garage.

A chest freezer holds more for the money and lets less cold air escape when you open the lid. Consumer Reports notes a 22 cubic foot chest takes up a 2x6-foot area, against a 2½ x 2½ foot area for an upright of the same size, and shelves and bins in uprights take up more space than in chest freezers, up to 20 percent. A 7 cu ft chest such as GE's FCM7SKWW measures about 37.5 x 21.75 inches and rises to about 53 inches with the lid open, so leave headroom above it.

Avoid if: you want to find things quickly. Consumer Reports says chest freezers can be harder to organize even with hanging baskets, and most need defrosting by hand. Whirlpool advises defrosting when frost has built up to about 1/4 inch, and at least once a year. Sorting food into bins by type, with the newest at the bottom, makes it easier.

Upright Freezers: Best for Small Spaces and Easy Access

Best for: kitchens, laundry rooms and apartments, and anyone who wants food at eye level.

An upright freezer works like a refrigerator: shelves, drawers and door bins keep everything in sight. Frigidaire's 13 cu ft frost-free upright is 27.75 inches wide and designed to run in temperatures from 0°F to 110°F. Many uprights are frost-free, which is convenient, but ENERGY STAR says manual defrost freezers use half the energy of automatic defrost models, and uprights use more electricity overall.

Avoid if: running costs come first, or you store large, awkward items such as whole turkeys. If you need more kitchen space for the overflow, our kitchen storage ideas and pantry organization ideas can help.

How to Size and Place Either One

  • Size: Home Depot suggests 2.5 to 3 cubic feet of space per person, so about 12 cu ft for a family of four.
  • Space: leave room for air. A Frigidaire chest freezer spec sheet asks for 3" minimum clearance on all sides and ENERGY STAR says to leave a few inches between the freezer and the wall.
  • Temperature: set it to 0°F. The Department of Energy says a freezer should be set at 0 degrees Fahrenheit.
  • Garage: ENERGY STAR recommends keeping a freezer indoors unless your climate is mild. If it must go in a garage, choose a model rated for garage temperatures and check the range on the spec sheet.

Power Outages and Safety

A full freezer holds its cold longer. The USDA says a full freezer will keep its temperature for about 48 hours, or about 24 hours if half full. Keep the freezer door closed as much as possible. If you have an old latch-style chest freezer (made roughly 1945 to 1970) you no longer use, the Consumer Product Safety Commission has warned that children have suffocated in them and advises people to properly dispose of these non-working freezers immediately or disable the latch if disposal is impossible.

Our Pick

  • Family of four with a basement: a 12 to 15 cu ft chest freezer with baskets and bins.
  • Apartment or small kitchen: a compact upright, which needs the least floor space.
  • Garage: whichever type you choose, buy a garage-ready model.
  • Bad back: an upright, so most food is on shelves you can reach without leaning into a deep chest.

Decide in Five Questions

  1. Do you have a 6-foot stretch of floor for a large freezer? No: upright.
  2. Do you buy meat or produce in bulk? Yes: chest.
  3. Is the lowest electric bill your top priority? Yes: chest.
  4. Do you open the freezer several times a day? Yes: upright.
  5. Will it live in a garage? Yes: either, but garage-rated.
