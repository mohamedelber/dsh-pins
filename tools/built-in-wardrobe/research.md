## 2. Verified facts (quote only these)

**Depth**
- "We recommend a minimum of 600mm if you want hanging rails". Reason given: carcass back and doors are each 18 mm. Source: online-bedrooms.co.uk/blogs/be-inspired/how-to-build-fitted-wardrobe-in-alcove
- "Standard depth of a wardrobe/closet is normally 600-650mm"; "minimum … could be as little as 500mm". Source: thelittledesigncorner.com/blog/wardrobe-design-tips
- Hinged: "The normal depth of this kind of wardrobe should be 60cm"; "a minimum of 55cm if you are not planning to hang any clothes". Sliding: "a depth of 66cm plus a running track of about 5cm" (table: 710 mm total recommended). Source: fcilondon.co.uk/blog/how-deep-should-built-in-wardrobes-be
- Sliding doors: "We recommend a minimum of 650mm for the wardrobe's depth". Source: hafele.co.uk/en/info/about-haefele/latest-news/how-to-measure-for-sliding-wardrobe-doors/185093
- Idealhome FAQ, attributed to Hammonds: "The minimum internal wardrobe depth for hanging clothes is 55cm"

**Hanging, shelves and drawers**
- "Allow about 1900mm (H) for long hanging space"; "about 950mm (H) for short hanging space"; consider "a 3/4 hanging level". Source: thelittledesigncorner.com
- FCI table, single hanging rail height: min 1750 / recommended 1800 / luxury 1900 mm. Lower rail of a double hang: 850 / 900 / 950 mm. Shelf spacing: 300 / 350–400 / 400–450 mm
- "shelving and drawers are normally finished to a depth of 450-500mm"; "Shoe shelves are normally around 300-350mm"; drawer bank to "a finished height of around 900-1100mm"; "Keep your shelves adjustable". Source: thelittledesigncorner.com

**Doors**
- Hinged door: "not be wider than 60cm, or the hinges will be overloaded". Sliding panel: "should not be wider than 120cm" (900–1,000 mm recommended). A two-panel sliding run needs "at least 210cm". Source: FCI
- Hinged door: "maximum width … around 600-650mm". Source: thelittledesigncorner.com
- **Not found:** a mm figure for clearance in front of hinged doors. Don't invent one. Say the doors must open past the bed and other furniture, which is what both sources say. FCI's walk-in aisle figure is "at least 900mm".

**Alcove / chimney breast / loft**
- Measure alcove height "in 3 different places" and use the tallest. Measure width at three heights and take "the smallest". Round down to the nearest 50 mm, then deduct 100 mm for scribe panels. Example: 2637 → 2600 → 2500 mm. Ignore skirting and coving. Source: online-bedrooms.co.uk alcove guide
- Shallow alcoves can leave "half a wardrobe extending into the room". The fix is "pull-out racks and drawers". Source: hammonds-uk.com alcove blog
- Alcove wardrobes suit "the small spaces on either side of a fireplace or chimney wall". Source: wrenkitchens.com
- "Loft spaces and built in wardrobes go together like bread and butter". Alcoves can be dark, so add LED strip lighting. Source: realhomes.com
- Idealhome, attributed to Sharps: "Chimney breasts can become a feature or hidden"; "eaves can be turned into storage spaces"

**IKEA PAX (ikea.com/gb)**
- "PAX Wardrobe frame, dark grey, 100x58x236 cm", £90. Depth 58.0 cm. Needs a ceiling of at least 237 cm to assemble upright, or 240 cm with a sliding door. Source: ikea.com/gb/en/p/-10509125
- "PAX Wardrobe frame, dark grey, 50x58x201 cm", £70. Needs 202 cm, or 205 cm with sliding doors. Source: ikea.com/gb/en/p/-50509128
- Both pages: doors and interiors are sold separately, and the frame must be fixed to the wall (tipping hazard)
- PAX sliding-door combinations are listed as 150x66x236 cm, but only on IKEA TR/GR, not GB. Use only as "about 66 cm, per IKEA listings" or leave it out.

**Draft FAQs**

**How much does it cost to put in a built-in wardrobe?**
Which? (August 2026) puts a professionally installed modular three-door wardrobe at about £2,900, and a four-door at about £4,900. Bespoke three-door builds run from £1,500 to £7,000. MyBuilder quotes £400–£800 per linear metre for mid-range work. Flatpack you fit yourself starts nearer £500. (51 words)

**Is it cheaper to build a wardrobe or buy one?**
Doing it yourself is usually cheaper. Which? lists DIY flatpack three-door wardrobes from £500, against about £2,900 installed. MyJobQuote says a joiner typically charges £20–£25 an hour and a fit takes one to nine days. IKEA PAX frames start at £70, but doors and interiors are extra. (50 words)

**How deep should a built-in wardrobe be?**
Allow at least 600 mm from the wall if you want a hanging rail. Online Bedrooms recommends 600 mm because the carcass back and the doors are each 18 mm thick. Sliding doors need more room: Häfele recommends at least 650 mm. IKEA PAX frames are 58 cm deep before doors. (51 words)

**What heights should rails and shelves be?**
One design guide allows about 1,900 mm for long hanging and about 950 mm for short hanging. FCI London recommends a single rail at 1,800 mm, and a lower double-hang rail at around 900 mm. It suggests 350–400 mm between shelves. Keep shelves adjustable. (46 words)

**How do I fit a wardrobe into an alcove?**
Measure the height in three places and use the tallest. Measure the width at three heights and use the smallest. Round down to the nearest 50 mm, then subtract 100 mm for scribe panels so the frame can be trimmed to uneven walls. Ignore skirting and coving when measuring. (49 words)
