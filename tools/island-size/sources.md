# Kitchen Island Size Calculator: Sources

Researched 2026-10-20. [2+] = two or more sources agree. [1] = one source only.

How the quotes were checked: direct downloads were blocked, so every quote below was checked with WebFetch. I asked it to confirm each string word for word, and each one came back as an exact match. Curly versus straight quote marks and the inch symbol (″ versus ") may differ from the page. Before publishing, open the 3 to 4 quotes you rely on most and check them by eye.

---

## 1. NKBA aisles, walkways and clearance behind seats

Source: NKBA, "Kitchen Planning Guidelines with Access Standards" (PDF). URL: https://kb.nkba.org/uploads/2022/05/Kitchen-Planning-Guidelines.pdf

- Work aisle, 42 in for one cook and 48 in for more than one [2+]
  - NKBA: "The width of a work aisle should be at least 42″ (1067 mm) for one cook and at least 48″ (1219 mm) for multiple cooks."
  - JLC Online (Morton Block), https://www.jlconline.com/remodeling/guidlines-for-kitchen-island-functionality: "the recommended work aisle width is at least 42 inches for one cook and at least 48 inches for multiple cooks"
  - CRD Design Build, https://www.crddesignbuild.com/blog/kitchen-dimensions-code-requirements-nkba-guidelines: "The width of a work aisle should be at least 42 inches for one cook and at least 48 inches for multiple cooks."
  - Homes & Gardens says the same in plain words (see section 3): "42 inches (3.5 feet / 1.07 metres): comfortable for one cook"
- Walkway, 36 in [2+]
  - NKBA: "The width of a walkway should be at least 36″ (914 mm)."
  - Homes & Gardens: "36 inches (3 feet / 0.9 metres): absolute minimum"
- Clearance behind seated diners [2+, NKBA and CRD agree]
  - No traffic behind the seat. NKBA: "allow 32″ (813 mm) of clearance from the counter/table edge to any wall or other obstruction behind the seating area"
  - Someone needs to edge past. NKBA: "allow at least 36″ (914 mm) to edge past"
  - Someone needs to walk past. NKBA: "allow at least 44″ (1118 mm) to walk past"
  - Wheelchair passing behind (NKBA Access Standard): "plan a minimum of 60″ (1524 mm) to allow passage for a person in a wheelchair"
  - The NKBA PDF does NOT contain a 65 in figure. The largest figure is 60 in (wheelchair). The Access Standard gives 36 in (not 32) when no one passes behind.
  - Note: Fixr says to leave "44 to 60 inches" behind barstools, and Hunker says 60 in on the stool side. These are designer advice, not NKBA rules.

## 2. Seat width and knee space

- 24 in of width per seat [2+]
  - JLC Online: "The NKBA guidelines recommend that each seat be 24 inches wide"
  - Hunker, https://www.hunker.com/13724624/kitchen-islands-a-homeowners-guide/: "at least 24 inches of countertop width per seat"
  - Bob Vila, https://www.bobvila.com/articles/kitchen-island-size.md: "give each one 24 inches of space in total"
  - Fixr, https://fixr.com/articles/kitchen-island-dimensions: "aim for 22 to 24 inches across per seat"
  - Real Homes (Jeremy Boulanger), https://www.realhomes.com/us/advice/kitchen-island-size: "You should plan on 20 to 24 inches (50 to 60cm) for each person along an island"
- Knee space by counter height, from the NKBA PDF [2+, JLC and CRD agree]
  - 36 in counter: "36″ (914 mm) high counters: Allow a 24″ wide × 15″ deep (610 mm × 381 mm) knee space for each seated diner"
  - 30 in table: "30″ (762 mm) high tables/ counters: Allow a 24″ wide × 18″ deep (610 mm × 457 mm) knee space for each seated diner"
  - 42 in bar: "42″ (1067 mm) high counters: Allow a 24″ wide × 12″ deep (610 mm × 305 mm) knee space for each seated diner"
  - JLC Online: "for a 36-inch height, it should be 15 inches"
- On the "28/24/30" question: NKBA gives 24 in width at all three heights. The 28 in and 30 in figures in the NKBA PDF appear only in the Access Standard (seat height 28 to 34 in, width 30 to 36 in for a wheelchair seat). Some designers say 28 to 30 in feels roomier: Ben Neely in Real Homes says "at least 28 inches", and Homes & Gardens says 30 in is more generous. These are designer opinions [1 each].

## 3. Island size (minimum, typical, average) and room size

- Smallest built-in island is about 2 x 4 ft (24 x 48 in) [2+]
  - Bob Vila: "At a minimum, your built-in kitchen island size will need to be four feet by two feet"
  - Hunker (citing Laurysen Kitchens): "at minimum, 2 feet wide and 4 feet long"
  - Fixr: "anything smaller than 2 feet by 4 feet might look odd"
  - World Coppersmith, https://worldcoppersmith.com/articles/kitchen-island-size-guide: lists "24-inch x 48-inch kitchen island" as the small option
- Other minimums (these sources disagree)
  - This Old House (Tom Silva), https://www.thisoldhouse.com/kitchens/21016275/how-to-size-a-kitchen-island: "Silva recommends a kitchen island be at least 3 feet by 5 feet" [1]
  - Homes & Gardens, https://www.homesandgardens.com/solved/kitchen-island-size-rule: "Minimum size: around 72–84 inches (1.8–2.1 metres) long x 36 inches (0.9 metres) deep" [1]
  - No NKBA guideline sets a minimum island size. The PDF does not cover island size.
- Prep-only versus prep plus seating (Fixr) [1]
  - "Combining prep space and seating will require a minimum kitchen island width of 42 inches"
- Average size is about 40 x 80 in (about 1 x 2 m) [2+]
  - Fixr: "The typical kitchen island dimensions are 40 inches wide by 80 inches long"
  - Real Homes: "there is a kitchen island average size and this is 40 by 80 inches (around 1 by 2 metres)"
  - Livingetc, https://www.livingetc.com/advice/how-big-should-a-kitchen-island-be: "On average, standard kitchen islands usually have dimensions of 80 x 40 inches"
  - Note: Kitchen Cabinet Kings gives a smaller "average" of 4 ft x 2 ft, so there is some disagreement.
- Typical size steps, 24x48, 36x72 and 48x96 in (World Coppersmith) [1]
  - "The 36-inch x 72-inch kitchen island is perfect for medium-sized kitchens."
  - "a 48-inch x 96-inch kitchen island offers generous space for both work and entertaining"
  - Homes & Gardens: "Ideal size: 96–120 inches (2.4–3 metres) long x 42–48 inches (1.1–1.2 metres) deep to allow prep + seating"
- Kitchen width needed, about 13 ft [2+]
  - Bob Vila: "If your kitchen is less than 13 feet wide, it is unlikely that a built-in island will be possible"
  - Hunker (citing Laurysen Kitchens): "requires a kitchen that is at least 13 feet wide"
- Kitchen floor area needed, about 150 sq ft [2+]
  - Fixr: "If your kitchen is at least 150 square feet, you should be able to fit an island"
  - Real Homes (Volodymyr Barabakh, Fortress Home): "you should have a kitchen of at least 150 square foot (14 square meters) to comfortably have an island"
- Island length for a sink or cooktop (Bob Vila) [1]
  - The page says about 7 ft for a cooktop OR a sink and "at least nine feet long" for both.

## 4. Overhang for seating and when it needs support

- 15 in at counter height and 12 in at bar height [2+, roughly]
  - This Old House: "a minimum overhang of 15 inches for standard-height counters and a 12-inch overhang for bar-height counters"
  - Slabwise, https://slabwise.com/guides/what-is-the-standard-overhang-for-kitchen-countertops: "Counter height (36 inches): 12 to 15 inches of overhang for standard stools" and "Bar height (42 inches): 12 to 14 inches of overhang for bar stools"
  - This matches the NKBA knee-space depths of 15 in at 36 in high and 12 in at 42 in high (section 2).
- 12 in is often called the "standard" seating overhang [2+]
  - Fixr: "A standard overhang over barstools is 12 inches"
  - Real Homes (Elyse Moody): "A standard counter overhang is 12 inches (30.5cm)"
  - Caesarstone US, https://www.caesarstoneus.com/blog/quartz-countertop-overhang-how-far-can-you-go/: "The 12 inches island overhang countertop is generally utilized for a bar seating area around the island"
- When the overhang needs support. Sources DISAGREE because it depends on the material and its thickness [1 each]
  - Hunker, https://www.hunker.com/13402767/how-much-counter-overhang-for-seating: "if you do extend the overhang over 12 inches, though, it will need reinforcements to be safe and stable"
  - Slabwise (stone, cites Natural Stone Institute guidance): "Stone past 6 inches needs corbels, brackets, or a steel rod."
  - Caesarstone (quartz): "it can support up to a 14-inch overhang without extra support" and "Overhangs over 24 inches can only be supported by posts or substrates"
  - Safe wording for the page: "Overhangs past about 10 to 12 in usually need brackets or corbels. Check the limit with your countertop maker."

## 5. Island height and depth

- Height of 36 in, the standard counter height [2+]
  - Bob Vila: "The standard height for a kitchen island is 36 inches"
  - This Old House: "typically 36 inches"
  - Fixr: "The average kitchen island height is 36 inches for a prep area"
- Bar height of 42 in [2+]
  - This Old House: "bar-height counters typically measure 42 inches high"
  - Bob Vila: "Raise that up to 42 inches if your island will also be used for dining"
- Depth with seating, about 42 in or more [2+]
  - Fixr: "Combining prep space and seating will require a minimum kitchen island width of 42 inches"
  - Homes & Gardens: ideal "42–48 inches (1.1–1.2 metres) deep to allow prep + seating"
- Depth for a cooktop island (World Coppersmith) [1]
  - "at least 3 feet deep to accommodate a small cooktop", and "3.5 to 4 feet deep" for a full-size cooktop
- Space behind a cooktop on an island (NKBA) [2+, CRD agrees]
  - NKBA: "the countertop should also extend a minimum of 9″ (229 mm) behind the cooking surface"

## 6. Rule of thumb: island is about 10% of the kitchen area

A real rule, but it comes from design magazines and blogs, NOT NKBA [2+]
- Fixr: "Most islands look best when they take up around 10% of the total kitchen square footage"
- Livingetc: "a kitchen island should take up around 10 percent of the total square footage of your kitchen"
- Real Homes (Volodymyr Barabakh): "Kitchen islands should take up between one tenth to one fifteenth of your overall kitchen". He says one fifteenth is ideal.
- Call it a "rule of thumb", not a standard. The NKBA PDF has no such rule.

## 7. UK and metric equivalents (mm)

- Gap between the island and the units, about 1,000 mm (1 m) [2+]
  - Wren Kitchens, https://www.wrenkitchens.com/blog/do-you-have-room-for-a-kitchen-island: "Generally the rule of thumb is a 1 metre clearance zone."
  - Homebuilding & Renovating (Tom Howley), https://www.homebuilding.co.uk/advice/kitchen-island-sizes: "you should always allow at least one metre of walkway space between your island and surrounding cabinetry"
  - Houzz UK (LWK London Kitchens), https://www.houzz.co.uk/ideabooks/50404095/thumbs/do-i-have-room-for-a-kitchen-island: "the island's worktop edge of at least 1000mm"
  - Livingetc (Tom Howley): "leave a metre of walkway space on either one or two sides of your worktop, island, or peninsula counter"
- 1,000 to 1,200 mm for busy work areas (Houzz, written by Anne Ellard, an Australian designer) [1]
  - URL: https://www.houzz.com.sg/ideabooks/124913644/thumbs/key-measurements-to-consider-when-designing-your-kitchen-island
  - "The ideal space between the working areas of the island and other work surfaces is 1,000 to 1,200 millimetres"
  - The same article gives a lower floor: "Plan for at least 900 millimetres of space between the island and fixed items"
  - Masterclass Kitchens goes lower still: "Generally, 800mm of space around the whole unit will ensure the kitchen offers a practical walkway for one person" [1]
- Smallest island is 1,000 x 1,000 mm [2+]
  - Houzz UK (LWK London): "the minimum recommended size of a fixed kitchen island is 1000mm x 1000mm"
  - Masterclass Kitchens, https://www.masterclasskitchens.co.uk/inspiration/ideas-and-inspiration/do-you-have-room-for-a-kitchen-island: "the minimum standard kitchen island size is 1,000mm by 1,000mm"
  - Homebuilding (Tom Howley): "The practical minimum size for an island is 1m x 1m"
- Average island is 1 x 2 m [2+]
  - Wren: "The average size of an island is 1 by 2 metres."
  - Homebuilding: "standard kitchen islands usually have dimensions of around 1 x 2 metres"
- Room width needed. UK sources DISAGREE [1 each]
  - Homebuilding (Richard Moore, Martin Moore): "you need the room to be at least 4.2 metres wide to accommodate a useful kitchen island"
  - Wren: "All in all your space needs to be 4.5 to 5 metres wide for a small kitchen island."
  - For comparison, the US figure of 13 ft is about 3.96 m.
- Seat width, 600 mm per person [2+]
  - Houzz (Anne Ellard): "Allow for at least 600 millimetres width per person when seated at an island"
  - This matches the 24 in (610 mm) NKBA figure.
- Overhang, about 300 mm [2+]
  - Homebuilding: "Add an overhang of around 30cm"
  - Livingetc (Georgina Cave): "a 300-400mm overhang to allow for comfort"
- Storage island depth of 900 mm or more (Homebuilding, Tom Howley) [1]: "should be at least 900mm deep"
