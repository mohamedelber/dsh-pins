## Caveats/unconfirmed

- **Not found anywhere: a Building Regs rule tying the front door to a heating appliance.** No source links keeping the front door to a heating appliance. The confirmed rules are separate: the front door must stay (#24), and the house heating must not be extended into the porch (#27). Only the gov.uk circular (#26–29) mentions heating. The Planning Portal page doesn't.
- **Not on the Planning Portal page: listed buildings and conservation areas.** The Planning Portal porch page only mentions "planning condition, Article 4 Direction or other restriction". Listed building consent is confirmed by Camden council (#13) and Homebuilding (#15). Conservation-area wording for England was not confirmed on gov.uk. The Scottish council PDF says conservation-area properties need permission for a porch of any size, but that was summarised and not quoted.
- **Approved Document K figures (1500 / 300 / 800 mm)** come from a council leaflet (Lancaster, #31–33), not from the gov.uk ADK itself. The gov.uk PDF I fetched (#34) was the 2013 draft, and its Diagram 21 dimensions were not readable. Treat the figures as accurate but cite them to Lancaster or to "Approved Document K".
- **Technical guidance PDF (#11–12):** the URL is the April 2017 gov.uk file, but the fetch tool called it "September 2019". The wording matches both editions.
- **Scotland (#19–21)** is from a council advice note quoting Scottish Government Circular 1/2012. mygov.scot returned an empty page. **Wales** matches England (3 m², 3 m, 2 m) and has the same Building Regs exemption.
- **Costs disagree a lot.** Brick porch: MyJobQuote says £5.5k–£8k, MyBuilder £7.5k–£12.5k, and Ideal Home/Oldham £2k–£3k. Suggest giving a range and naming the sources. No Checkatrade or Which? porch cost page came up in search, so none is cited.
- **Coat hook height** is from a US retailer guide (inches). No UK source was found. Bench height and depth are from Wayfair UK and are general, not specific to hallways.
- **Doormat 80% rule** is from mats4u (UK retailer). Its "29 inches for a three-foot door" example is US-style.
- **No source gives a minimum clear space for doors opening into a porch.** The only depth guidance is 1–1.5 m (Assaf, #48).
- **Condensation in uPVC porches:** only MyBuilder Q&A answers from tradespeople (#94–96) confirmed it, which is weak authority. Homebuilding's porch/heat article doesn't cover condensation.
- **R11 for porches** is not stated directly. The source only says many outdoor tiles carry R11 (#58). "Use R11 for open porches" would be our recommendation, not a quote.
- **The Energy Saving Trust page doesn't mention porches.** The buffer-zone quotes come from Homebuilding experts (#87–89). The Ideal Home draughts article quotes EST at "around £45 a year" while the current EST page says "around £30 a year", so use £30 (#90).
- **Homebuilding's 17-ideas article** says a building-regs exemption needs the porch separated "by external quality doors or windows". That was paraphrased by the fetch tool and is not in the table.
