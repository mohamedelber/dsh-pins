## New FAQs
- **How much does it cost to build a utility room?** A refresh can cost a few hundred pounds: paint £100–£300, units £200–£800. A small conversion typically costs £10,000–£15,000, and an extension £7,500–£25,000, taking 6–12 weeks. Costs rise with the distance to plumbing, appliances, and architect or planning fees (MyBuilder).
- **What usually goes in a utility room?** A washing machine and tumble dryer (or a washer dryer), a sink, a folding worktop and a tall cupboard for the mop, broom, vacuum and ironing board. Most also have shelves for detergents and an airer for drying, and some have a spare fridge or freezer.
- **What is a bootility room?** A utility that doubles as a boot room, usually by the back door. It combines laundry space with a bench, coat hooks, boot storage and a hard-wearing floor. Some include a low shower tray for rinsing dogs or muddy kit, which needs hot and cold water and a waste pipe (Heritage).
- **What is the best worktop for a utility room?** Pick a surface that copes with water, detergent and knocks, such as laminate, compact laminate or quartz. Solid wood needs regular oiling and good sealing around the sink. Make sure the worktop clears the machines underneath. (No source opened for this; keep it general.)
- **What is the smallest size for a utility room?** A working utility can fit into as little as 0.36 m² if you build upwards. The average utility room is 2.5–5 m². A single run of units needs about 1.5–1.8 m of wall plus about 1 m of clear floor in front (Ideal Home, Heritage).
- **Do I need building regulations approval for a utility room?** Converting an existing room usually doesn't need approval, but the electrical and ventilation work must comply. Any new utility room must have extract ventilation. A utility built as part of an extension is covered by that extension's approval (Heritage, Planning Portal).
