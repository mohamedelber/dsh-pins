## 6. New FAQ

**What is the best shelving for a pantry?** For most homes, adjustable shelving is the best choice because you can set each gap to what you store. Sealed wood or laminate gives a flat, solid surface for jars; wire is cheaper and airier but needs liners for small items. Keep eye-level shelves 12 to 16 inches deep so nothing hides.

**What is the latest trend for pantries?** Current pantry trends mix materials, such as warm wood accent shelves alongside wire or built-in shelving, and hide storage behind wall panels in so-called Murphy pantries. Open shelves with clear, uniform canisters, LED strip or motion-sensor lighting, and pocket or sliding doors that save swing space are also popular choices.

**How do you build simple pantry shelves?** Find and mark the studs, then screw level 1x2 cleats into them along the back and side walls. Cut 3/4-inch boards about 12 inches deep, rest them on the cleats and screw them down. Add a corner post or L-brackets where shelves meet, then caulk, prime and paint with semi-gloss.

**What should not be stored in a pantry?** Skip anything that needs refrigeration once opened, such as many condiments, plus fresh dairy, meat and cut produce, since pantries aren't climate controlled. Keep oils and heat-sensitive foods away from walls shared with an oven, range or dishwasher, and store cleaning chemicals separately from food. Check labels for "refrigerate after opening."
