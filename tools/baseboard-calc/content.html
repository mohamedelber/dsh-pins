    How to Measure for Baseboard

    1. Measure each wall at floor level, from corner to corner, and add them up. For a simple rectangle, that is 2 x (length + width).
    2. Take off the doorways. Baseboard stops at the door casing, so subtract each opening. A standard interior door plus its casing is about 3 ft.
    3. Add 10% for waste to cover cuts, corners and mistakes.
    4. Divide by the board length and round up to whole boards.

    Bob Vila says to measure the length of each wall from corner to corner, subtracting any openings for doors or closets, and This Old House adds that it’s wise to add an extra 10% to account for waste and mistakes.

    Baseboard Lengths: 8, 12 or 16 Feet?

    Bob Vila notes that home stores sell baseboard and crown molding in 8-foot and 12-foot lengths, and many stores also carry 16 ft boards. Lowe's advises: use longer lengths of moulding in large rooms so that fewer cuts will be required. If a wall is longer than one board, This Old House explains that scarf joints are used to join two pieces of baseboard along a long wall where a single piece isn’t long enough.

    Longer boards also mean fewer seams. A 10 ft wall cut from 8 ft boards needs a seam and leaves a 6 ft offcut; one 12 ft board covers it in one piece.

    How Much Extra Baseboard for Waste?

    Most guides say to add 10%; Lowe's says to add 10% to the total length for mitering. Bob Vila suggests more, about 20 percent extra material to account for mistakes, offcuts, and overlapping joints, which makes sense for a first DIY job. Rooms with many outside corners, like a bay window or a chimney breast, need more too: Home Depot says that for outside corners you should cut them a few inches longer than the wall to allow for miter cuts.

    For inside corners, This Old House says coping one piece to fit the profile of the adjoining board typically produces the best results. Coping gives a tighter joint than a miter when the walls are not perfectly square.

    Baseboard Cost per Linear Foot

    Material prices are from Angi’s guides to baseboard types and MDF vs. pine. For installation, HomeAdvisor says contractors charge between $5.70 and $9.00 per linear foot for baseboard installation, and HomeServe gives a similar $6 to $9 per linear foot. Taller and more detailed profiles cost more.

    What Height Baseboard Should You Use?

    Angi says the most common baseboard heights are 3 1/4 inches, 4 1/4 inches, and 5 1/4 inches, and that rooms with ceilings ten feet or higher need taller baseboards from five to seven inches or more. Some designers go taller: a Houseplans.com guide suggests a baseboard height that’s approximately 7% of the wall height, which is about 7 to 8 in for an 8 or 9 ft wall. Treat both as style choices, not rules.

    Nails, Caulk and Shoe Molding

    • Nails: Bob Vila says to nail into the lower 1½ inch of the trim with 2-inch finishing nails spaced roughly 16 inches apart. A 15 or 16 gauge finish nailer works well for baseboard.
    • Caulk: each 10.1 fl oz cartridge will cover 55 linear feet with a 3/16” bead, so a 12 x 12 ft room needs one tube for the top edge.
    • Shoe molding and quarter round: measure them the same way. A Pergo planner says to subtract the doorways from the perimeter to determine the total length of wall base or quarter round. The calculator adds it for you when you tick the box.

    Baseboard Calculator FAQ

    How much baseboard do I need for a 12 x 12 room?

    A 12 x 12 ft room has 48 ft of wall. Take off about 3 ft for one door to get 45 ft, then add 10% for waste: buy about 50 ft, or five 12 ft boards.

    How do I calculate linear feet of baseboard?

    Measure each wall in feet and add them up, or use 2 x (length + width) for a rectangle. Subtract about 3 ft for each doorway, then add 10% for waste. The result is the linear feet to buy.

    How much does baseboard cost per foot?

    MDF baseboard costs about $1 to $1.40 per linear foot and pine about $1.40 to $1.85. Professional installation, with materials, runs about $5.70 to $9 per linear foot.

    Do you subtract doors when measuring for baseboard?

    Yes. Baseboard stops at each side of the door casing, so subtract the width of every doorway and open closet, about 3 ft for a standard interior door. Do not subtract windows unless they go down to the floor.
