# Baseboard Calculator: Sources and Facts

Researched 2026-10-20.

**How the quotes were checked:** Every quote was pulled from the live page with WebFetch, and I asked for the exact words each time. Direct curl downloads were blocked by the network proxy, so I could not grep the raw HTML myself. Quotes are word-for-word as far as WebFetch returned them. Spot-check any quote you plan to print exactly.

Labels: **[2+]** means two or more independent sources agree. **[1]** means one source only.

## 1. How to calculate baseboard (perimeter, minus doors, plus waste)

**Method: measure each wall or the room perimeter, then round up [2+]**
- The Home Depot, "How to Install Baseboards" (updated Apr 29, 2026). https://www.homedepot.com/c/ah/how-to-install-baseboard/9ba683603be9fa5395fab901a97a162
  > "Measure the perimeter of the room(s) with a tape measure, rounding up to the nearest whole-foot dimension divisible by two."
- Home Depot Canada, "How to Install Baseboards". https://www.homedepot.ca/en/home/ideas-how-to/decor/how-to-install-baseboards.html
  > "First, you'll need to figure out how many linear feet of baseboard moulding you'll need."
- This Old House, "How To Install Baseboards" (updated 04/07/2026). https://www.thisoldhouse.com/walls/how-to-install-baseboards
  > "Round up to the nearest whole-foot dimension divisible by two to determine the amount of material needed."
- Angi, "Baseboard Trim Sizes" (Rachel Hoffman, updated Jul 8, 2026). https://www.angi.com/articles/baseboard-trim-sizes.htm
  > "measure the perimeter of the room and round up to the nearest foot"

**Subtract door openings [2+]**
- Bob Vila, "6 Things to Know Before Installing Interior Trim" (updated Oct 18, 2020). https://www.bobvila.com/articles/installing-trim/
  > "Measure the length of each wall from corner to corner, subtracting any openings for doors or closets."
- Lowe's, "Moulding Buying Guide" (Brian Gregory, updated July 4, 2025). https://lowes.com/n/buying-guide/moulding-buying-guide
  > "Measure the length of each wall, taking into account spaces for windows and doors."
- Pergo project planner PDF (hosted by Home Depot). https://images.thdstatic.com/catalog/pdfImages/27/27182e9f-21f8-436a-9def-9e127a172672.pdf
  > "Subtract that from the perimeter (C) to determine the total length of wall base or quarter round."
  ("That" means the total doorway widths.)

**Typical door width to subtract [1 for each number]**
- Bob Vila, "Standard Door Size 101" (updated Nov 15, 2023). https://www.bobvila.com/articles/standard-size-for-doors
  > "The standard door width for interior doors is 32 inches."
  > "Typically, front doors are 36 inches wide"
- Angi, "Door Casing Sizes" (updated Jul 9, 2026). https://angi.com/articles/door-casing-sizes.htm
  > "Door casings come in various sizes, but the standard width is 2 1/4 inches."
  > "Door casing trim comes in widths from 2 1/4 to 3 1/2 inches"
- My own math for the calculator (not quoted from a source): a 32 in door plus 2 x 2.25 in casing is about 36.5 in, so **about 3 ft per interior door** is a fair default. Use about 3.4 ft for a 36 in entry door with casing.

**Waste allowance: 10% is the most common advice [2+]**
- This Old House (above):
  > "It's wise to add an extra 10% to account for waste and mistakes."
- Lowe's Moulding Buying Guide (above):
  > "Remember to add 10% to the total length for mitering."
- Angi, Baseboard Trim Sizes (above):
  > "ensuring you have at least 10% extra in case of errors"
- Higher number from Bob Vila, Installing Trim (above), **20% [1]**:
  > "You'll want to purchase about 20 percent extra material to account for mistakes, offcuts, and overlapping joints."
- Home Depot (above), beginner tip:
  > "If you are a beginner, add six inches (or more) to your total measurement to give yourself some leeway."

**15% for rooms with many corners [1, calculator site only, not a major brand]**
- Miniwebtool, Baseboard Trim Calculator. https://miniwebtool.com/baseboard-trim-calculator/
  > "A 10% waste factor is the standard recommendation for a typical room."
  > "Rooms with many corners, bay windows, or angled walls warrant 15%,"

## 3. Baseboard heights and ceiling height

**Common heights: 3-1/4, 4-1/4, 5-1/4 in [1 article, plus product titles]; 7-1/4 in shown in a product title**
- Angi, Baseboard Trim Sizes (above):
  > "The most common baseboard heights are 3 1/4 inches, 4 1/4 inches, and 5 1/4 inches"
- Home Depot product title (7-1/4 in). https://homedepot.com/p/HOUSE-OF-FARA-8815-5-8-in-x-7-1-4-in-x-96-in-Primed-MDF-Baseboard-Moulding-1-Piece-8-Total-Linear-Feet-8815/204318367
  > "8815 5/8 in. x 7 1/4 in. x 96 in. Primed MDF Baseboard Moulding (1-Piece − 8 Total Linear Feet)"
- Angi, Baseboard (no date). https://www.angi.com/home-interior/baseboard
  > "The standard height for baseboards is 3 to 5 inches"
- Common thickness, Angi Trim Sizes (above):
  > "Baseboard thickness varies, but the most common widths are 1/2 inch, 9/16 inch, and 5/8 inch."

**Height by ceiling height [2+ that taller ceilings need taller base; exact numbers differ]**
- Angi, Baseboard Trim Sizes (above):
  > "Rooms with a standard eight- or nine-foot ceiling may look best with a shorter baseboard, from three to five inches high"
  > "rooms with ceilings ten feet or higher need taller baseboards from five to seven inches or more"
- Houseplans.com Blog, "Interior Trim: Scale and Proportion" (Bud Dietrich, Aug 28, 2020). https://www.houseplans.com/blog/interior-trim-scale-proportion
  > "A baseboard height that's approximately 7% of the wall height provides for a solid and definable base without being too big."
  > "So in a room with a 9 foot high ceiling, a baseboard that's about 8 inches tall works."
- Hunker, "How To Choose Baseboards" (Dec 10, 2010). https://www.hunker.com/12434119/how-to-choose-baseboards/
  > "One rule of thumb is to use baseboard that is 5-1/4 inches tall for a room that is 8 feet tall."

Note: the 7% rule gives about 6.7 in for 8 ft and 7.6 in for 9 ft. That is taller than Angi's 3-5 in. Show both as "style choices", not as a hard rule.

## 5. Nails, caulk, paint

**Nail into studs about every 16 in [2+]**
- Bob Vila, "How to Install Baseboard Trim" (updated Mar 25, 2022). https://www.bobvila.com/articles/how-to-install-baseboard/
  > "When performing baseball molding installation, it's always a good idea to nail into the studs."
  (Note: "baseball" is a typo on the live page.) The page also says to use a finish nailer "approximately every 16 inches, alongside doors and windows, and in corners".
- Bob Vila, Installing Trim (above):
  > "nail into the lower 1½ inch of the trim with 2-inch finishing nails spaced roughly 16 inches apart."
- Lowe's-hosted install guide PDF (doc source not clear). https://pdf.lowes.com/productdocuments/4bffa135-120a-453c-befa-8da4095e2048/71751461.pdf
  > "Use a stud finder to locate the studs and drive finishing nails into the wall studs every 16–18 inches."

**Nail gauge and length [1 per point]**
- This Old House, "How To Choose a Finish Nailer" (updated 05/28/2026). https://www.thisoldhouse.com/tools/how-to-choose-a-finish-nailer
  > "A 15-gauge finish nailer is the heavyweight of the bunch, shooting thick nails between 1 1/4–2 1/2 inches long."
  > "These nailers typically shoot nails ranging from 3/4–2 1/2 inches long." (about 16-gauge; the page lists "attaching baseboards and crown molding" as a use)
- This Old House, How To Install Baseboards (above):
  > "use 8d finish nails, driving them at a slight downward angle near the top and bottom edges of the board."
- Lowe's Moulding Guide (above), for general/small moulding:
  > "The most common size used in moulding is 1-1/4 inches (3d) or 1-1/2 inches (4d)."

**Caulk coverage: about 55 linear ft per 10.1 oz tube at a 3/16 in bead [2+]**
- DAP ALEX PLUS technical data sheet. https://www.dap.com/media/23120/alex-plus-acrylic-latex-caulk-white_tds_5-12-25.pdf
  > "10.1 fl oz: 55 linear feet at a 3/16"diameter bead"
- BEHR Multi-Purpose caulk data sheet (Home Depot hosted). https://images.thdstatic.com/catalog/pdfImages/35/3579c54d-a385-4155-9c15-a555d615d4eb.pdf
  > "Each 10.1 fl oz cartridge will cover 55 linear feet with a 3/16" bead."
- Caulk the top edge, Bob Vila install page (above):
  > "Load the caulking gun with a tube of latex caulk and caulk along the top seam of each baseboard"

**Paint coverage per linear foot:** no authoritative source found. Leave it out of the calculator.

## 6. Inside vs outside corners

**Cope inside corners, miter outside corners at 45 degrees [2+]**
- This Old House (above):
  > "For inside corners, coping one piece to fit the profile of the adjoining board typically produces the best results."
  > "Cut both pieces at 45-degree angles and dry-fit them before installation to ensure a tight seam."
- Bob Vila install page (above):
  > "Whereas a coped joint is recommended for inside corners, a mitered joint is appropriate for outside corners."
- Bob Vila Installing Trim (above):
  > "Outside corners can be formed by cutting two boards at 45 degrees"
- Home Depot Canada (above):
  > "A cope joint is where one piece of moulding is cut to fit the profile of another."

**Extra length for outside corners [2+]**
- Home Depot how-to (above):
  > "For the boards that meet outside corners, cut them a few inches longer than the wall to allow for miter cuts."
- This Old House (above):
  > "For outside corners, add a few inches to allow for miter cuts."
- Designing Buildings Wiki (UK) says to add 50-100 mm per measurement for mitres (paraphrased). https://www.designingbuildings.co.uk/wiki/How_to_install_skirting_boards

## 7. Shoe moulding and quarter round

**Sold and measured in linear feet; same perimeter-minus-doorways method [2+]**
- Pergo planner PDF (above) uses the same calculation for both:
  > "Subtract that from the perimeter (C) to determine the total length of wall base or quarter round."

Quarter Round Moulding (1-Piece - 8 Total Linear Feet)". The page itself was not fetched.)
- What it is, Home Depot Types of Baseboards (above):
  > "The main use of shoe moulding is to run along the intersection of the baseboard and floor."
- Lowe's Moulding Guide (above):
  > "Base cap, shoe and quarter round moulding are typically installed on the bottom edges of a baseboard."
