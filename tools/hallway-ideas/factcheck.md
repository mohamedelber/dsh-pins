# Fact-check: hallway-ideas/content.html

Checked 25 Oct 2026. For every source below I ran a WebSearch, then fetched the page and compared it with the article. Each "Current text" string is copied exactly from content.html so you can find and replace it.

Severity: **MUST-FIX** = wrong attribution or misread number. **SHOULD-FIX** = overstated, out of context, or guidance presented as a rule. **MINOR** = wording, consistency or dating.

- **Evidence:** https://sheerluxe.com/home/how-to-choose-a-colour-for-your-front-door (24 Mar 2020). Her heading is just "Katharine Paravicini". ### 3. MUST-FIX: "9 out of 10" refers to floors, not tiles
- **Current text:** `The good news is that around <a href="https://www.buildingconservation.com/articles/tiles/tiles.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener">9 out of 10 are generally repairable</a>.`
- **Problem:** The sentence just before it ends "around 4,000 tiles", so readers will take this as 9 in 10 tiles. In the source it means 9 in 10 floors. The source is also a 2004 article by restoration consultant Peter Thompson (Original Features), reproduced from *The Building Conservation Directory*, so it is not a current Building Conservation statement.

### 4. SHOULD-FIX: The 900 mm hall width reads like a general rule
- **Current text:** `Building Regulations guidance for accessible new homes says`
- **Problem:** The 900 mm line comes from para 2.22 of M4(2), the *optional* "accessible and adaptable dwellings" standard. It applies only when a planning condition requires it. The default for new homes, M4(1), sets no flat 900 mm: corridor width depends on door width (Table 1.1). None of this applies to existing homes.
- **Evidence:** https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/government/uploads/system/uploads/attachment_data/file/540330/BR_PDF_AD_M1_2015_with_2016_amendments_V3.pdf, para 2.22: "The minimum clear width of every hall or landing is 900mm." Para 0.3 says M4(2) and M4(3) are "optional requirements".
- **Suggested replacement:** `For new homes built to the optional "accessible and adaptable" standard (M4(2)), Building Regulations guidance says`

### 5. SHOULD-FIX: The 750 mm pinch point is credited to a superseded standard, and the anchor text is not on the page
- **Current text:** `and the Lifetime Homes standard allows a pinch point of 750 mm at <a href="https://cae.org.uk/our-services/housing-services/lifetime-homes/6-internal-doorways-and-hallways/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">an obstruction such as a radiator</a>.`
- **Problem:**
  - (a) In England, Lifetime Homes was superseded in 2015 by M4(2), so the sentence presents a retired standard as current.
  - (b) Approved Document M already contains the 750 mm rule, with conditions: the obstruction must be no longer than 2 m and not opposite or close to a doorway. The diagram credits the 750 mm to Approved Document M while the body credits Lifetime Homes, so the two are inconsistent.
  - (c) The CAE page never uses the word "obstruction". It says "pinch points (e.g. beside a radiator)".
- **Evidence:**
  - AD M PDF (above), para 2.22(b), with the same wording in M4(1) para 1.15(b): a localised obstruction "such as a radiator… is no longer than 2m in length; and the corridor is not reduced below a minimum 750mm width at any point."
  - https://cae.org.uk/our-services/housing-services/lifetime-homes/6-internal-doorways-and-hallways/ says "This may reduce to 750mm at 'pinch points' (e.g. beside a radiator)".
  - https://cae.org.uk/?p=5424 says "the Lifetime Homes standard was superseded in 2015 by M4(2) Part M of Building Regulations."
- **Suggested replacement:** `and the same guidance lets a short obstruction such as a radiator narrow it to 750 mm, as long as it is under 2 m long and not beside a doorway (the older Lifetime Homes standard, which it replaced in England in 2015, used the same <a href="https://cae.org.uk/our-services/housing-services/lifetime-homes/6-internal-doorways-and-hallways/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">750 mm pinch point beside a radiator</a>).`

### 6. SHOULD-FIX: The LABC 300 mm spacing is new-work guidance, not a general rule
- **Current text:** `Keep new lights clear of the smoke alarm: LABC advises mounting alarms <a href="https://www.labc.co.uk/news/dos-and-donts-mains-powered-smoke-alarms-and-battery-alarms" target="_blank" rel="noopener">300mm away from walls and light fittings</a>.`
- **Problem:** The LABC page (31 Oct 2018) is building-control guidance about alarms in new homes and in building work such as loft conversions and extensions. The quote is accurate, but the article should frame it as that guidance.
- **Evidence:** The LABC URL above says "Mount them 300mm away from walls and light fittings" and that mains-powered, interlinked alarms "must now be installed in all new homes".
- **Suggested replacement:** `Keep new lights clear of the smoke alarm: building control body LABC's guidance for new alarm installations is to mount them <a href="https://www.labc.co.uk/news/dos-and-donts-mains-powered-smoke-alarms-and-battery-alarms" target="_blank" rel="noopener">300mm away from walls and light fittings</a>.`

### 11. SHOULD-FIX: "Needs a qualified electrician" overstates the legal position
- **Current text:** `Any new wired light needs a qualified electrician.`
- **Problem:** Under Part P in England, adding a light point to an existing circuit is not notifiable work, as long as it is outside a kitchen or special location. New circuits are notifiable. Recommending a registered electrician is good advice, but the sentence reads as a legal requirement.
- **Evidence:** https://www.richmond.gov.uk/building_regulations_and_electrical_works lists "Adding lighting points (light fittings and switches) to an existing circuit" as non-notifiable, adding "They may be carried out by a competent person who can issue a Minor Works Certificate".
- **Suggested replacement:** `Use a registered electrician for any new wired light: they can certify the work, and new circuits must be notified to building control.`

### 12. MINOR: Landlord smoke alarm rule wording
- **Current text:** `In England, landlords must fit <a href="https://www.oxford.gov.uk/landlords-agents/private-housing-smoke-regulations" target="_blank" rel="noopener">a working smoke alarm on each storey</a> of a rented home.`
- **Problem:** Broadly correct. The duty is to ensure there is an alarm on each storey that has a room used as living accommodation, under the rules in force since 1 Oct 2022. Saying so is more precise.
- **Evidence:** The Oxford URL says landlords must ensure "a working smoke alarm on each storey of a premises where one or more rooms are used as living accommodation". The 2022 amendment "came into force" on 1 October 2022.
- **Suggested replacement:** `In England, landlords must make sure there is <a href="https://www.oxford.gov.uk/landlords-agents/private-housing-smoke-regulations" target="_blank" rel="noopener">a working smoke alarm on each storey</a> of a rented home that has a room used as living accommodation.`

### 15. MINOR: Diagram: "one third" doesn't fit the drawn 240 cm wall

### 16. MINOR: Diagram: 105 cm threshold vs "under a metre" in the body

- **Problem:** The body says `If the hall is under a metre wide, choose wall-hung shelves`. The two thresholds don't match, and neither has a source. The 105 cm figure is derived: 105 minus a 30 cm console leaves 75 cm.

### 17. MINOR: Diagram desc: "pinch-point allowance" wording

- **Problem:** AD M allows 750 mm only at a localised obstruction under 2 m long and away from doorways. "Pinch point" is the Lifetime Homes term (see item 5). The comparison still works.

### 18. MINOR: The mirror rule is general, and "size" means width
- **Current text:** `should be at least half the size of the wall</a> it hangs on, with some clear space around the frame.`
- **Problem:** Furniturebox gives this as a general rule, not one for hallways. Its example (an 8 ft wall needs a mirror at least 4 ft wide) shows it means width. Read literally, it would mean a mirror half the length of a long hall wall.
- **Evidence:** https://furniturebox.co.uk/blog/how-big-should-a-mirror-be says "As a general rule, a statement mirror should be at least half the size of the wall it will be mounted on or leaned against", and also "leave at least 50cm of free space around the mirror frame".
- **Suggested replacement:** `should be at least half the size of the wall</a> it hangs on (in a hall, think of the stretch of wall above your shelf or console), with some clear space around the frame.`
