## Caveats

1. **Summariser risk.** Every quote went through the WebFetch summariser. Quotes marked **(unconfirmed)** are paraphrased or cut short (#19, #43, #77, #79, #93). Short spec strings from IKEA (titles, "Storage capacity") were requested character for character and look reliable. Even so, open the live pages before publishing, because IKEA prices and specs change often.
2. **MALM and KOPPANG are missing from IKEA US.** The US MALM series page (https://www.ikea.com/us/en/cat/malm-series-07468/) listed no MALM dresser or chest on 2026-10-26, only beds and a dressing table. The US dresser category showed no MALM or KOPPANG among the 24 of 158 items it displayed. They may be discontinued in the US or just not shown. Short IKEA URLs such as /us/en/p/-30403571 and /ca/en/p/-60403584 led to generic category pages. **HEMNES 6-drawer depth was not captured** (only W x H in the listing).
3. **IKEA names everything a "dresser" in the US.** Tall single-column units (STORKLINTA 27 1/2 x 47 1/4, BRIMNES 4-drawer, FÅGELFJÄLLET 32 1/4 x 48 3/8) are titled "dresser", yet the product copy says "chest of drawers" and older or Canadian URLs say "chest". Pottery Barn does the same ("Tall Dresser"). This supports the article's point that retailers blur the terms. When computing, classify by shape (W vs H), not by product title.
4. **IKEA "storage capacity" (cu ft)** is IKEA's own figure. It is not the same as inner drawer dimensions × drawer count, and wide and tall units in the same series can have different drawer heights. Both STORKLINTA 6-drawer units list identical inner drawer W x D (24 x 15 3/4) but different capacities (8.4 vs 6.8 cu ft). Use IKEA's figures as published, or compute your own from inner W x D, but do not mix the two methods.
5. **Wayfair source is Canadian.** The US Wayfair guide could not be reached (wayfair.com blocks fetches, and the Container Store page returned a captcha). The wayfair.ca guide is in English and gives inches. The Wayfair organising tip (#118) sits at a URL whose slug says "patio seating", but the page content and search title were "Tips & Ideas for Organizing a Dresser". Recheck it before linking.
6. **Clearance guidance varies: 24" to 48"** (Hunker 24", Wayfair and Coleman 36", dimensions.com 42–48"). I found **no NKBA or Architectural Graphic Standards** figure for bedroom drawer clearance. NKBA covers kitchens and baths. Dimensions.com is the closest architectural-reference source. Suggest saying "24" bare minimum to walk past; 36" to open drawers and stand there", backed by the sources above.
7. **The two CPSC datasets do not overlap.** The 2022 report covers deaths 2000–2021 (592; 38% CBD). The 2023 report (posted Feb 2024) resets to Jan 2013–Jul 2023 (217; 36% CBD) and notes incomplete reporting for 2021–2023. Do not add or compare the two series directly. The CPSC 2022 press release gives 472 child deaths (2000–2020); the 2022 report gives 482 (2000–2021). Both are correct for their periods. I did not find a newer annual tip-over report (2024 or 2025) on cpsc.gov.
8. **CBD injury share.** 28% is the share of *child* furniture-related ER injuries involving chests, bureaus or dressers (per kidsindanger and the 2022 report). The 2023 report's Table 8 gives CBD as 21% of furniture-related injuries across all ages, as reported by the summariser. Name the population when you cite either figure.
9. **Scope volume threshold.** Intertek and Compliancegate say 3.2 cu ft. BCLP's page text reads "3.23" with no unit (likely 3.2 ft³ with a footnote). The Federal Register summary also gave 3.2 ft³. Use "3.2 cubic feet".
10. **Tip restraints.** Intertek says CSUs must include an ASTM F3096 anti-tip device. Compliancegate notes the stability tests exclude tip restraints. In other words, the unit must pass without the strap, and the strap is supplied in addition. I could not confirm the second point verbatim from CPSC. The answers on the CPSC FAQ page (https://www.cpsc.gov/FAQ/Clothing-Storage-Units) did not load, only the question headings.
11. **CHP "nearly half of tip-over deaths in a bedroom"** (#103) and **"every 43 minutes"** on the same page are hospital figures with no cited source. CPSC's current figure is **every 53 minutes** (#92). Prefer the CPSC number.
12. **Highboy, tallboy and lowboy definitions conflict.** Britannica and Oxford treat tallboy (UK) and highboy (US) as the same tall double chest. Wikipedia's "Tallboy (furniture)" page says a tallboy is a chest of drawers with a wardrobe on top. Wikipedia's "Lowboy" page calls a lowboy a dressing table, while Dictionary.com calls it a low chest of drawers on short legs. Use the dictionary or Britannica senses.
13. **Merriam-Webster "bureau"** could not be fetched (PROVENANCE_REQUIRED, permission timed out): https://www.merriam-webster.com/dictionary/bureau. The US vs UK bureau senses come from Oxford Learner's (#6–7) instead.
14. **The organising tip "most-used items at waist height" was not found verbatim** in any source. The closest sourced versions are "top drawers for items you put on first" (#117) and "most frequently used at the top" (#118), plus CPSC's "heavier items in lower drawers" (#96).
15. **Pottery Barn and Crate & Barrel pages** did not show full W x D x H for most wide dressers (only width in the name). Full dimensions were captured only for the Cayman tall dresser and the Sausalito tall wide dresser.
16. **Recall article (#104)** is trade press (Home News Now), not CPSC.gov. The figures are from Oct–Nov 2025.
