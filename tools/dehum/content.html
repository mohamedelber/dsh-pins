Dehumidifier: Best for Small Homes and Damp

Best for: flats and rentals with no room or venting for a dryer, homes with condensation, and anyone who dries washing on airers already.

A dehumidifier does two jobs. Which? found it almost halved the drying time compared with an airer alone in the same room, and it also pulled around two litres of water out of the air. Manchester City Council says drying clothes indoors can add 10 - 15 litres of water a week, and that a dehumidifier helps reduce moisture. Dorset Council puts normal indoor humidity at around 40 to 55%, with the risk rising above 60%.

Pick the right type. Meaco says a desiccant dehumidifier is better for temperatures below 10°C, such as an unheated utility room or garage, while compressor models are generally cheaper to run in a heated home. Which? advises keeping the unit at least 20cm from walls or furniture, and Meaco's manual says to place it on a flat stable surface with the air vents clear.

Avoid if: you need a full load dry in a couple of hours, or you dry several loads a day. Our guide on how to dry clothes indoors covers the rest of the routine.

Heat Pump Tumble Dryer: Best for Speed and Running Costs

Best for: families, people with no outdoor drying space, and anyone who wants towels dry the same evening.

A heat pump dryer is the cheapest tumble dryer to run. Which? puts it at about £54.32 a year for three loads a week at the current price cap, against about £139 for condenser and vented models. It costs more to buy: Which? lists heat pump dryers from £327 to £1,699. From 19 January 2027, new vented, condenser and gas dryers can no longer be placed on the market in Great Britain, so heat pump dryers will be the main choice for new machines. Avoid if: you have no space, or you rent and cannot install one. Look after it safely: Cornwall Fire and Rescue advises you to clean the filter in your dryer after every use and never leave it running overnight.
