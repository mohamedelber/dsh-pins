For drying clothes, a heat pump tumble dryer is usually cheaper per load and faster than a dehumidifier, but a dehumidifier is cheaper to buy, takes up less space and also dries out a damp home. Which? tested both and found a dehumidifier would still cost more and take longer than the most cost-effective tumble dryers. Over five years, though, our sums put a dehumidifier and airer within about £20 of one of the cheapest heat pump dryers, and about £130 cheaper than a more typical £500 model.

  • Choose a heat pump tumble dryer if you wash most days, need clothes dry in hours, and have room for one.
  • Choose a dehumidifier if you rent, have no space for a dryer, or also battle condensation and mould.
  • Avoid a new condenser dryer if running costs matter; it costs the most to run of the three.

Dehumidifier: Best for Small Homes and Damp

Best for: flats and rentals with no room or venting for a dryer, homes with condensation, and anyone who dries washing on airers already.

A dehumidifier does two jobs. Which? found it almost halved the drying time compared with an identical load dried without one, and it also pulled around two litres of water out of the air. Manchester City Council says drying clothes indoors can add 10 to 15 litres of water a week, and that a dehumidifier helps reduce moisture. Dorset Council puts normal indoor humidity at around 40 to 55%, with the risk rising above 60%.

Pick the right type. Meaco says a desiccant dehumidifier is better for temperatures below 10°C, such as an unheated utility room or garage, while compressor models are generally cheaper to run in a heated home. Which? advises keeping the unit at least 20cm from walls or furniture, and Meaco's manual says to place it on a flat stable surface with the air vents clear.

Avoid if: you need a full load dry in a couple of hours, or you dry several loads a day. Our guide on how to dry clothes indoors covers the rest of the routine.

Heat Pump Tumble Dryer: Best for Speed and Running Costs

Best for: families, people with no outdoor drying space, and anyone who wants towels dry the same evening.

A heat pump dryer is the cheapest tumble dryer to run. Which? puts it at about £54.32 a year for three loads a week at the current price cap, against about £139 for condenser and £140 for vented models. It costs more to buy: Which? lists heat pump dryers from £327 to £1,699. From 19 January 2027, new vented, condenser and gas-fired dryers will no longer be allowed onto the market in Great Britain, RETRA reports, so heat pump dryers will be the main choice for new machines. Our heat pump vs condenser dryer guide explains the rule and the running costs in detail.

Avoid if: you have no space, or you rent and cannot install one. Look after it safely: Cornwall Fire and Rescue advises you to clean the filter in your dryer after every use and never leave it unattended or running overnight.

Condenser Tumble Dryer: Cheapest to Buy

Best for: people who already own one, and anyone who needs a dryer now on the smallest upfront budget.

Condenser dryers are the cheapest to buy, with Which? listing them from £220 to £529, and they need no vent to the outside. But they cost the most to run: about £138.91 a year for three loads a week at the current price cap, more than twice a heat pump dryer. Over five years that makes them the most expensive option in our sums.

Avoid if: running costs matter to you, or you are buying a new machine after January 2027.
