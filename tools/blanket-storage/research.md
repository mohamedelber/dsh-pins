## Caveats

1. **Quote fidelity.** All quotes came through the WebFetch summariser. Rows 22, 75 and 76 are marked as paraphrases. Treat the rest as near-verbatim and check them against the live page before publishing. Some quotes were shortened with an ellipsis to stay under 15 words (rows 33, 45, 55, 63, 76).
2. **Clearance numbers conflict.** Castlery says "12 to 18 inches" (row 23), but IKEA low frames list only 7⅞" (NEIDEN) and 8¼" (MALM). Write it as "measure first". Many platform and IKEA frames have under 8", which rules out the 7½" SKUBB on a 7⅞" frame unless you allow a carpet margin. A 6" fabric bag or the 7" Sterilite fits; risers add about 5¼".
3. **The MALM high-bed box (row 21) is 11⅜" tall**, but its usable drawer depth is only 5⅞". Use this to show that outside height and usable height are different. IKEA gives no official clearance figure for the MALM High bed. The "11 inches clearance" figure is from a customer review and was not used.
4. **Vacuum bags and down.** I found no Pacific Coast Feather or Brooklinen statement. The Pacific Coast FAQ (https://Pacificcoast.com/frequent-questions.html) did not load (client_error). The Brooklinen linen-closet page does not cover vacuum bags. Consumer Reports' vacuum-bag test is paywalled; only the intro was visible. I found no Good Housekeeping Institute page. The down caution is sourced to Puffy (a bedding and mattress brand), Mattress Nerd, and organizer quotes in Homes & Gardens, Livingetc and Ideal Home (UK, APDO). Present it as broad expert consensus, not as lab data. The wool-compression line (row 44) is unattributed.
5. **Plastic vs breathable conflicts by fibre.** Organizers and bedding sellers favour breathable cotton bags for comforters and down (rows 49–50). Wool sources disagree: Pendleton, UMD Extension and Clemson recommend sealed or airtight containers to keep moths out (rows 57, 59). Suggested wording: clean and fully dry everything first. Use breathable cotton for down and cotton. Use a sealed bin for wool if moths are a concern.
6. **Cedar evidence is mixed.** UF/IFAS, Clemson and Oregon State say cedar has limited, fading or no effect. UMD Extension says "Cedar chests, blocks, or closets also provide protection," and Pendleton says cedar repels but does not fully protect. The defensible line: cedar is a weak, short-lived supplement and no substitute for storing items clean. The Penn State and University of Kentucky pages were not obtained: the UK Entfact ef609 PDF redirected to entomology.mgcafe.uky.edu, and the refetch was refused (PROVENANCE_REQUIRED, permission request not answered).
7. **Price and spec problems.** Several retailer pages list conflicting specs. Simplify bag (row 26): title says 40x18x6, spec table says 24x18x12. Ziploc XL (row 37): 34x27 vs 26.5x39.5. Household Essentials trunk (row 30): its "151.21 cubic inches" capacity is clearly wrong and was left out. VASAGLE (row 2): 17.6 lb is listed both per rung and as the total. The Wayfair items show only an "original" price. The DecMode and Ziploc XL listings were out of stock. Prices are as of 2026-10-26.
8. **Ladder gaps.** IKEA US does not appear to sell a dedicated blanket ladder. I found no Pottery Barn ladder page in search, and Pottery Barn was not fetched. Only Yamazaki gave real rung spacing (8.25"). Lowe's DIY guidance gives 18" center-to-center. No product page included anti-tip hardware. I found no CPSC or other authority source specific to blanket ladders. The safety rows (75–77) rely on IKEA's rail warning, Hunker and the product load limits.
9. **Ottoman and bench interiors.** The HomePop interior (row 12) comes from a brand answer in the Q&A, not the spec table. Lucinda (row 11) lists compartment dimensions in its specs.
10. **Syndicated copies.** The Martha Stewart (row 17) and Real Simple (row 71) material was read via AOL syndication. Link the original publisher if you can find the URL. I did not obtain a separate Better Homes & Gardens, The Spruce or Good Housekeeping organizer quote. The Spruce piece on AOL had no named experts.
