## Caveats/unconfirmed

- **NFPA pages could not be read.** nfpa.org tip sheets and PDFs block fetching (robots). All smoke alarm, CO and extinguisher facts above come from USFA (FEMA) pages, which say the same things. Not confirmed from NFPA directly: the "10 feet from cooking" smoke alarm tip; the share of home fire deaths in homes with no working smoke alarms (an NFPA blog headline says "almost three out of five", but the page was not fetched); CO alarm replacement intervals. Row 11 (99% / a third never test) is from the NFPA page's meta description only, not the body text.
- **Testing CO alarms monthly:** USFA's CO page shows a "test carbon monoxide alarms" graphic but has no written monthly-testing line. Say "test with your smoke alarms" or "follow the manufacturer" instead.
- **Where to keep the extinguisher:** USFA gives no specific spot (such as "near an exit" or "in the kitchen"). It only says to keep it reachable (row 22).
- **Average first-apartment setup cost:** no credible survey was found. Row 73 ($6,000, Landing) adds up retailer and blog price lists. It is not a survey, so present it as a rough estimate or leave it out.
- **Renters insurance cost:** sources differ. NAIC says $15 to $30 a month; Triple-I/NAIC 2022 data says $171 a year (about $14 a month); Texas says about $20 a month; Washington says $10 to $18 a month. The Univ. of Illinois tenant page says "as little as $10 per month". A safe phrasing is "often $15 to $30 a month (NAIC)", plus the $171 national average.
- **Landlords requiring renters insurance:** confirmed only in general terms (TX and WA insurance regulators say "some landlords" require it). No federal rule exists; state limits were not researched.
- **Move-in photos:** confirmed from Army JAG and Univ. of Illinois. No state AG or CFPB page fetched here explicitly says "take photos at move-in". The California courts and Minnesota AG pages cover deadlines but not move-in photos. Massachusetts requires a written Statement of Condition (rows 51 and 52).
- **Security deposit deadlines differ by state:** NY 14 days, CA 21, MN 21, TX 30, MA 30. That is enough to say "anywhere from 14 to 30 days depending on your state." No 50-state source (such as NCSL) was fetched.
- **Utilities "2 to 3 weeks before":** not confirmed in that wording. Sources say at least one month ahead (Univ. of Illinois), up to 60 days ahead (PG&E), and up to 30 days ahead (Con Edison). A safe phrasing is "2 to 4 weeks before move-in." Internet is mentioned only as "cable television" or a phone company. No FCC page was fetched.
- **HUD severe cost burden (50%):** not confirmed. The fetched HUD page defines only the 30% threshold.
- **USDA FSIS food thermometer page:** fetch failed. Thermometer advice comes from FoodSafety.gov instead (row 82).
- **Rug and sofa sizing:** not researched. No authoritative non-retailer source exists; retailer guides only.
- **Cleaning supply list:** no credible government list was found (a Virginia Cooperative Extension PDF exists, but its text was not available). Use the CDC basics: soap/detergent, water, an EPA-registered disinfectant, gloves.
