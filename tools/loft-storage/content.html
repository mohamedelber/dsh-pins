How to Board a Loft for Storage Safely

Before you add a single box, check how the loft is built. Most loft floors are just ceiling joists with insulation between them, designed to hold up the ceiling below, not a floor full of storage.

Which? recommends 270 mm of mineral wool loft insulation and warns that squashing it under boards reduces how well it works, which is why raised loft legs or a raised floor are used to lift boards above it. Council guidance on boarding out a loft for storage adds three practical rules: boarding should not compress the insulation, it should not block the ventilation the loft needs to avoid condensation, and it should leave flues alone. It also limits boarded lofts to light storage.

If you plan to use the loft as a room, the rules change. The Planning Portal explains that building regulations approval is required to convert a loft into a liveable space, and that existing joists are usually not designed for significant extra weight, so even boarding for storage can need approval. Check with your local council or building control before a big boarding job.

How to Organise a Loft in 6 Steps

  1. Clear a landing space. Bring everything down to a spare room or landing so you can see what you have.
  2. Declutter. Donate, sell or recycle what nobody has used in the last few years.
  3. Check the floor. Make sure you have a safe walkway of boards on the joists, raised above the insulation.
  4. Box it up. Use identical clear lidded boxes, one category per box, and label the ends.
  5. Zone by use. Things you need every year go nearest the hatch; rarely used items go furthest away.
  6. Keep a list. Note what is in each box on your phone so you only go up when you need to.

Loft Storage Ideas for Boarded Lofts

1. Raise the Boards on Loft Legs

Loft legs are plastic or metal stilts that screw onto the joists and hold the boards above the insulation, so you get a storage floor without squashing it. They are the most common way to board a loft that already has a full 270 mm of insulation. Keep the boarded area to a central walkway and leave the edges near the eaves clear.

2. Use Identical Clear Lidded Boxes

Matching clear boxes stack neatly, keep out dust and let you see what is inside with a torch. Lids also help keep out insects. Choose a size you can carry up a loft ladder with one hand free, and avoid open cardboard boxes, which soften in damp air.

3. Add Low Shelving Between the Rafters

A low, freestanding shelving unit on the boards doubles your storage without stacking boxes four high. Keep it below the slope of the roof, put the heaviest boxes on the bottom shelf and leave a gap behind it so air can move. Our DIY shelves guide shows simple builds you can adapt.

4. Zone the Loft by How Often You Need Things

Put the things you fetch every year, such as Christmas decorations and suitcases, closest to the hatch. Keep rarely used items like keepsakes and spare bedding further back. For the festive boxes, our Christmas decoration storage ideas help you pack them well.

5. Keep a Clear Central Walkway

A straight path of boards from the hatch to the far end means you never have to step between joists, where a foot can go straight through the plasterboard ceiling below. Store boxes either side of the walkway, never on it.

6. Light It Properly

A dark loft is where accidents happen. A battery LED lantern or a rechargeable light is the simple fix; a fixed light switched from the landing should be fitted by a qualified electrician. The council guidance linked above notes that sockets are not allowed in lofts boarded out under its scheme, so check your local rules before adding any.

7. Fit a Proper Loft Ladder

Carrying boxes up a stepladder is the riskiest part of using a loft. A folding loft ladder with a handrail makes access safer and quicker. If you need a new or larger hatch, check current building regulations first, as the council guidance points out.

8. Protect What You Store From Heat and Damp

Lofts get hot in summer and cold and damp in winter. The Library of Congress advises against storing paper in attics and basements, so keep photos, documents and keepsakes in the house. Store bedding and soft toys in sealed bags inside boxes, and keep candles, paint and electronics out of the loft altogether.

Loft Conversion and Eaves Storage Ideas

A converted loft has the same awkward slopes but more chances for built-in storage. These ideas use the low spaces under the roof that are too short to stand in.

9. Build Drawers Into the Eaves

Pull-out drawers set into the low knee wall turn dead space under the slope into a chest of drawers that takes up no floor space. They suit loft bedrooms where a wardrobe will not fit. See our box room bedroom ideas for other ways to make a small bedroom work.

10. Line Eaves Cupboards With Boxes

Behind a small door, the triangular eaves space is ideal for suitcases and seasonal items. Put the tallest boxes at the front and the smallest at the back where the roof gets low, and add a battery puck light so you can see in.

11. Fit Shelves That Follow the Slope

Built-in shelves that step down with the roof line use every centimetre of a sloping wall on a loft landing or in a bedroom. Keep heavy books on the lower shelves and display pieces at the top.

12. Choose a Bed With Storage

In a loft bedroom, an ottoman or drawer bed holds bedding and out-of-season clothes without using wall space you do not have. Our small bedroom storage ideas cover the best styles.

13. Put Wardrobes Where the Ceiling Is Highest

Full-height hanging needs about 1.7 m of headroom for long coats, so place a wardrobe or open rail under the ridge and use the low sides for drawers and baskets.

Small Loft and Rented Home Ideas

14. Board Just the Area by the Hatch

If you rent or your loft is small, you do not need to board all of it. A few raised boards around the hatch give you space for lightweight boxes; ask your landlord before fixing anything. For more space-saving ideas inside the home, try our under stairs storage ideas and airing cupboard shelves.

15. Use Vacuum Bags for Bulky Soft Items

Duvets, pillows and winter coats take up most of a small loft. Vacuum bags shrink them to a fraction of the size; put the bags inside a lidded box so they do not get punctured.

16. Do a Yearly Loft Check

Once a year, ideally when you bring the Christmas boxes down, check for damp patches, droppings or damage and pass on anything you no longer need. If you are moving soon, our moving box calculator helps you plan the boxes.

Common Loft Storage Mistakes

  • Laying boards straight on top of insulation. Squashed insulation keeps your home less warm.
  • Overloading the joists. Ceiling joists are not a floor; keep storage light.
  • Blocking the eaves. Boxes against the eaves can stop air flowing and lead to condensation.
  • Storing paper and photos up there. Heat and damp damage them; keep them in the house.
  • Using open cardboard boxes. They soften, collapse and let in insects.
  • Stepping between the joists. Stay on the boards so you do not go through the ceiling.

Loft Storage FAQ

Can you put boards on top of loft insulation?

You should not lay boards directly on top of loft insulation, because squashing it reduces how well it insulates. Instead, fit loft legs or a raised floor on the joists so the boards sit above the insulation, which Which? recommends at 270 mm for mineral wool, and leave the eaves clear for ventilation.

Do you need building regulations to board a loft?

Boarding a loft for light storage is often done without approval, but the Planning Portal warns that joists are usually not designed for significant extra weight and that you may still need approval. Converting a loft into a room always needs building regulations approval, so check with your local council first.

What should you not store in a loft?

Avoid storing photos, documents, paper keepsakes, candles, paint, aerosols, electronics and anything heavy in the loft. Heat, cold and damp damage paper and electronics, and heavy items can overload the ceiling joists. Keep the loft for light, robust items such as suitcases, bedding in sealed bags and seasonal decorations.

How do you organise a small loft?

Board a walkway by the hatch on raised loft legs, use identical clear lidded boxes and keep a list of what is in each box. Store the items you need every year nearest the hatch and rarely used things further back, and use vacuum bags to shrink bulky bedding.

Final Thoughts

The best loft storage ideas start with a safe floor: raised boards, full-depth insulation and clear eaves. After that, matching boxes, simple zones and a yearly check keep the loft useful instead of a place where things disappear.
