## Suggested idea list (10–12), with the facts behind each

1. **Measure the "chair zone" first.** 60 cm is the tight minimum, 75 cm is comfortable, and 100–110 cm lets someone walk behind you (#1–4, #6).
2. **Size the table at 60 cm per diner.** That gives 120 cm for four and 90 cm minimum width (#7–10).
3. **Go round, on a pedestal.** 90–100 cm seats four, 70–80 cm seats two (#13–14, #16–17, #23–24).
4. **Extending tables.** Pay for six-seat capacity only when you use it: ÅLHULT 120/170 cm £149, VIHALS 120/180 cm £99 (#18, #25).
5. **Drop-leaf and wall-mounted tables.** HAUGA £99, NORBERG £39 folding to near-zero, Clifford 41 cm folded (#19–22).
6. **Banquette against the wall.** The wall side needs 30 cm instead of 90 cm, which saves about 1 m. Use a 45 cm seat height and 45–50 cm depth (#5, #26–32).
7. **Storage benches with drawers** (#33–35).
8. **Slim sideboard, 30–40 cm deep, not 42–45 cm** (#62–66).
9. **Wall shelves and ladder shelving** (#63, #67–68).
10. **Pendant at 70–90 cm above the table, 1/2–2/3 of the table width** (#43–51).
11. **Rug at the table size plus 60 cm on each side; mirror; light colours** (#52–61).
12. **Make it a desk too.** 27% of homeworkers already use the kitchen or dining room (#71–72).

Worked example for the article (author's arithmetic, not sourced): a 120 x 80 cm table with 75 cm on every side needs (120+150) x (80+150) = 270 x 230 cm. Put a banquette on one long side with a 30 cm allowance and it needs 270 x 185 cm.

## Caveats

- **Verbatim status.** WebFetch summarises pages. Items marked [v] were returned as exact sentences. Items marked [s] were returned in quote marks but I could not check them word for word, which covers all the designer quotes from Ideal Home, H&G, SheerLuxe and Livingetc. Check these on the live pages before you use them as direct quotes. #23 runs to 16 words and #68 to 15, so trim them.
- **bookabuilderuk.com** is a UK trade-directory blog, not a standards body. Its figures are consistent with Oak Furnitureland, La Redoute and IKEA, but cite it as "UK builders' guide" rather than as an authority. Its lighting article (#46–47) is a separate page on the same site.
- **Clearance figures differ between sources** (60 / 75 / 90 / 100–110 cm). Present them as tight / comfortable / walk-behind tiers, not as one number.
- **Lighting heights differ too.** Tom Raffield says 60 cm, Oak Furnitureland 75–85 cm, Tala 75–90 cm, Bookabuilder 70–90 cm. "70–90 cm" covers most of them.
- **IKEA rug rule (#52)** comes from IKEA Switzerland's English page. The IKEA GB version of the same article (pub69273980) did not appear in search, so Oak Furnitureland (#53) is the UK backup.
- **IKEA GB product rows (#17–19)** come from the category listing. The summariser took seat counts from image text. Check each product page before publishing. The NORDEN gateleg GB product page fetch was refused (PROVENANCE_REQUIRED / permission timeout): https://www.ikea.com/gb/en/p/norden-gateleg-table-white-10423886/ . NORDEN was not in the GB dining-tables listing, so it may be discontinued in the UK.
- **John Lewis**: the Iona price of £374.25 looks like a sale price. The X-Ray extending page returned no specs. The Enza small sideboard (D45 cm) is no longer available. A Habitat product with a confirmed price was not found (gap).
- **Dunelm**: the Fulton storage bench, Maddox bench and woven storage bench pages returned only a "Winter Sale" holding page, so I could not confirm a storage bench with dimensions. Montreal (#35) is a plain bench without storage.
- **NDSS** figures come from the Essex Design Guide and Urbanist Architecture, which reproduce the DCLG March 2015 table. The gov.uk PDF itself was not fetched. NDSS sets no dining-specific minimum.
- **London Housing Design Guide (#40)** is the 2010 interim edition. The London Plan/Housing SPG carried these minimums forward, but I did not verify that, so cite it as "London Housing Design Guide (2010)".
- **English Housing Survey**: I found no sourced figure for the share of homes that are open plan or have a kitchen-diner (gap). YouGov 2024 (#41) is the best proxy. The Halifax/Country Life figure (#42) is from 2008, so use it only as history, if at all.
- **Ideal Home's small-dining article** quotes Helena Davies saying "at least ten inches between chairs". It is in inches only, so I did not use it.
- **Livingetc's "make it look bigger" article** quotes mainly US designers. I left them out to keep the piece UK-focused.
- **Banquette "saves 1 m" (#30)** is one designer's estimate. The 90 cm vs 30 cm comparison (#5) is the more defensible, measurable version: it saves 60 cm per wall side.

## SERP notes

- **Barker & Stonehouse** ("10 Small Dining Room Ideas That Make a Big Impact", by Emma Hughes): 10 headings (bright room, extending table, mirror, space-efficient seating, storage, intimate feel, blend rooms, circular table, décor, stick to walls). It has **no measurements, no clearance figures and no expert quotes.**
- **Dulux UK** (small dining room décor ideas): colour-led. It names colours (Cornflower White, Egyptian Cotton, California Days, and others) but has no measurements and no experts. There is an opening for one colour idea with named paints.
- **Ideal Home** (28 ideas, updated 24 Oct 2025): it has many UK brand-spokesperson quotes but **no cm figures** (only "ten inches between chairs"). This is the strongest editorial competitor for quotes, and a weak one on numbers.
- **Homes & Gardens** dining layout piece: 36 mostly US designers and no figures for clearance or pendants. The H&G banquette article does have Jamie Blake's 90 cm / 30 cm figures.
- **House & Garden gallery, Pinterest and IKEA GB gallery** were not fetched (the H&G gallery did not appear in searches; Pinterest is image-only). From the brief, they are image galleries.
- **The gap to fill**: none of the top results gives a clearance table, a seats-per-size table, banquette dimensions or pendant height and width rules in cm. A "planning numbers" box near the top (60 cm per diner, 75/100 cm clearances, 90–100 cm round for four, 45 cm banquette seat, 70–90 cm pendant drop, rug = table + 60 cm) is the differentiator.
- **Secondary keywords** (living/lounge + dining, about 1.1K combined): add an H2 such as "Small living and dining room ideas" covering rug zoning (#61), the London LDK minimums (#40), the YouGov sofa-dining stat (#41) and the dining-table-as-desk point (#71).
