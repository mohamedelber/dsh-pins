Small Dining Room Measurements to Plan Around

Retailers agree on the basics. IKEA says each seat needs 60 centimetres in width, so a table for four should be at least 120 cm long, and Oak Furnitureland says that in a smaller space your table should be no narrower than 90cm to allow for comfortable place settings, though plenty of 120 cm tables, such as IKEA's ÅLHULT, are 80 cm wide. For space around the table, a UK builders' guide calls 60 cm from table edge to wall a tight minimum, Oak Furnitureland recommends a minimum of 75cm of space around the dining table, and La Redoute suggests at least 90cm between the edge of the table and the nearest wall. If people need to walk behind a seated diner, the builders' guide says to aim for around 1,000 to 1,100 millimetres.

Tables That Fit

1. Choose a Round Table on a Pedestal

A round table has no corners to walk around, and a single central leg leaves room for knees and chairs. Rebecca Snowden of Furniture and Choice told Ideal Home that round tables with a central pedestal are perfect due to their smaller footprint. As a guide, a table 900 to 1,000 millimetre across seats four and 70 to 80 cm seats two. The John Lewis Iona, for example, is 100 cm across and offers seating for up to 4 diners, and IKEA's VIHALS round table is 107 cm and costs £59. Both stand on legs rather than a pedestal, so check where the legs fall before you choose chairs.

2. Size a Rectangular Table at 60 cm a Head

La Redoute's chart puts a 120cm table at four people and 180 cm at six. A UK builders' guide notes that wide carver chairs can add 100 to 150 millimetres per person, so in a small room, armless chairs that tuck fully under the table save the most space. An 80 cm wide table, like the one in our diagram, is a common size for four; go up to 90 cm if you want more room for place settings and serving dishes.

3. Buy for Four, Extend for Six

If you host a few times a year, pay for the extra seats only when you use them. Adam Brown of The Painted Furniture Company told Ideal Home that the flexibility of an extending table is crucial. IKEA's ÅLHULT extends from 120 to 170 cm for £149, and its VIHALS from 120 to 180 cm for £99. Store the leaf where you can reach it, or you will never use it.

4. Fold It Away: Drop-Leaf and Wall-Mounted Tables

In a kitchen or a studio flat, a table that folds flat against the wall is the biggest saving of all. IKEA's NORBERG wall-mounted drop-leaf table is 74 x 60 cm when it is up, seats 2 and costs £39. The freestanding HAUGA drop-leaf goes from 55 to 109 cm long for £99, and Dunelm's Clifford is just 41 cm wide with both leaves down, opening to 152 cm and comfortably seats up to four people. Seating That Saves Space

5. Build a Banquette Along the Wall

Chairs need room to pull out; a fixed bench does not. Jamie Blake of Blakes London told Homes & Gardens that you should allow about 90 cm between a chair and the wall as a minimum, but you can reduce that down to as little as 30cm if the seat fits directly onto the wall. Interior designer Christian Bense told SheerLuxe that building in a banquette in an open-plan room effectively buys you an extra metre in the room. For comfort, aim for a finished seat height of about 450 millimetres with a standard 75 cm table, a seat 45 to 50 cm deep and 60 cm of bench per adult. A UK builders' guide puts a carpenter built bench without upholstery at £600 to £1,200, and a fully upholstered bespoke banquette at £1,200 to £3,000.

6. Put Drawers Under the Bench

A built-in bench is also storage. Interior designer Clare Gaskin told Homes & Gardens: we're big fans of drawers, rather than lid-style lift up bench storage, because you do not have to clear the seat to get at what is inside. Keep table linen, candles and the spare table leaf there.

7. Swap Chairs on One Side for a Bench

Even without building anything, a loose bench that slides right under the table takes less room than two chairs. Kris Manalo of Atkin and Thyme told Ideal Home that adding bench seating rather than chairs is a way to save space. Dunelm's Montreal 2 Seater Dining Bench, for example, has a seat 122 cm wide and 48 cm high (£249).

Storage, Light and Making It Feel Bigger

8. Choose a Slim Sideboard

A UK builders' guide suggests you aim for units between 300 and 400 millimetres deep in a small dining room. That is a real saving: Dunelm's Georgi slim sideboard is D 30cm (£199), while a standard IKEA BESTÅ combination is 42 cm deep. Oak Furnitureland says to allow 80 to 120cm of space in front of drawers and dressers so they can open comfortably. Our storage ideas for small spaces have more shallow options.

9. Go Up the Walls

Floating shelves or a ladder shelf hold glasses, cookbooks and serving dishes without any floor at all. Rebecca Snowden suggests you utilise wall space like installing floating shelves. 10. Hang One Pendant, Low and the Right Size

A pendant over the table marks out the dining area and keeps the floor and table clear of lamps. UK lighting maker Tala suggests hanging it 75–90cm above table surface, and says the pendant diameter should be about 1/2 to 2/3 the width of the table, so a 40 to 50 cm shade suits an 80 cm wide table. For a long table, use two smaller pendants; a UK builders' guide says 600 mm to 800 mm between fittings works well, measured between the centres. 11. Use a Rug, a Mirror and Light Walls

A rug under the table should be big enough that the chairs stay on it when they are pulled out: Oak Furnitureland says to leave at least 60cm on each side of the table, and IKEA's rug guide suggests 60-70 centimetres on both sides. Our rug size calculator does the sum. Magdalena Gierasinska of Barker and Stonehouse told Ideal Home that a well-placed mirror can also work wonders to create the illusion of space; hang it opposite or beside a window so it reflects daylight. For walls, Lucy Mather of Arighi Bianchi says light or neutral colours usually work best in a small room.

Small Living and Dining Room Ideas

Many UK homes do not have a separate dining room. The 2010 London Housing Design Guide set the minimum combined floor area of living, dining and kitchen spaces for a two-person home at just 23 m², and a YouGov poll found that only 45% of Britons say they eat their dinner at a dining table, against 42% who have dinner on the sofa or an armchair. If your table shares a room, give it its own zone: Amy Hicks of Blakes London told Homes & Gardens that placing a rug under the dining table helps to zone the dining area. A pendant over the table and a sofa with its back to the dining space do the same job. Our room divider ideas go further.

12. Make the Table Work Twice

A dining table is often the desk too. The 2024 Skills and Employment Survey found that 27% of people who work from home do it in spaces intended for other purposes such as the kitchen or dining room. Keep a box or a drawer in the sideboard for the laptop and papers, so the table can be cleared for dinner in a minute.

If You Only Do One Thing

Measure from the table edge to the wall on every side. If any side is under 60 cm, a different table shape or a banquette on that side will help more than any decor change. Then light the table with one low pendant.
