## 1. Minimum size
- **Absolute minimum 4x4 ft [2+]**
  - CC: "The minimum viable size for a walk-in closet is about 4' x 4'"
  - HG: "The absolute minimum size for a walk-in closet is 4 by 4 feet (16 square feet)"
  - HUN-A: "Walk-in closets should be a minimum of 4 feet wide by 4 feet deep."
- **Practical minimum 5x5 ft [2+]**
  - HG: "most designers recommend no smaller than 5 by 5 feet (25 square feet) for practical daily use."
  - ANGI: "Walk-in clothes closet dimensions include a minimum of 5 feet deep by 5 feet wide."
  - LZ: "a walk-in becomes technically possible at around 5 by 5 feet, or 25 square feet"
- **Single-sided (one wall), about 4-5 ft deep [2+]**
  - HG: "A single-wall walk-in closet places all storage along one wall, requiring at least 4 to 5 feet of depth."
  - WF: "between 4' – 5' in overall depth"
  - CC: "(about 24" of actual built-in storage)."
  - DIM-S, split single-sided: "Each single sided closet should have a minimum width of 5' (1.52 m)"
- **Double-sided (two parallel walls), 6-7 ft wide [2+]**
  - HG: "You need at least 6 to 7 feet of total width to ensure 24 inches of storage depth on each side"
  - DIM-D: "6'-6.5' (183-198 cm) for mixed storage."
  - DIM-D: "7' (213 cm) for two sets of comfortable hanger rods at 24" (61 cm) each."
  - CC: "If you're including hanging rods on opposite walls, aim for a minimum of 6' of space between those walls."
  - TOH1: "walk-ins should be at least 6 feet wide to allow for a 2-foot aisle and 2 feet of hanging space on either side"
  - HUN-A: "a closet must be 6 feet wide to allow for hanging areas on both side walls"
  - CC, typical size (not a minimum): "Perfect for average closet sizes (around 7' x 7')"
- **L-shape, about 5 ft wide [2+]**
  - HG: "The minimum width for this layout is 5 feet, with depths commonly between 5 and 6 feet."
  - WF: "an L-shaped closet can have a minimum depth of 5' – 6'"
  - CC: "For this functional and compact closet (roughly 5' x 8')"
- **U-shape, about 7 ft wide (7x10 ft typical) [2+]**
  - HG: "require at least 7 feet wide and 10 feet deep."
  - WF: "A U-shaped closet should have a minimum depth of 7'"
  - CC: "For deeper spaces and the largest of the average closet (about 7' x 10'),"
- **Island, about 10x10 ft or more [2+]. Range: 10 ft (CC, HG) up to a 13 ft depth (WF).**
  - CC: "In larger areas (10' x 10' or more), consider an island layout"
  - HG: "Island walk-in closets require a minimum of 10 by 10 feet"
  - WF: "An island walk-in closet layout has a minimum depth of 13'"

## 2. Average and large sizes
- **Typical walk-in is about 6.5-7 ft x 10 ft [2+]. Range: 5x7 (WF) to 7x10.**
  - HG: "The standard walk-in closet measures approximately 6.5 to 7 feet wide by 10 feet deep"
  - CW: "A standard size is typically around 6.5 feet by 10 feet"
  - CC: "More commonly, a walk-in closet is typically at least 6' x 10'."
  - HG: "For most homes, the sweet spot is 7 by 10 feet."
  - WF (smaller figure): "The average walk-in closet size measures about 5' x 7' wide"
  - Note: WF also says "about 7' x 10' or 100 square feet of area". 7x10 is really 70 sq ft, so don't reuse that number.
- **By square feet [2+]**
  - LE (Coates): "A small walk-in closet is typically around 25-30 square feet."
  - LE (Coates): "A medium walk-in closet usually ranges from 40-70 square feet"
  - HG table: "Average walk-in (single user) | 6.5 x 8 feet | 52 sq ft"
  - HG table: "Average walk-in (two users) | 7 x 10 feet | 70 sq ft"
- **Large or primary-suite walk-in is about 100-120+ sq ft [2+]**
  - LE (Coates): "A large walk-in closet can be 100 square feet or more"
  - ANGI: "A large walk-in closet is 120 square feet or larger"
  - HG: "Luxury walk-in closets typically start at 10 by 12 feet (120 square feet)"
- No source found that gives a measured national (Census/NAHB) average. All "average" numbers are industry rules of thumb.

## 3. Depths
- **Hanging depth 24 in (20 in at a squeeze) [2+]**
  - HG: "The comfortable recommended depth for a hanging section is 24 inches from the wall"
  - DIM-D: "comfortable recommended depth of a closet is 24" (61 cm)"
  - DIM-S: "closet depths as narrow as 20" (50.8 cm) can be used"
  - LE (Coates): "the closet needs to be at least 24 inches deep"
  - CW: "the standard depth for hanging clothes is around 24 inches"
  - HUN-R: "Hanging clothes require a closet depth of at least 24 inches."
  - Coats need more. HUN-C: "Coat closets should have a minimum of 28 inches of depth (30 inches is ideal)."
- **Shelf depth 12-16 in [2+]**
  - TOH2: "use shelves 12 to 16 inches deep."
  - LE (Juzenaite): "Shelves should be between 12-16 inches deep for storing standard clothes, shoes, and boxes"
  - HG table: "Shelf depth (general storage) | 14 – 16 inches"
  - HUN-R: "Most shoes need 12 to 14 inches of shelf depth."
  - DIM-S: "Shelf depths of 12" (30.5 cm)"
- **Drawer depth 18-24 in [2+]**
  - HG: "Drawers for folded clothes should be at least 18 inches deep, with 24-inch drawers preferred"
  - DIM-S: "a minimum of 18" (45.7 cm)" (folded-clothes drawers); deep drawers "a depth of 24" (61 cm)"
  - Shallow dresser example. TOH1: "sized at 4 feet wide and only 16 inches deep"

## 4. Walkway and island clearance
- **Aisle: 24 in minimum, 30-36 in comfortable [2+]**
  - HG: "Walkway clearance should be at least 24 inches, though 30 to 36 inches is more comfortable"
  - CC: "allow for 24" - 30" of walking space in pass-through areas."
  - HUN-A: "provide 24 inches of clear walkway space between any shelves, rods or other obstructions"
  - DIM-D: "central 36" (91.4 cm) clearance zone."
  - WF: "36" of internal clearance"
  - LZ: "About 36 inches of clear walkway."
  - LZ, two people at once: "Walkway, two people 48 in (122 cm)" [1]
- **Island clearance: 36 in on all sides is most common [2+]. Range: 24 in (HG, HUN-A) to 36 in.**
  - CC: "Adding a center island? You'll want at least 36" of clearance on all sides."
  - WF: "requires 3' of clearance on all four sides."
  - LZ: "A center island needs roughly 36 inches of clearance on all four sides"
  - HG (lower figure): "clearance of at least 24 inches around all sides of the central island."
- No source found for 42 in island clearance.

## 5. Rod heights
- **Single rod: 66 in is the standard [2+]. Range: 60-72 in.**
  - BV: "For a single-rod system, mount the rod 66 inches above the floor."
  - ANGI-H: "the rod should be 66 inches from the floor"
  - HG table: "Single rod (standard) | 66 inches"
  - HUN-C (higher): "If you plan on using only one rod, install it 72 inches from the floor."
  - HUN-R (lower): "The minimum closet rod height for a single-rod system is 60 inches."
- **Double hang: upper about 80-82 in, lower about 40 in [2+]**
  - BV: "mount the top rod 81¾ inches above the floor and the lower rod 40½ inches above the floor."
  - HUN-A: "Standard height for double-hang rods is 40.5 inches high for the low rod and 82 inches for the high rod."
  - TOH2: "place the top rod 80 to 82 inches up from the floor." and "install the bottom rod about 40 inches up from the floor."
  - ANGI-H: "your top rod should be 80 inches from the floor"
  - HG: "use double hanging rods installed at 40 and 80 inches from the floor"
  - HUN-C: "install the higher rod at 80 to 84 inches" and "the lower rod between 40 and 42 inches."
- **Long hang (dresses, coats): rod about 66 in; needs about 50-70 in of clear drop [2+]**
  - HUN-A: "66-inch-high long-hang rods work for dresses, coats and other long garments"
  - HG: "Reserve a separate area with a single rod at 66 inches for longer garments like dresses, coats, and robes."
  - TOH2: "Long dresses and coats require 50 to 60 inches of hanging space."
  - LZ: "Coats and long dresses need 60 to 70 inches of vertical clearance"
  - OSU: "Street dresses and coats 63 inches"
- **Medium hang [1]**
  - HUN-A: "Medium-hang rods, which are mounted 54 inches above the floor, are best for pants and longer garments"

## 6. Hanging rod per person and per garment
- **About 4 ft of rod per person (bare minimum) [2+]**
  - HUN-R: "The general rule is to provide 4 feet of closet rod space per person using the closet."
  - OSU: "For hanging clothes allow at least four feet of rod space per person."
  - HUN-C (coat closet): "48 inches of width for each person who uses the closet."
  - Real example, much larger. TOH1: one side has "10 feet of hanging space" and "Erik's side holds half as much."
- **Inches per garment: about 1.5-2.5 in for normal clothes, about 4 in for coats [2+]**
  - HG: "Each linear foot of rod space holds approximately 6 to 8 hangers, depending on clothing thickness." (about 1.5-2 in each)
  - HUN-R: "Factoring about 2 1/2 inches of rod space per apparel item allows ample room for reaching in for a garment."
  - Coats. OSU: "Four inches of rod space should be allowed for each coat."
  - Coats. HUN-C: "allow about 4 inches of width for an individual coat."
- **Rod span [1]**
  - HG: "A standard closet rod should not exceed 48 inches without a center support bracket to prevent sagging."
- No source found for "1 in per shirt" or "2 in per suit". Use the 6-8 hangers per foot and 2.5 in figures instead.

## 7. UK walk-in wardrobes (metric)
- **Hanging depth 60 cm [2+, but all from FCI London]**
  - FCI1: "the minimum depth for wardrobe carcassing with a standard hanging rail is 60cm internally"
  - FCI2: "at least 60 cm depth for each closet side and 90cm for the walkway"
  - FCI1: "anything under 55cm internal depth will have hangers rubbing against the wardrobe carcass or door"
  - Matches the US 24 in (61 cm) figure from DIM-D.
- **Minimum overall size: about 1.5 x 1.5 m for one person, 2 x 3 m for two people [single publisher (FCI)]**
  - FCI1: "at least 1.5m x 1.5m for one person, or 2m x 3m for a shared space"
  - FCI2 (larger figure): "requires a minimum of 1.5 metres by 2 metres"
  - FCI2: recommended "is around 2.5 by 3 metres"
  - FCI3: "the standard full-size walk-in wardrobe for two people should measure at least 2 x 3m"
- **Layout widths [1, FCI3]**
  - "Double-sided walk-in wardrobes require a minimum width of 1.8 to 2.6m."
  - "Wrap-around L-shaped walk-in wardrobes are usually 1.5m wide"
  - "U-shaped layouts are slightly larger at a minimum width of 2.1m."
  - Matches the US numbers: 6-7 ft (1.8-2.1 m) double-sided, 5 ft (1.5 m) L-shape, 7 ft (2.1 m) U-shape.
- **Aisle width: 75-120 cm. Sources disagree, even within FCI.**
  - FCI2: "a comfortable 90cm walkway"
  - FCI1: "at least 1.2m of clear width for the walking aisle itself"
  - HZ (NZ): "a comfortable 820-millimetre-wide aisle"; also "aisles of about 750 millimetres"
  - FCI2, island: "at least a one-metre clearance on all sides of the island"
- **Tight galley example (Houzz NZ) [1]**
  - HZ: "A simple galley-style walk-in wardrobe can be comfortable in spaces as small as 1950 millimetres."
- Hammonds UK page was blocked (permission timeout) and not checked. No Sharps or Ideal Home page with numbers was found.

## Quick defaults for the calculator
- Hanging depth: 24 in (61 cm)
- Shelf depth: 12-16 in
- Drawer depth: 18-24 in
- Aisle: 24 in minimum, 36 in comfortable
- Island clearance: 36 in
- Rods: single 66 in; double 80-82 in and 40 in
- Rod per person: 4 ft minimum
- Rod per garment: about 2 in (6-8 per ft); coats 4 in
- Minimum widths: single-sided 4-5 ft, L-shape 5 ft, double-sided 6-7 ft, U-shape 7 ft, island 10 ft+
