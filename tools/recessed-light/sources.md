## 1. Spacing between lights
- **Spacing = ceiling height / 2 (8 ft ceiling gives 4 ft) [2+]**
  - HD-B: "divide the height of the ceiling by two to get the distance the lights should be from one another."
  - HD-B: "For example, an 8-foot tall ceiling should have recessed lights spaced 4 feet apart."
  - HD-L: "For even lighting throughout the room, divide the height of the ceiling by two."
  - KIC: "A good rule is to divide your ceiling height by 2 to give you the space between each light"
  - WAC: "Divide the ceiling height by two to decide how far apart these ceiling fixtures should be."
  - PH: "Divide the ceiling height by two (e.g., 8 ft ceiling = 4 ft spacing)"
  - HUN-M: "If, for example, your ceiling is 10 feet high, you should space your lights 5 feet apart."
  - HUN-S: "In a room with 12-foot ceilings, that means to space the lights 6 feet apart."
  - 1KB: "The spacing between each light fixture is your ceiling height simply divided in half."
  - LE (Mohammad Ahmed): "A good general rule for placement is to divide the height of the ceiling by two,"
- **Cap at about 6 ft between lights [1]**
  - KIC: "but don't go more than 6 feet apart, or you'll lose the impact."
- **Lower limit: at least 2 ft between fixtures [1]**
  - HZ: "leave at least 2 feet between each fixture."
- **Spacing by light size: 4 in about 4 ft apart, 6 in about 6 ft apart [1]**
  - LE: "A 4-inch recessed light fixture should be placed around 4 feet apart"
  - LE: "a 6-inch recessed light should be placed around 6 feet apart"
  - No second reputable source found. Don't use this as the main rule. It also clashes with the ceiling/2 rule: a 6 in light on an 8 ft ceiling would be 6 ft vs 4 ft.
- **Beam angle: no reputable source gives a number, only the direction [2+, qualitative]**
  - RL-S: "each cone should intersect at the surface you are illuminating."
  - LE (Libby Rawers): "Space them evenly around the room making sure that the beam spread of each light overlaps,"
  - FL: "Wider beams cover more floor and wall area, so they can sit further apart."
  - FL: "Narrower beams focus the light, which usually means tighter spacing."
  - Tall ceilings. RL-N: "For ceilings taller than 16-feet, using LED lights with a narrow flood beam angle will get more of the light down"
  - Tall ceilings. KIC: "For ceilings higher than 10ft, use 1000 lumens in your downlights to make sure light reaches the floor."
  - Dark floors. KIC: "if you have darker wood floors, darker tile or dark carpet, install the downlights closer together"
- **Accent lights 4-6 ft apart [1]**
  - HD-L: "For accent lighting, a good rule of thumb is to place the recessed lighting fixtures 4 to 6 feet apart."

## 2. Distance from walls (general lighting)
- **Wall offset = half the spacing [2+]**
  - RL-S: "The distance between the lights is always double what it is at the ends."
  - RL-P: "the distance between your lights is always twice (2x) the distance from the wall to the first light."
  - RL-P: "Divide the width of the area by twice (2x) the number of lights to be placed in that row and write down your answer."
  - HUN-M: "If you place a light 2 feet from your wall, your remaining lights should be 4 feet apart from each other."
  - RL-S, common mistake: "A common mistake is to install recessed lighting with equal spacing between the lights and the walls."
- **Fixed figures range from 1.5 ft to 3 ft. Sources disagree.**
  - HUN-M: "install lights within 1.5 to 3 feet of them."
  - PH: "Position recessed lights about 1.5–2 ft from the wall"
  - KIC: "Start with downlights no less than 2 feet away from your walls."
  - HZ: "Measure at least 2 feet off all your walls onto the ceiling to start."
  - HD-B: "Place recessed lights about 3 feet away from the wall to avoid creating shadows that make your ceilings look lower."
  - HUN-S: "A good guideline is to place lights about 3 feet from walls."
  - 1KB: "keep your lights about 3 feet from any wall as well."
  - Note: the half-spacing rule gives 2 ft for an 8 ft ceiling and 2.5 ft for a 10 ft ceiling, which sits inside the 1.5-3 ft range.
- **Wall offset by ceiling height (PH table) [1]**
  - PH: 8 ft ceiling "1.5–2 ft (50–60 cm)," 9 ft "2 ft (60 cm)," 10 ft "2–2.5 ft (60–75 cm)."

## 3. How many lights and how much light
- **Old rule: sq ft x 1.5 = total watts [2+, from the incandescent era]**
  - HZ: "Multiply the area by 1.5; this is the total wattage you'll need to light the room."
  - HZ: "Multiply 340 by 1.5, and you'll get 510, so you'll need 510 watts of total light to efficiently light your room."
  - UK-LY: "Multiply the square footage by 1.5 to give the total wattage needed in the room."
  - UK-LY (brighter rooms): "you can multiply the footage by 2.5 rather than the 1.5 figure used above."
  - Note: both examples use 40 W/60 W incandescent bulbs (HZ: "you want to use 60-watt bulbs"; UK-LY: "A 6 x 40W installation"). For LEDs, convert to lumens. Using DOE's 800 lm = 60 W, 1.5 W/sq ft works out to about 20 lm/sq ft. That figure is derived, not quoted.
- **Total lumens = sq ft x foot-candles (lumens per sq ft) [2+]**
  - DB: "multiply the square footage of your room by the number of foot-candles required for the space."
  - DB: a foot-candle is "the number of lumens cast on a one square foot area."
  - SBL: "Footcandles are the amount of lumens per square foot."
  - ALC worked example: "10-20 lumens x 250 square feet"
- **Lumens per sq ft by room: two families of numbers. They agree on living, kitchen and hallway, and disagree on bathroom and bedroom.**

| Room | HUN-L / ALC / DB (rule-of-thumb table) | PSU (IES) | SBL (IES) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Living | 10-20 | 10-20 | 10-20 |
| Bedroom | 10-20 | 10-20 | 20-50 |
| Kitchen, general | 30-40 | 30-40 | 20-50 |
| Kitchen, task (sink/stove) | 70-80 | 50-100 | 50-100 |
| Bathroom | 70-80 | 20-50 | 20-50 |
| Dining | 30-40 | 30-40 | n/a |
| Home office | n/a | 20-50 | 50-100 (difficult reading/writing) |
| Hallway | 5-10 | 5-20 | 5-10 |

  - Living [2+]. HUN-L: "10–20 lumens per square foot"; ALC: "Living Room 10-20"; PSU: "Living Rooms, Bedrooms, and Relaxation Areas | 10–20 foot candles (ambient)."
  - Kitchen general [2+]. HUN-L: "Kitchen (general lighting): 30–40 lumens per square foot"; ALC: "Kitchen General 30-40"; PSU: "Kitchen (General) | 30-40 foot candles"
  - Kitchen task [2+]. HUN-L: "Kitchen (task areas): 70–80 lumens per square foot"; ALC: "Kitchen Stove 70-80"; PSU: "Kitchen (Task Areas - Stove & Sink) | 50-100 foot candles"
  - Bathroom [2+, but they DISAGREE]. HUN-L: "70–80 lumens per square foot"; ALC: "Bathroom 70-80"; PSU: "Bathrooms | 20-50 foot candles"; SBL: "20–50"
  - Bedroom [2+]. ALC: "Bedroom 10-20"; HUN-L: "10–20 lumens per square foot"; SBL (higher): "20–50"
  - Dining [2+]. HUN-L: "30–40 lumens per square foot"; PSU: "Dining Room | 30-40 foot candles"
  - Home office [2+, they differ]. PSU: "Home Office/Reading Area | 20-50 foot candles"; SBL, "Rooms for Difficult Reading and Writing": "50–100"
  - Hallway [2+]. ALC: "Hallway 5-10"; HUN-L: "5–10 lumens per square foot"; PSU: "Hallways & Entryways | 5-20 foot candles"
  - PSU names its source: "the Illuminating Engineering Society (IES) has come up with guidance to determine the appropriate lighting levels."
  - Caveat: foot-candles measure light that lands on the surface. Fixture lumens are light that leaves the fixture. Simple calculators treat them as equal, which ignores losses, so real rooms may need somewhat more. No source gave a loss factor.
- **Minimum lumens per fixture [1]**
  - RL-N: "Minimum Brightness: 600 lumens" (fixed downlight); "Minimum Brightness: 750 lumens" (gimbal)
  - RL-N: "Most often you'll be better off installing brighter lights rather than more lights."

## 4. Foot-candles / lux by room (IES, US)
- See the PSU and SBL columns in the table in section 3. Both pages attribute their numbers to IES. The IES site itself was not fetched, because the IES handbook values sit behind a paywall.
- No energy.gov page with per-room foot-candles was found.
- Conversion: 1 foot-candle = 10.764 lux. This is the standard definition, not quoted from these sources.

## 5. Wall washing, accent lighting, kitchen counters
- **Wall wash: space the lights as far apart as they are from the wall [2+]**
  - LRC: "They should be spaced as far from each other as they are from the wall."
  - 1KB (track): "Space the fixtures the same distance apart as the track is from the wall to evenly spread out the light."
  - Note: this is the opposite of the general-lighting rule, where spacing is twice the wall offset.
- **Wall wash distance: at least 1.5 ft, typically 1.5-3 ft [2+]**
  - LRC: "Wall-wash luminaires mounted closer to the wall than 1 foot 6 inches produce harsh, scalloped patterns of light on the wall."
  - PH (wall washing): "1.5–2 ft (50–60 cm) from the wall"
  - 1KB (track): "Mount the track 2 to 3 feet from the wall as long as your ceiling is nine feet or less."
  - Wall grazing (texture) goes closer. 1KB: "mount the track closer, about 6 to 12 inches from the wall"
- **Accent / art lights: aim about 30 degrees [2+]**
  - RL-P: "The optimal aiming angle to minimize glare is 30-degrees from the ceiling" (the page then works this out as a 60-degree angle measured from the wall)
  - 1KB: "tilt your track lights about 30 degrees, using the vertical from floor to ceiling as your zero-degree starting point."
  - Derived (not quoted): at a 30-degree aim from vertical, wall distance = (ceiling height - height of the art's center) x 0.577. Example: 9 ft ceiling, art centered at 5 ft, gives about 2.3 ft from the wall.
  - 1KB: "Usually, you only need one fixture for each object being lit."
- **Kitchen: distance from upper cabinets. Sources DISAGREE; 12-18 in is most common [2+]**
  - LE (Libby Rawers): "Make sure recessed lighting is placed 12- 18 inches from a cabinet so that there aren't any dark spots,"
  - MOD: "install recessed light 12-18 inches away from the wall above countertops and cabinets."
  - HD-L: "Leave about 14 to 18 inches of space between the light cannisters and the cabinets to avoid lost light."
  - 1KB (further out): "In your kitchen, set your recessed fixtures 18 to 22 inches from your cabinets to avoid unwanted shadows."
  - PB (builder, furthest): "36 inches off the back wall, which puts it 24 inches in front of the cabinet." Also: "Now typically I see a lot of builders that put the lights 12 inches in front he cabinets, and that simply is not enough."
- **Over the counter edge [1]**
  - MOD: "Place lights 12-18 inches from the edge of the countertops."
  - No reputable source was found that says "center over the counter edge" word for word. LRC kitchen page (lrc.rpi.edu .../lightingRooms/kitchen.asp) says only: "Locate these fixtures to the side and slightly in front of the position where a person would usually stand to see the task."
- **Kitchen fixture size [2+]**
  - HD-B: "Choose lights that are 4 inches or 5 inches in diameter to provide enough concentrated light for meal prep and cleanup."
  - LE: "If you're planning to fit the ceiling of a large and open-plan kitchen, a 6 inch fixture will work."

## 7. Bathroom wet rating and IC rating (keep short)
- **Over a shower: use a wet-location-listed fixture or shower trim [2+]**
  - HD-B: "This trim is specifically designed for installation above the bathtub and in shower stalls."
  - HD-L: "Specialty lighting has specific trims and is typically used for showers, steam rooms or other wet locations."
  - HALO4 / HALO6: "Wet location-listed: For all application including showers and covered outdoor spaces"
  - JUNO6S: "Certified for wet locations"
  - ECM (NEC 2005 wording, check the current NEC edition): "Luminaires (lighting fixtures) located in this zone shall be listed for damp locations" "or listed for wet locations where subject to shower spray." The zone is "the area measured three feet horizontally and eight feet vertically from the top of the bathtub rim"
- **IC-rated can touch insulation; non-IC needs about 3 in of clearance [2+]**
  - LRC: "A recessed luminaire installed in an insulated ceiling or roof cavity must be IC rated."
  - LRC: "IC-rated luminaires are designed so insulation can cover and abut the luminaire."
  - TOH: "Non-IC-rated fixtures require a minimum of three inches of clearance from insulation."
  - 1KB-IC: "Non-IC rated fixtures require at least 3 inches of space between the metal of the can and the ceiling insulation."
  - NORA: non-IC "requires a minimum clearance from insulation, typically 3 inches"
  - HD-L: "make sure any insulation is at least 3 inches away from all sides of the housing."
  - Spray foam caveat. NORA: "maintain 3 inch clearance from spray foam to luminaire."
  - Examples. HALO4 / HALO6: "AIR-TITE, IC-Rated, and ENERGY STAR(R) certified"; JUNO6S: "IC Rated for direct contact with insulation"

## 8. UK / metric downlights

  - UK-LY: "Dividing the ceiling height by two is a way of gauging how much space to leave between each downlight."

## Calculator defaults
- **Spacing (S) = ceiling height / 2.** Clamp to 3-6 ft (KIC cap of 6 ft). Metric: H/2, so 2.4 m gives 1.2 m.
- **Wall offset = S / 2.** For 8-10 ft ceilings this gives 2-2.5 ft, which sits inside the 1.5-3 ft range. Show "about 2-3 ft" as help text.
- **Grid count:** lights per row = round(room length / S), lights per column = round(room width / S), total = rows x columns. Then check total lumens against the target below. If it falls short, suggest brighter lights over more lights (RL-N).
- **Target lumens = sq ft x lm/sq ft.** Defaults (range midpoints):
  - Living room 15 (10-20)
  - Bedroom 15 (10-20)
