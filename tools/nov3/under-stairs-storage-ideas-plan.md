### A. New H2 "Walk-In Under Stairs Cupboard Ideas" (about 300–380 words + 1 SVG)
**Where:** insert it **before** the H2 "Ways to Use the Space as a Nook", which is directly after idea #10. Do not touch the closet intro or ideas #5–10.

**Outline:**
1. **Opening paragraph.** A walk-in cupboard is worth it when the tall end is close to door height and the space is deep enough to stand in. Otherwise pull-outs beat a walk-in. Say this in the first sentence after the heading.
2. **H3 "Check the door and standing space first".**
   - A standard UK internal door is 1981 mm high and 762 mm wide. Compact cupboards often use 610 mm doors.
   - Keep a standing strip of about 90 cm.
   - Put shelves on one side only if the cupboard is narrower than the strip plus the shelf depth.
3. **H3 "An L-shaped layout that uses the whole slope".**
   - Hooks and the vacuum go at the tall end, by the door.
   - Shelves 30–40 cm deep run along the long back wall, with shallower shelves up high.
   - A rolling crate or pull-out sits under the lowest part.
   - A battery motion light or LED strip goes at the entrance.
4. **H3 "Walk-in larder (pantry) version".** Use this when the cupboard is next to the kitchen.
   - Narrow shelves at eye level for tins and jars.
   - The floor for drinks and bulk packs.
   - Link `pantry-shelving-ideas` for shelf depths, and `pantry-organization-ideas`.
5. **H3 "Keep meters and the stair safe" (UK).**
   - Keep the gas meter and electric meter reachable, and don't pack boxes around them.
   - Gas emergency number: 0800 111 999.
   - In a loft-converted or three-storey house, the stair is usually a protected route, so ask building control before adding a new door or opening.
   - In the US, the IRC asks for 1/2-inch gypsum inside enclosed under-stair storage (the source is already cited on the page).
6. **One cross-link sentence:** for the door itself, link to `under-stairs-cupboard-doors`. Do not repeat its door-type content.

### C. FAQ additions and fixes (PAA-style, 40–55 words each)
**Where:** inside the FAQ, after "How do you use the space under stairs in a kitchen?" and **before** the link paragraphs. Then insert one H2 "More Under Stairs Guides" above the 4 link paragraphs so they leave the FAQ. That is one heading insert with no moving. Optionally add `storage-ideas-for-small-spaces` there, which supports the "for small spaces" query (320/month).

1. **What is a cupboard under the stairs called?** In the UK it is usually called the understairs cupboard or the cupboard under the stairs. In the US it is an under-stair closet. A larger one you can step into is a walk-in understairs cupboard, and one used for food is an understairs larder or pantry. Open versions are often called stair nooks.
2. **Can you store things under the stairs?** Yes. In a home it is one of the best storage spots, but keep the gas and electric meters easy to reach and don't pack boxes around them. Cheshire West and Chester Council says gas cupboards "need to be easily accessible for maintenance, inspection, and emergencies." Keep the stairs and hall clear as an escape route.
3. **Can you make a walk-in cupboard under the stairs?** Often, yes, if the tall end is close to door height. A standard UK internal door is 1981 mm high, and compact ones are 610 mm wide. Leave a standing strip of about 90 cm, put shelves along the back wall and add a light. If you can't stand inside, pull-out drawers use the space better.
4. **How much does under stairs storage cost?** (replaces the current answer) Freestanding cube units cost about £22–£40. A DIY set of pull-out drawers can cost about £80 in materials. A fitted three-door cupboard typically costs £1,000–£2,500, around £1,750 on average according to Checkatrade, and adding drawers costs about £400 more. In the US, DIY projects run about $200–$500, while custom built-in drawers cost $2,000–$5,000.
5. **Do you need planning permission for under stairs storage?** Checkatrade says "planning permission isn't normally needed for fitted under stairs storage inside your home." Building regulations matter more if the stair is a protected escape route, as in many loft conversions and three-storey homes. Check with building control before adding a new door there.
