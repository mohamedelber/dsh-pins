### Edit 2: NEW H2 "Sample Cut List for a 5-Foot Reach-In Closet" (biggest addition)

**Placement:** directly after the "What You Need" materials image and before "How to Build... in 7 Steps". It will appear in the TOC.

**Content outline (about 180 words + 1 table):**
- Intro line: the example is a 60" wide x 24" deep reach-in with one rod and two shelves above it. Tell readers to swap in their own width and depth.
- **Table: Cut list** (columns: Part | Qty | Material | Size)
  - Shelves | 2 | 3/4" plywood | 14" x 59 7/8" (1/8" fit allowance is our recommendation)
  - Front edge strips | 2 | 1x2 | 59 7/8"
  - Back cleats | 2 | 1x2 | 60" (closet width)
  - Side cleats | 4 | 1x2 | 13" (about 1" shorter than the shelf depth)
  - Center support | 2 | shelf-and-rod bracket (lower) + shelf bracket (upper) | at the stud nearest the middle
  - Closet rod | 1 | 1 3/8" wood rod + 1 pair of sockets | measure between sockets
  - Screws | about 20 | 3" wood screws | two at each stud
- **Buying line:** one 4x8 sheet of plywood ripped into three 14" strips gives both shelves plus offcuts for side shelves (simple math, 3 x 14 = 42 of 48 inches). Four 8-ft 1x2s cover all strips and cleats (291 3/4" needed vs 384" bought).
- **Heights for this example:** rod at 66" to the bottom of the rod. Underside of the first shelf at about 70" (the 2" gap above the rod plus the rod's 1 3/8"). Second shelf 12" higher (about 82 3/4" underside), close to the 84" top shelf height Bob Vila gives.
- **Why the center support:** a 60" run is past the 48" limit for cleated plywood (Edit 3 table), and past the 3-ft rod bracket spacing.

  | 3/4" yellow pine | 36" |

**Sourced facts:**

### Edit 4: Add 2 FAQ items (PAA-driven), at the end of the FAQ section

**Q: Is it cheaper to build or buy closet shelves?**
Answer: DIY cleat shelves cost a fraction of a custom system. One blogger's itemized melamine build came to $56.89. Bob Vila puts a custom closet at $1,067 to $2,981, $2,005 on average, and a reach-in at $500 to $2,500. Close with the trade-off: you supply the time and tools.
- Reality Daydream, https://realitydaydream.com/custom-closet-shelving-tutorial/ : itemized total "$56.89" (melamine sheet $29.99, 1x6 x2 $13.74, 1x4 $1.49, edge banding $10.88, L-bracket $0.79).
- Bob Vila custom closet cost, https://www.bobvila.com/articles/custom-closet-cost/ : "ranges from $1,067 to $2,981 or $2,005 on average"; reach-in "$500 to $2,500"; wall-mount organizers "$140 to $240 per linear foot."

**Q: How far apart should closet rod supports be?**
Answer: no more than about 3 feet between brackets, with a center support screwed to a stud on longer rods. Wood rods are stronger than hollow telescoping rods.
- This Old House, https://www.thisoldhouse.com/storage-organization/closet-rod-reinforcement : brackets "no more than 3 feet apart"; center support at the midpoint, attached to a stud; telescopic rods "might support only 20 to 30 pounds per linear foot."
- Hunker, https://www.hunker.com/13413647/what-is-the-proper-height-for-closet-rods-shelves/ : "Hanging clothes require a closet depth of at least 24 inches."

(Optional, one sentence only: add Bob Vila's load guidance to the existing "best wood" FAQ, since PAA asks "What wood to make closet shelves?". Bob Vila (diy-closet-shelves URL above): furniture-grade plywood for medium-to-heavy loads; particleboard, pine, melamine or MDF for light-to-medium loads.)
