The best alcove shelving ideas make the most of the recesses either side of a chimney breast without fighting their odd sizes. For most homes, the winner is a low cupboard with three or four shelves above it: closed storage for clutter, open shelves for books. If your budget is small, oak floating shelves or simple shelves on battens look almost as good for a fraction of the cost. UK alcoves are typically 300–450mm deep, so measure yours first; the ideas below are grouped by budget, with the sizes that make each one work.

Alcove Shelving, Measured

Three numbers matter most. For depth, Livingetc's designer suggests at least 9 inches (about 23 cm) for paperbacks and a nice middle-ground is 12 inches (about 30 cm) for a mix of books and objects. For spacing, Hunker puts the average gap between bookshelves at 8 to 12 inches (about 20 to 30 cm). For span, 18 mm MDF sags visibly at around 600 to 700 mm, so in a wider alcove use 25 mm boards or a support in the middle. Our shelf spacing calculator works out the gaps for your height.

2. Shelves on Battens, Three Sides

The traditional way to shelve an alcove is to fix a timber batten to the back wall and both side walls, then rest the shelf on top. Practical DIY suggests ordinary planed timber (50x18mm for the battens, with the side battens cut about 38 mm shorter than the shelf depth so they're hidden from the front. Space the fixings about 450 mm apart for light loads and no more than 300 mm for heavy ones, using screws that go at least 38 mm into the wall. Because the shelf is held on three sides, this is the strongest cheap option for books.

Tip: a furniture maker on MyBuilder suggests heavily books or load are best toward the ends of the shelves, not in the middle, where shelves sag.

3. Adjustable Twin-Slot Shelving

If you are renting or your books keep changing, twin-slot uprights let you move shelves without new holes. Wickes' 320 mm twin-slot bracket costs £4.50 and has a maximum weight loading of 24kg per bracket, with shelves up to 450 mm deep. Paint the uprights the wall colour and they almost disappear.

Works best: home offices and kids' rooms. Skip it: if you want a built-in look.

4. Scaffold Board Shelves

Chunky, rustic and cheap, scaffold boards are about 38 mm thick, so they don't sag easily. B&Q sells them at 2.4m x 22.5cm x 38mm, which is enough for several alcove shelves from one board. Sand them well and seal them before use, and fix them on battens or heavy-duty brackets.

6. Lit Shelves Against a Dark Back Wall

Painting the back of an alcove a deep colour turns ordinary shelves into a display. Livingetc says dark colors are great for alcoves because they make colours pop. Add low-voltage LED strips under each shelf: Bookabuilder suggests warm white around 2700K to 3000K for living spaces, and puts the cost of supplied and fitted shelf lighting at £80 to £200 per alcove. Most strips run at 12 V or 24 V, and Simple Lighting advises you to match the driver's output voltage to the strip. Have an electrician wire anything that isn't plug-in.

Bespoke: Built-In Alcove Storage

8. Cupboards Below, Shelves Above

This is the classic for good reason. A designer in Homes & Gardens made the lower shelving being deeper to create practical internal storage, with shallower open shelves above. Bookabuilder puts bespoke alcove joinery at between £1,200 and £3,000 per side, depending on materials and finish, and shelving-only joinery with floating shelves at £150 to £400 per alcove. Paint the units the same colour as the walls for a calm, built-in look, or a contrast colour to make them a feature.

9. An Alcove Desk

A spare bedroom alcove is often exactly the right width for a desk. Fit a solid worktop wall to wall on battens, with one or two shelves above. UK office furniture sellers put standard desk height at between 69cm and 71cm, and our desk height calculator adjusts that for your own height. Keep the shelves above the desk shallow so you don't hit your head when you stand up.

10. An Alcove Wardrobe

In a bedroom, an alcove can hold a fitted wardrobe, but check the depth first. FCI London says the standard answer is 600mm minimum for a hanging rail, with 650 to 680 mm ideal. Most alcoves are shallower than that, so an alcove wardrobe usually stands forward of the chimney breast. If you don't want that, use the alcove for folded clothes on shelves behind doors, which needs less depth. Our built in wardrobe ideas have UK measurements for every type.

Before You Drill

  • Find the cables. Wiring regulations guidance says cables can run within 150mm from the top of the wall and from corners, and in line with sockets and switches, so don't drill directly above or below them. Bosch notes that a cable must have a current to be picked up by a detector, so scan with the power on, then switch it off at the consumer unit before you drill.
  • Know your wall. Brick takes plugs and screws; on a stud or lath and plaster wall, fix heavy shelving into the timber studs, as Ultimate Handyman advises.
  • Leave the chimney breast alone. Redbridge Council says removing or altering one needs an application to Building Control, and damage can let fumes in from a neighbour's flue.
  • Keep timber clear of a stove. Stove World UK says the safe distance to combustible materials such as wooden shelves can be anywhere between 300-600mm+, depending on the stove, so check your manufacturer's figure.

How Deep Should Alcove Shelves Be?

Whichever of these alcove shelving ideas you choose, the depth rule is the same. For books, 200 to 250 mm; for media or storage, 300 to 400 mm, according to Bookabuilder. Most alcoves are 300 to 450 mm deep, so a 250 to 300 mm shelf leaves a little air behind it and looks neat.
