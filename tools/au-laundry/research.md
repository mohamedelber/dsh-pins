## 2. Verified facts (URL + quote)
**Minimum laundry / what's required**
- NCC Housing Provisions 10.4(1)(c): "clothes washing facilities, comprising at least one washtub and space in the same room for a washing machine". "A kitchen sink or washbasin must not be counted as a laundry washtub." No minimum room size is given. https://cpd.nccb.gov.au/editions/ncc-2022/adopted/housing-provisions/10-health-and-amenity/part-104-facilities
- SA DPTI Advisory Notice 04/18 (Sep 2018): "laundry facilities for clothes washing purposes are permitted in a bathroom or kitchen". Where a washing machine sits there, "that area must also have a laundry tub installed". https://dit.sa.gov.au/__data/assets/pdf_file/0004/497452/Advisory_Notice_04_18_-_Laundry_facilities_in_residential_buildings_-_September_2018.pdf
- SA Housing Safety Authority (rental minimums): "Must have hot and cold plumbed running water to a laundry wash trough or basin" and "Must be adequately ventilated". https://housingsafetyauthority.sa.gov.au/minimum-housing-standards/laundry

**Appliance and tub sizes (mm)**

- F&P DH8060P1 8kg heat pump dryer: H 850, W 600, D 645 mm. "We recommend allowing an extra 150mm in depth for hoses and connections." The listing sells a separate stacking kit (A$169). https://www.winnings.com.au/p/fisher-paykel-8kg-heat-pump-dryer-dh8060p1

**Dryers**
- energyrating.gov.au: vented dryers "are expensive to run" and "pump hot, humid air into the surrounding space". "The National Construction Code requires that where venting clothes dryers are installed, they are discharged (either directly or through a shaft or duct) to outside air." Heat pump dryers are "much more energy efficient as they capture and reuse their heat energy." https://energyrating.gov.au/node/37
- NCC Housing Provisions 10.8.2(3) requires "exhaust ducting directly from the clothes dryer to outdoor air". This "only applies to venting clothes dryers". https://ncc.abcb.gov.au/editions/ncc-2022/adopted/housing-provisions/10-health-and-amenity/part-108-condensation-management

**Waterproofing (NCC 2022 Housing Provisions Part 10.2)**
- 10.2.5(2)(a): "The floor of the room must be water resistant." (b): "Wall/floor junctions must be water resistant."
- Walls behind the washing machine: water resistant "to a minimum of 75 mm above and 75 mm each side of the washing machine tap outlets".
- If there is a floor waste, the floor and junctions must be waterproof. https://ncc.abcb.gov.au/editions/ncc-2022/adopted/housing-provisions/10-health-and-amenity/part-102-wet-area-waterproofing

**Licensed trades**

**Draft FAQs (verified facts only):**
1. **What is the minimum size for a laundry in Australia?** The NCC sets no minimum room size. It does require "at least one washtub and space in the same room for a washing machine." A 600mm-wide washer next to a 499mm 45L tub cabinet fits on about 1.1m of wall. You also need roughly 900mm of standing room in front. (49 words)
2. **Can a laundry go in a bathroom?** Yes. SA's building advisory notice says laundry facilities "are permitted in a bathroom or kitchen." A washbasin does not count as the tub, though. If the washing machine is in the bathroom, a separate laundry tub must be installed there too. Moving water or waste pipes is work for a licensed plumber. (52 words)
3. **What should you not store in the laundry?** Don't keep detergents, capsules or chemicals at child height. NSW children's health advice is to store poisons "at least 1.5 metres off the ground in a child-resistant locked cupboard," in their original containers. If anything is swallowed, call the Poisons Information Centre on 13 11 26. (49 words)
4. **Do I need to vent a dryer in a small laundry?** Only if it is a vented dryer. The NCC requires vented dryers to be ducted "directly from the clothes dryer to outdoor air." Condenser and heat pump dryers are exempt. Energy Rating says heat pump models are "much more energy efficient," which suits closed, windowless laundries. (51 words)
