The best walk in robe ideas start with one decision: width. A walk-in robe with hanging on one side needs a room about 1.5 metres wide, and one with robes on both sides needs about 2.1 metres, because each run of hanging is about 600 mm deep and you want around 900 mm to walk between them. Get the layout right for your space and every other idea, from shoe towers to LED strips, slots in around it.

In Australian floor plans you will often see it shortened to WIR, which Stroud Homes notes is simply short for walk-in robe. The 10 walk in robe ideas below are grouped into layouts, the inside of the robe and finishing touches, with costs at the end.

Which Walk-In Robe Layout Fits Your Room?

Silva Wardrobe Co describes 900 to 1000 mm of walkway as everyday comfort, with 800 mm as the absolute minimum between facing units. If two people will use the robe at the same time, TNT Wardrobes suggests 1100 to 1200 mm.

Walk In Robe Layout Ideas

1. Run Robes Down Both Sides in a Galley

A galley, with hanging on both long walls, gives the most storage for a narrow room. TNT Wardrobes says two opposing hanging runs need a room width of roughly 2,100 mm. Put long hanging on one side and double hanging with shelves and drawers on the other, as in the galley robe pictured at the top, so each side does a different job.

2. Go Single-Sided in a Narrow Space

If the room is only about 1.5 metres wide, put everything on one wall and keep the other wall clear, perhaps for a full-length mirror. A single-sided robe is also the easiest to fit with flat-pack units, because every unit sits against the same wall. Our walk-in closet size guide works through the maths for other room sizes.

3. Use an L-Shape for a Corner

An L-shape wraps robes around two walls and leaves the rest of the floor open, which suits a square room or a corner of a large bedroom. Kitchenfox, an Adelaide cabinetmaker, gives about 2,400 x 1,800 mm as a minimum room for this layout. The corner itself is awkward to reach, so use it for shelves of folded clothes rather than hanging.

4. Plan a Walk-Through Robe Carefully

A walk-through robe is one where the robe forms a corridor between bedroom and ensuite. It saves a separate hallway, but your clothes sit next to a steamy bathroom. SA Health advises keeping cupboards and bedrooms uncluttered and well ventilated to prevent mould, so make sure the ensuite has a working exhaust fan and keep the door closed while you shower.

Inside the Robe

5. Make Some Sections Shallow

Not everything needs a full 600 mm. Hanging needs about 520 to 550 mm of internal depth, but Houzz Australia says shoes, underwear drawers and shelves work perfectly fine between 300 to 380 millimetres deep. In a tight galley, using shallow shelves on one side can turn an uncomfortable walkway into a usable one. Another Houzz guide notes shelving is commonly 450 millimetres deep.

6. Double Up the Hanging

Most shirts, skirts and folded trousers hang shorter than about 900 mm, so two rails stacked in one bay hold twice as much as one. Kitchenfox suggests top at 1,800mm, bottom at 900mm for double hanging, and a single rail at about 1,800 mm for long dresses and coats. Keep one bay of long hanging and make the rest double. Folding knitwear instead of hanging it saves more space; see our guide to folding clothes to save space.

7. Build a Shoe Tower

Shoes waste space when they are piled on the robe floor. A tower of shallow shelves, one pair per shelf, keeps them visible and off the walkway. Houzz Australia suggests spacing shoe shelves 220 to 260 mm apart, which suits most flat shoes; leave a few taller gaps for ankle boots. More ideas are in our shoe storage ideas.

8. Add a Shallow Drawer for Accessories

Belts, scarves, sunglasses and jewellery get lost on shelves. One shallow drawer with dividers keeps them in sight. If you are adding a freestanding chest of drawers or a tall unit rather than built-in joinery, fix it to the wall: the ACCC tells households to secure furniture and TVs using furniture straps, angle braces or anchors screwed into walls. Our jewellery storage ideas and belt storage ideas have more ways to organise small things.

Finishing Touches

9. Light It With LED Strips

A walk-in robe often has no window, and a single ceiling light leaves the shelves in shadow. LED strips under each shelf and above each rail make colours easier to see, and Kitchenfox specifies warm white 3,000K for both strips and downlights. Battery or plug-in sensor strips need no wiring. Anything hard-wired must be done by a licensed electrician: NSW Government advice says by law any electrical installation work must be done by a licensed electrician, and Energy Safe Victoria warns that unqualified electrical work is illegal.

10. Start With Flat-Pack and Upgrade Later

Custom joinery looks best but costs the most. Flat-pack systems let you start smaller and add pieces later. At the time of writing, IKEA Australia lists two PAX frames 200 x 58 x 201 cm for A$460, or A$480 in the 236 cm height, with doors and interiors extra. Bunnings sells a Flexi Storage walk-in unit with one rail and two shelves, 606 x 2000 x 480 mm, for A$199. Keep damp gear out of any robe: WA Government guidance on mould says to make sure damp footwear and clothes are not put away in wardrobes.

What Does a Walk-In Robe Cost in Australia?

Prices vary a lot with size, finish and city, so treat these as rough guides and get several quotes.

Airtasker quotes higher figures for walk-in robes, from about A$20,000 for simple designs and sometimes over A$30,000, so the gap between quotes can be large.

Mix and Match

Most of these ideas work best in combination. Three that suit common Australian homes:

  • Small budget, narrow room: single-sided flat-pack units, double hanging, a shoe tower at the end and plug-in LED strips.
  • Family robe for two: a galley with 1100 to 1200 mm between sides, his-and-hers halves and one shared drawer bank.
  • Walk-through to the ensuite: shallow shelves on one side to keep the corridor wide, closed drawers rather than open shelves, and a strong exhaust fan in the bathroom.
