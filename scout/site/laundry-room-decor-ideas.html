Laundry Room Decor Ideas: 16 Easy Ways to Style Yours

ByMohamed Elber October 17, 2026October 18, 2026

These laundry room decor ideas make the room you visit every few days feel like part of the home instead of a storage closet with machines. Most laundry rooms are small, plain and built for function, which is exactly why a few warm touches change them so much: a soft wall color, one good light, art you like and a rug under your feet.

Below you will find a simple decor map, a 6-step plan and 16 easy ideas for walls, shelves, floors, lighting and the counter, plus a before and after and the mistakes to skip. Every idea keeps the room practical, and the dryer and detergent notes keep it safe.

Key Takeaways

  • Quick answer: Start with a warm wall color, add one statement light, hang art on a ledge or shelf above the machines, lay a washable runner and style the counter with three pieces. Keep the area around the dryer clear of things that can burn, and keep detergent in its original container out of children’s reach.
  • Decorate up high and on the walls so the counter and floor stay free for sorting and folding.
  • Many ideas cost under $100, and several are renter-friendly.

Time: an afternoon to a weekend · Cost: $20–400 · Difficulty: Easy

Before You Decorate: The Laundry Room Decor Map

A laundry room has two jobs: washing and folding, and being pleasant enough that you do not dread it. Good decor supports the first job. Put decorative pieces where they will not get in the way, such as the upper wall, a ledge, the ceiling and the floor, and keep the counter mostly clear for folding. All the laundry room decor ideas below follow that rule.

Safety matters too, because a dryer makes heat and lint. The Idaho Department of Insurance’s clothes dryer safety tips say to keep the area around your dryer clear of things that can burn, like boxes, cleaning supplies and clothing, to make sure the exhaust vent pipe is not restricted and to turn the dryer off if you leave home or go to bed. So do not tuck baskets, fabric or decor around or behind the dryer.

Detergent is the other thing to watch. Glass jars of colorful pods look nice on Pinterest, but the American Academy of Pediatrics’ laundry room safety page advises keeping laundry products in their original containers with the original label, closing them tightly and putting them away out of sight and reach of children and pets. Decant only safe items, such as clothespins or wool dryer balls.

How to Decorate a Laundry Room in 6 Steps

  1. Clear the tops. Take everything off the machines and counter. Return only what you use each week.
  2. Pick a palette. Choose one wall color and one accent, such as clay and cream or sage and white, and repeat it in the rug, towels and art.
  3. Fix the light. Swap a bare bulb for a pendant or flush mount with a warm bulb.
  4. Decorate up high. Add a ledge or shelf above the machines for art, plants and pretty storage.
  5. Ground the floor. Lay a washable runner or rug with a non-slip pad.
  6. Finish the counter. Style three pieces and leave the rest free for folding.

Wall Ideas

Walls are where laundry room decor ideas make the biggest difference, because they are the largest surface you see. Pick one wall idea and let the rest of the room stay simple.

1. Paint the Walls a Warm, Washable Color

Builder-white walls make a laundry room feel like a utility closet. A warm clay, soft terracotta, sage or greige makes it feel finished, as in the room at the top of this guide. A limewash or limewash-look paint adds soft movement that hides scuffs; for a laundry room, choose a scrubbable satin or eggshell finish, or a limewash product rated for the room, because the walls get splashed and steamy.

2. Add Peel-and-Stick Wallpaper

One wall of small-pattern wallpaper behind the machines gives the room a focal point in an afternoon. Peel-and-stick paper is renter-friendly and easy to wipe. Keep it to one wall in a small room, and see the botanical wallpaper example in our laundry closet ideas for how it looks in a tight space.

3. Hang Art on a Picture Ledge

A slim ledge above the counter lets you lean framed prints instead of drilling a hole for each one, and you can swap art with the seasons. Vintage botanicals, landscapes or simple abstracts all suit a laundry room. Use frames with acrylic instead of glass if the ledge is above the machines, since washers vibrate.

Shelf and Wall Storage That Doubles as Decor

4. Style an Open Shelf

An open shelf on iron brackets holds the things you reach for, and styled well it becomes decor. Mix a wire basket of towels, a ceramic crock and glass jars of wooden clothespins or wool dryer balls, and leave a little empty space between groups. Our DIY floating shelves guide shows how to mount one securely.

5. Hang a Shaker Peg Rail

A wooden peg rail is both storage and decoration. Hang a linen laundry bag, a small basket for lost socks and a bundle of dried lavender, and the wall looks styled even on laundry day. Mount it into studs or with anchors rated for the weight, since a full laundry bag is heavy.

6. Make the Drying Rack Part of the Look

A wooden wall-mounted drying rack or a simple rod for hangers can be decorative when it matches your shelves. Choose wood or brass instead of plastic, and fold it flat when it is not in use so the wall looks calm.

Floor and Window Ideas

7. Try a Checkerboard Floor

A terracotta and cream or sage and white checkerboard floor adds character, and its pattern can make everyday lint and dust less noticeable. If you rent or cannot replace the floor, peel-and-stick vinyl tiles give the same look over an existing smooth floor.

8. Lay a Washable Runner

A runner softens a long, narrow laundry room and makes standing at the machines more comfortable. Pick a machine-washable or low-pile rug in a pattern that hides drips, and add a non-slip pad underneath, especially on tile. Keep it clear of the dryer door swing.

9. Hang a Cafe Curtain

A linen cafe curtain covers the lower half of a window for privacy while letting light in at the top. It is cheaper than a custom shade and makes a laundry room with a sink feel like a little farmhouse kitchen. Use a tension rod if you cannot drill.

Lighting Ideas

10. Add a Statement Light

One good light changes the whole mood. A woven rattan pendant, a brass schoolhouse light or a simple flush mount with a linen shade feels far warmer than a bare bulb. In a small room with a low ceiling, choose a flush or semi-flush fixture so it does not hang in your way. Hardwired fixtures should be installed by an electrician if you are not comfortable with wiring. A white beadboard ceiling, like the one above, adds texture overhead too.

11. Light the Shelves

A plug-in or battery LED strip under a shelf or upper cabinet puts light right on the counter, where you sort stains and fold. Pick a warm white strip so it matches the main light.

Counter and Finishing Touches

12. Style the Counter in Threes

A tall piece (a vase or crock), a low piece (a bowl or tray) and a basket look intentional without crowding the folding space. Group them at one end of the counter and leave the rest clear. This is the cheapest of all the laundry room decorating ideas here, because you can use pieces you already own.

13. Decant Only the Safe Stuff

Matching jars for clothespins, dryer balls and stain brushes look neat on a shelf. Keep detergent, pods, bleach and stain removers in their original containers, closed and stored in a high, locked cabinet, which the AAP says is best, and never on top of the washer or dryer.

14. Bring In Plants

A potted herb on the windowsill or a trailing plant on a high shelf makes a laundry room feel alive. If you have cats or dogs, note that the ASPCA lists golden pothos as toxic to both, so keep it high or choose a pet-safe plant. In a windowless room, a good faux stem looks better than a struggling real one.

15. Swap the Hardware

New brass or matte black knobs and pulls on cabinets cost little and make plain doors look custom. Repeat the same finish on the light, hooks and curtain rod so the room feels planned.

16. Add a Counter Over Front-Loaders

A butcher block or laminate counter over a front-loading washer and dryer turns two boxes into a styled workspace and gives you a place to fold. Check your machines’ installation guide for the clearance they need around and behind them, and use our washer and dryer space calculator before you buy. Common Laundry Room Decor Mistakes

Even simple decor can backfire. Avoid these:

  • Decor on top of the dryer. Machines vibrate and get warm; keep that area clear and decorate the wall instead.
  • Filling the counter. If you cannot fold on it, it is too full.
  • Pods in clear jars. They look like candy to small children. Keep detergent in its original container.
  • A rug that cannot be washed. Laundry rooms get drips and lint; pick a washable rug.
  • Only a bare bulb. Harsh light makes even a pretty room feel like a basement.
  • Too many colors. One wall color and one accent look calm in a small room.

Laundry Room Decor FAQ

How can I make my laundry room look nice on a budget?

Clear the tops of the machines, paint one wall, swap the bare bulb for a simple fixture, add a washable rug and style the counter with three pieces you already own. Those steps cover most small laundry room decorating ideas for around $150.

What colors are best for a laundry room?

Warm neutrals, clay, soft terracotta and sage feel cozy; white and pale gray keep a dark room bright. Pick a scrubbable finish, because laundry room walls get splashed and steamy.

How do I decorate a laundry room with no window?

Use a warm main light plus under-shelf LEDs, a light wall color, a mirror or art with landscapes to suggest a view, and faux greenery instead of real plants.

Can I put decor on top of my washer and dryer?

It is better not to. Machines vibrate, and dryer safety advice is to keep things that can burn away from the dryer. Put a counter over front-loaders or decorate the wall above instead.

Final Thoughts

The best laundry room decor ideas are simple: a warm color, one good light, art up high, a washable rug and a counter you can still fold on. Keep the dryer area clear and the detergent in its original container, and the room will be both pretty and safe. For another small room that loves bold decor, see our powder room decor ideas.
