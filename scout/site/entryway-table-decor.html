Entryway Table Decor: 15 Easy Ideas for a Welcoming Entry

ByMohamed Elber October 8, 2026October 20, 2026

Good entryway table decor does two jobs at once: it makes the first thing you see when you walk in feel calm and welcoming, and it gives keys, mail and bags a place to land. Without a plan, an entry table quickly turns into a drop pile. With one, it becomes the easiest corner of the house to keep tidy.

This guide gives you a simple five-part formula, a 6-step method and 15 ideas for styling a console or entry table, from mirrors and lamps to baskets, books and seasonal touches. It works for a wide hallway console, a narrow entry or even a small apartment with just a floating shelf by the door.

Key Takeaways

  • Quick answer: To decorate an entryway table, hang a mirror above it, add height on one side with a lamp or tall vase, place one tray for keys in the middle, bring in some greenery and tuck baskets underneath for shoes and bags, then keep the rest of the surface clear.
  • Size the mirror to about two-thirds to three-quarters of the table width and hang it about 6 to 10 inches above the tabletop (our rule of thumb).
  • Give everyday clutter a home first: a key tray, a spot for mail and baskets below keep the styled pieces looking good.

Time: 1–2 hours · Cost: $0–100 (more for a new mirror or lamp) · Difficulty: Easy

The Entryway Table Formula

Most good entryway table decor follows the same pattern. There is something on the wall (a mirror or art), something tall on one side (a lamp or a tall vase), a small drop zone for keys, something alive like greenery, and closed storage underneath. You do not need all five on day one, but each one solves a problem: the mirror adds light, the lamp adds a warm welcome, the tray stops keys from scattering and the baskets hide the things that would otherwise pile up.

A note on cords: if you need an extension cord to reach the lamp, do not slide it under the runner or across the doorway. Electrical Safety Foundation International’s extension cord safety tips warn that if a cord is covered, heat cannot escape, which may result in a fire hazard. Choose a table near an outlet or a cordless rechargeable lamp instead.

How to Decorate an Entryway Table in 6 Steps

  1. Clear the table completely. Remove everything, sort the mail and put away anything that does not belong in the entry.
  2. Decide what the table must hold. Usually keys, sunglasses, outgoing mail and maybe a charger. Give each one a tray, dish or drawer.
  3. Anchor the wall. Hang a mirror or lean a piece of art centered above the table.
  4. Add height on one side. Place a lamp or a tall vase near one end so the arrangement is not flat.
  5. Fill in with low pieces. Add the key tray, a short stack of books and some greenery, grouped rather than spread out.
  6. Use the space underneath. Slide in baskets or a small bench for shoes, bags and scarves.

The Basics: Size, Mirror and Light

1. Start With the Right Size Table

In a hallway, the table should not crowd the walkway. A console about 10 to 15 inches deep usually fits a narrow hall, while a wider entry can take something deeper. If there is no room for legs at all, a wall-mounted shelf works as a slim entry table (see idea #12). Our small entryway ideas have more layouts for tight spaces.

2. Hang a Mirror Above the Table

A mirror bounces light around a dark entry and lets you check your look on the way out. As a rule of thumb, choose one about two-thirds to three-quarters as wide as the table and hang it about 6 to 10 inches above the top so the two read as one unit. Mirrors are heavy, so hang them on studs or on wall anchors rated for the weight.

3. Add Warm Light With a Lamp

A table lamp is one of the most welcoming things you can put by the door, especially on dark winter evenings. Pick a linen shade and a warm white bulb for a soft glow, and keep the lamp at one end so the middle stays free for keys.

4. One Tray for Keys

Keys, sunglasses and earbuds need a home, or they spread across the table. A small wooden tray or a ceramic dish keeps them together and looks intentional. Make it the only place where small items land, and empty it once a week.

5. Greenery or a Plant

Fresh or faux greenery softens the hard lines of a mirror and table. Olive branches, eucalyptus or a small potted plant all work. If you have pets, check plants before you buy them: the ASPCA lists golden pothos as toxic to dogs and cats, so keep trailing plants like it out of reach or choose faux stems.

Entryway Table Decor Styling Tricks

6. Balance Without Matching

You do not need two identical lamps. A lamp on one side and a tall vase or dried stems on the other look relaxed and balanced. Keep the tallest pieces at the ends and the lower pieces toward the middle.

7. Lean Art Instead of Hanging It

A large framed print leaned against the wall feels casual and is easy to change with the seasons. It is also a renter-friendly option if you cannot put many holes in the wall. Layer a small object or a vase in front of the frame to add depth.

8. Stack Books and Add One Object

Two or three books stacked flat create a little platform for a small brass box, a candle holder or a dish. Choose books with plain linen or neutral covers so they look calm, and keep the stack low.

9. Group in Threes

A simple styling trick is to group items in threes with different heights: tall, medium and low. One grouping on each side of the key tray is plenty. If the table still feels busy, take one thing away.

Under and Around the Table

10. Baskets Underneath

The space under the table is where shoes, bags and dog leashes end up anyway, so give them baskets. Lidded woven baskets hide everything, and two matching ones look neat. Our shoe storage ideas and purse storage ideas have more ways to tame the drop zone.

11. A Stool or Small Bench

A small stool under or beside the table gives you a spot to sit while you put on shoes, and it can hold a basket or a bag when not in use. In a bigger entry, a bench next to the console does the same job. See our mudroom ideas for bench setups with hooks.

12. A Floating Shelf and Hooks in a Tiny Entry

No room for a table? Mount a sturdy floating shelf at table height by the door and hang a mirror above it. Add three hooks beside it for a tote, a scarf and a jacket, and put a basket on the floor for shoes. Our DIY floating shelves guide shows how to build and mount one.

13. A Runner That Ties It Together

A washable or jute runner in front of the table defines the entry and catches dirt from shoes. Use a non-slip rug pad underneath so it stays put, and keep cords out from under it, as noted above.

Practical and Seasonal Touches

14. A Home for Mail

Mail is one of the quickest ways to clutter a styled table. Use a slim letter holder, a small drawer or a lidded box for incoming mail, and sort it weekly. Recycle junk mail before it even lands on the table. Our guide on how to declutter your home has more tips for paper clutter.

15. Swap One Thing for Each Season

Instead of redecorating, change one grouping. In fall, add small pumpkins and a jug of dried wheat; in winter, pine sprigs and a few ornaments in a bowl; in spring, fresh tulips. When the holidays end, our guide to Christmas decoration storage shows how to pack seasonal pieces away neatly.

If you add candles, follow the U.S. Fire Administration’s candle safety tips: keep candles at least 12 inches from anything that burns and blow them out when you leave the room. Near an entry with dried stems, coats and a busy door, a battery-operated flameless candle is the easier choice.

Entryway Table Decor Mistakes to Avoid

  • Too many small things. A dozen little objects look like clutter. Choose a few larger pieces.
  • No place for everyday items. Without a key tray and a mail spot, real life will cover the decor within days.
  • A mirror that is too small. A tiny mirror over a long table looks lost. Aim for most of the table’s width.
  • Blocking the walkway. A deep table, a big basket sticking out or a loose runner can become a tripping hazard.
  • Open flames by the door. Coats, dried stems and drafts from the door make a flameless candle the safer pick.

Frequently Asked Questions

What should I put on my entryway table?

A mirror or art above it, a lamp or tall vase for height, a tray for keys, some greenery and baskets underneath. Keep the rest of the surface clear.

How high should a mirror be above an entryway table?

There is no strict rule, but about 6 to 10 inches above the tabletop usually makes the mirror and table look like one unit. With a taller mirror, also check that the center sits near eye level for the people who use it most.

How do you decorate a long, narrow console table?

Place taller pieces at the ends, such as a lamp on one side and a tall vase on the other, keep low pieces like a tray and books in the middle and add a wide mirror or art above.

How do I keep my entryway table from getting cluttered?

Give every daily item a home: one tray for keys, one spot for mail and baskets for shoes and bags. Spend a minute each evening clearing the table and sort the mail once a week.

The same simple formula of a tray, a few books and something living works in the living room too; see our coffee table decor ideas for the details.

Final Thoughts

The best entryway table decor is simple: a mirror, a little height, one tray, something green and storage below. Set up those five pieces, give mail and keys a home, and your entry will be easier to keep tidy and nicer to come home to.
