Christmas Decoration Storage: 18 Smart Ideas That Work

ByMohamed Elber October 2, 2026October 18, 2026

Good Christmas decoration storage starts the day you take everything down, not the week you get it back out. If you toss baubles, lights and garland into whatever boxes are nearby, next December you open a jumble of tangled cords, crushed ribbon and at least one broken ornament. A little system at packing time saves you an hour of untangling and a trip to the store for replacements.

This guide covers 18 practical ideas for ornaments, lights, wreaths, the tree and wrapping paper, plus where each item is safe to keep in a typical home. It works whether you have a basement and a garage or just a closet in a small flat, and it uses both US and UK terms (closet and cupboard, attic and loft) so you can follow along wherever you live.

Key Takeaways

  • Quick answer: Store Christmas decorations in lidded clear plastic bins sorted by category and labeled by room, wrap glass ornaments in acid-free tissue in divided boxes, wind lights on cardboard or a reel, and keep fragile items in a dry indoor closet rather than a hot attic or damp garage.
  • Check every light set for cracked sockets or frayed wires before you pack it, and throw out damaged sets instead of storing them.
  • Pack an “open first” bin with hooks, extension cords and the tree stand so setup takes minutes next year.

Time: 2–3 hours · Cost: $0–$80 (£0–£65) for the basics · Difficulty: Easy

Where to Store Christmas Decorations in Your Home

In Christmas decoration storage, where the bins live matters as much as what goes in them. Attics and lofts get very hot in summer, which can soften candles, loosen glued ornament caps and age plastic. Garages and sheds bring damp air, dust and pests that love cardboard and fabric. A closet or the space under a bed, inside the heated part of your home, is the safest spot for anything fragile or sentimental.

Lights deserve a quick check before they go into storage. The U.S. Consumer Product Safety Commission advises inspecting every light set, new or old, for broken or cracked sockets, frayed or bare wires and loose connections, and throwing away any damaged sets (CPSC holiday decorating safety guide). Doing it while you pack means nothing faulty goes back on the tree next year.

How to Pack Up Christmas Decorations in One Afternoon

  1. Take everything down room by room and bring it to one clear surface, like the dining table.
  2. Sort into groups: ornaments, lights, wreaths and garland, textiles, tree parts and wrapping supplies.
  3. Set aside anything broken, faded or unused for two years to repair, donate or throw away.
  4. Test and inspect each light set, remove batteries from battery-powered decor, and make sure everything is fully dry.
  5. Pack by group into labeled bins, heaviest items at the bottom and glass on top.
  6. Store bins in the right spot for what is inside, with the “open first” bin at the front.

Christmas Ornament Storage Ideas

1. Use a divided ornament box

A box with cardboard or fabric dividers gives every bauble its own cell, so nothing knocks together when the box is moved. Look for 2–3 in (5–8 cm) cells for standard baubles and keep one tray per layer. If you already have the cardboard trays your ornaments came in, keep them; they are made to fit.

2. Make a free organizer from egg cartons and cups

Small ornaments fit neatly in cardboard egg cartons, and clear plastic cups glued to a piece of cardboard hold slightly larger ones. Set the whole tray in a shallow lidded bin. It is one of the easiest Christmas ornament storage ideas if you want to spend nothing.

3. Wrap glass in acid-free tissue, not newspaper

Newspaper ink can rub off onto pale or matte finishes, and bubble wrap can trap moisture against painted surfaces. Plain white acid-free tissue is soft, breathable and cheap. Twist a little extra around hooks and caps, which are the parts that snap first.

4. Pack heavy items low and fragile items on top

Inside any bin, put sturdy, heavy items like wooden figures or shatterproof balls at the bottom, then a layer of tissue, then glass on top. On shelves, apply the same rule: heavy bins on the lowest shelf, light ornament boxes up high.

5. Give heirlooms their own small box

Keep handmade, vintage or sentimental ornaments in one small box stored indoors, never in the garage or attic. A shoebox-sized container in a bedroom closet is enough for most families, and it means your most precious pieces are never buried under the tree bag.

Ideas for Lights, Wreaths and Garland

6. Wind lights around flat cardboard

Cut a piece of sturdy cardboard about 8 x 12 in (20 x 30 cm), notch one end to hold the plug, then wrap the strand around it loosely. Write the length and the room on the card. Stack the cards in a basket or bin so you can grab the right set in seconds.

7. Use a cord reel for long strands

Outdoor strands and long tree lights wind faster on a reel, the kind sold for extension cords or garden hoses. Start winding from the far end so the plug ends up on the outside, ready to plug in first when you decorate.

8. Test and label every set before it goes away

Plug each set in for a minute, check it as described above, and add a tag: “Tree, warm white, 25 ft (7.6 m)” or “Porch, works.” Next year you will know which sets work and where they go without testing them all again on a cold evening.

9. Hang wreaths in a closet or round bag

Wreaths lose their shape when something heavy sits on them. Hang one from a padded hanger on a closet rod, or zip it into a round wreath bag and hang it on a sturdy hook. Our coat closet organization guide shows how to free up rod space for seasonal items.

10. Coil garland and store it loose

Coil garland in loose loops, never tight, and lay it in a wide bin on top of other items. For long faux garland, wrap it around a pool noodle or a cardboard tube so the branches keep their shape.

Christmas Tree Storage Ideas and Bulky Items

11. Store an artificial tree upright in a rolling bag

The original tree box often wears out after a few seasons. An upright bag on wheels lets you store the tree partly assembled in sections, so it takes less time to fluff next year. It fits neatly in a closet corner or the space under the stairs; see our under stairs storage ideas for ways to fit it in.

12. Keep the stand, skirt and topper with the tree

Put the tree stand, skirt, topper and any spare bulbs in a small labeled bag tied to the tree bag handle. Most “lost” tree parts are simply stored in a different bin from the tree itself.

13. Set up a wrapping paper station

Stand rolls upright in a tall basket or bin so they do not crush, hang ribbon spools on a wall-mounted dowel, and keep tape, scissors and gift tags in one tray. A station like this can live in a spare room or closet all year and works for birthdays too. For a full system for rolls, gift bags, tissue and ribbon, see our gift wrap storage ideas.

14. Store stockings and textiles clean and dry

Wash or air out stockings, tree skirts and table runners, then fold them into a breathable cotton bag or a lidded basket. Store them with your other linens; our linen closet organization tips help you make room for one seasonal shelf.

15. Dry outdoor decor completely before packing

Inflatables, outdoor lights and porch decor need to dry fully indoors, for at least 24 hours, before they go into bins. Packing anything damp invites mildew and a musty smell when you open the bin next year.

Build a System You Will Thank Yourself For

16. Use matching clear bins labeled by room

Clear, lidded plastic bins in one or two sizes stack neatly, show what is inside and form the backbone of any Christmas decoration storage system. Label each by category and room, such as “Living room, mantel” or “Kitchen, window lights,” so you can decorate one room at a time. For more ways to fit bins into tight homes, see our storage ideas for small spaces.

17. Pack an “open first” bin

Fill one bin with the things you need on day one: tree stand, extension cords, ornament hooks, a light tester, adhesive hooks, scissors and tape. Mark it clearly and store it in front of everything else.

18. Slide flat bins under the bed

Low, wide bins under a bed are ideal for ornaments and lights in small homes with no attic or garage. They stay at room temperature and out of the way. Our small bedroom storage ideas cover bed risers and other ways to gain height underneath.

Common Christmas Decoration Storage Mistakes

  • Keeping every decoration. Pack only what you used or truly love; a quick edit, like the method in our guide on how to declutter your home, can noticeably cut the number of bins you need.
  • Using cardboard boxes in the garage or basement. Damp cardboard sags, tears and attracts pests.
  • Storing candles or wax decor in the attic. Summer heat can warp or melt them.
  • Leaving batteries inside. Batteries left in decor for 11 months can leak and ruin the item.
  • Overfilling bins. If a lid has to be forced shut, the ornaments on top will crack.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the best way to store Christmas decorations?

Sort your decorations by category, pack them into lidded clear plastic bins labeled by room, and store the bins somewhere dry with a steady temperature, such as a closet, under a bed or a dry basement shelf. Fragile and sentimental items should always stay indoors.

How do you store Christmas ornaments so they do not break?

Wrap each glass ornament in acid-free tissue, place it in a divided box or a cell of an egg carton, and keep that box on top of heavier items. Do not overfill the container, and store it where it will not be knocked or crushed.

Is it OK to store Christmas decorations in the attic or garage?

Sturdy items such as an artificial tree, plastic bins of shatterproof ornaments and outdoor decor can go in an attic, loft or garage if they are sealed and off the floor. Keep candles, glass, photos, textiles and anything with batteries inside the home. In the UK, our loft storage ideas show how to board a loft without squashing the insulation.

How can I store Christmas decorations in a small apartment?

Use flat bins under the bed, the top shelf of a closet, and the back of a coat or linen closet. Choose a slim upright tree bag, wind lights on cardboard so they take little room, and let go of decorations you no longer use.

Final Thoughts

The best Christmas decoration storage is simple: fewer, better bins, clear labels, and the right spot for each type of decoration. Spend one afternoon packing carefully this year and next December starts with lights that work, ornaments that are whole and a tree you can set up in under an hour.

Start with the two items that cause the most trouble in your house, usually lights and glass ornaments, and add the rest of the system as you go. For year-round storage help, browse our DIY floating shelves guide for an easy way to add a seasonal shelf.
