Laundry Room Cabinet Ideas: 18 Smart Ways to Add Storage

ByMohamed Elber October 3, 2026October 20, 2026

The right laundry room cabinet ideas can turn a wall of loose bottles and a wobbly wire shelf into a room that feels finished. Cabinets hide the visual noise of detergent, cleaning sprays and spare towels, and when you plan them around the washer and dryer, they also give you a place to fold, sort and hang clothes.

This guide covers 18 practical ideas for upper, base and tall cabinets, plus small-room and budget options. Before you shop, it walks through the measurements that matter most around washers and dryers, the main cabinet types and what they roughly cost, and the safety rule every family with small children should know.

Key Takeaways

  • Quick answer: the best laundry room cabinet setup pairs wall cabinets above the machines with base cabinets or a tall cabinet beside them, so supplies sit behind doors and a counter gives you a folding spot.
  • Measure before you buy: front-loaders are about 39 in (99 cm) tall, and top-loaders need about 2 ft (61 cm) of clear space above for the lid, so your machine type decides whether a counter can go on top.
  • Keep detergent and laundry pods in a high, locked cabinet, never on top of the machines or in drawers under them.

Time: a weekend for flat-pack cabinets · Cost: about $250 to $2,000+ · Difficulty: Easy to moderate

Measure Around Your Washer and Dryer First

Laundry cabinets have to work around two large machines, so start with their sizes, not with the cabinet catalog. According to the Whirlpool laundry sizing guide, a typical front-load washer is about 27 in wide, 39 in tall and 32–34 in deep, a stacked front-load pair is about 78 in tall, and a top-load washer needs about 2 ft of clearance above it so the lid can open. The same guide suggests leaving about 1 in on each side and 6 in behind the machines for hoses and venting.

Washer and Dryer Sizes at a Glance

Add the gaps on top of these sizes: about 1 in at each side and 6 in behind. A side-by-side pair of full-size machines needs a space at least 56 in wide, Whirlpool says, before any cabinets. Sizes vary by model, so check the install guide of the machines you own.

Those numbers explain the most common planning mistakes. A standard kitchen counter is about 36 in high, which is lower than most front-loaders, so a counter over the machines has to sit higher. Front-loaders are also deeper than a standard 24 in (61 cm) base cabinet, so they usually stick out past a normal cabinet run unless you plan for it. To check your own room, try our washer and dryer space calculator.

Safety matters just as much as fit. The American Academy of Pediatrics laundry room safety advice says to put laundry products out of sight and reach of children and pets, notes that “a high, locked cabinet is best,” and warns against keeping them on top of the washer and dryer or in storage drawers under laundry machines. It also recommends keeping detergent in its original container with the label and closing it tightly after use.

How High to Hang Laundry Room Wall Cabinets

Measured from the floor to the bottom of the wall cabinet, these are the heights we work to:

  • Over front-loaders with a counter: the machines are about 39 in tall, the gap about 1 in and the counter 1 to 1.5 in thick, so the counter top sits near 41 in. Add the usual 15 to 18 in kitchen gap, and the cabinets start about 56 to 60 in from the floor.
  • Over a top-loader: the washer is 42 to 44 in tall and the lid needs about 24 in, so the bottom of the cabinet should be at least 66 to 68 in from the floor. Open the lid fully and measure, because lids differ.
  • Over a stacked pair: about 78 in of machines leaves roughly 18 in below an 8 ft (96 in) ceiling. After a small gap, that fits a cabinet about 14 to 16 in tall.

These are our own working numbers, built from the Whirlpool sizes above and standard kitchen spacing, not a building code. Before you fix anything, check that the cabinet doors clear the machine doors and that, except above a stacked pair, you can reach the lowest shelf without a step stool.

Laundry Cabinet Types and What They Cost

Most laundry rooms use the same cabinet boxes as kitchens (our kitchen storage ideas show more ways to use them): base cabinets (usually about 34.5 in tall and 24 in deep, before the counter), wall cabinets (usually about 12 in deep) and tall cabinets for brooms and bulky items. Where they differ is how they are made and sold.

  • Ready-to-assemble (flat-pack): the cheapest option. You build the boxes yourself, and they come in standard widths only. Good for a straight wall of base cabinets.
  • Stock cabinets: pre-built in standard sizes and finishes, sold at home centers. Quick to get and easy to install.
  • Semi-custom cabinets: standard boxes with more sizes, depths and finishes, which helps around deep machines or awkward corners.
  • Custom cabinets: built for your exact room. The most expensive, but the best choice for a full wall like the one in the photo above.

As a rough guide, Bob Vila’s laundry room remodel cost guide puts laundry room cabinets at around $250 at the low end and as high as $2,000 at the upper end, with countertops from about $300 to $1,400 depending on the material. Prices vary a lot by region and size, so treat these as a starting point and get quotes for anything custom.

What a Full Laundry Cabinet Project Costs

RTA Cabinet Store’s laundry cabinet guide gives a sample budget for an 8 x 10 ft laundry room: about $1,500 to $3,000 for cabinets, $800 to $2,000 for installation and plumbing, and $300 to $1,000 for extras such as a sink or pull-outs, or roughly $2,500 to $6,000 in total. The Bob Vila guide was last updated in 2023, so expect current prices to be somewhat higher.

Best Materials and Hardware for a Damp Room

A laundry room sees steam, spills and the odd leaking hose, so the materials matter more than in most rooms:

  • Cabinet boxes: plywood holds up better to humidity than particleboard. If you buy particleboard or MDF boxes, seal every cut edge.
  • Doors and finish: solid wood or painted MDF doors with a tough satin or semi-gloss paint that wipes clean.
  • Counters: quartz and laminate shrug off water and detergent. Butcher block looks warm but needs sealing, especially around a sink.
  • Hardware: soft-close hinges, full-extension slides for hampers and drawers.

How to Plan Laundry Room Cabinets in 5 Steps

  1. Measure the wall width, ceiling height and the exact size of your machines, including the open door or lid.
  2. Decide where you need a folding surface and where you need tall storage for a broom, mop or ironing board.
  3. Sketch the wall: machines first, then tall cabinets at the ends, then base cabinets and uppers in the gaps.
  4. Pick one high, lockable cabinet for detergent, pods and stain removers before you plan anything else.
  5. Choose moisture-friendly finishes and hardware, then order or build one section at a time.

What to Store in Each Laundry Cabinet

Leave at least 36 in of clear floor in front of the cabinets and machines so their doors open fully, as RTA Cabinet Store’s laundry cabinet guide recommends. Whirlpool suggests about 4 ft in front of a front-loader for loading.

Full-Wall and Upper Cabinet Ideas

1. Run cabinets from floor to ceiling around the machines

The most finished look is a full wall of cabinets with a built-in nook for the washer and dryer, like the white oak wall at the top of this guide. Tall cabinets at each end hold bulky items, uppers fill the space above the counter, and everything shares one finish, so the machines look built in rather than parked against a wall.

2. Use glass-front uppers for things you reach every day

Glass doors keep dust off towels and supplies but still let you see what is inside. Reeded or frosted glass blurs the contents, so slightly mismatched bottles look calm. Keep the glass cabinets for neat items such as folded towels and clothespins, and put mismatched cleaning products behind solid doors.

3. Add lighting under the wall cabinets

Uppers cast a shadow over the counter, which makes it harder to spot stains while you sort. A strip of LED lights under the cabinets fixes this. Plug-in and battery strips are an easy start; for wired lights, hire an electrician, and keep any cords well away from a utility sink.

Base Cabinet and Counter Ideas

4. Put a counter over front-loaders

A counter across side-by-side front-loaders gives you a long folding surface without losing floor space. Because most front-loaders are about 39 in tall, the counter has to sit higher than a kitchen counter, with a gap of about 1 in above the machines so their vibration does not shake it. Support it on base cabinets at each end and wall cleats along the back, never on the machines themselves. This only works with front-loaders; a top-loader needs the space above it clear.

5. Build in pull-out hamper baskets

Swap the shelves in a base cabinet for baskets on full-extension drawer slides. One basket for lights, one for darks and one for towels means laundry is sorted the moment it comes off the floor. Pick baskets with handles and liners so you can carry them straight to the washer.

6. Add a row of drawers for small supplies

Shallow drawers above the baskets hold the small things that clutter a counter: lint rollers, sewing kits, spare buttons, clothespins and stain sticks. Keep detergent and pods out of these low drawers if children are around; they belong in a high, locked cabinet instead.

7. Fit a utility sink into the base cabinets

A sink set into a run of base cabinets is handy for soaking stains, hand-washing delicates and rinsing muddy boots. The cabinet below hides the plumbing, and a sink base with doors leaves room for a bucket and spare sponges. A drop-in sink is the easiest to fit into a laminate counter, while an undermount sink suits quartz or stone; if you skip the cabinet, a wall-mounted or freestanding utility sink does the same job.

8. Mount a drying rack above the base cabinets

A wall-mounted rack above the counter, like the one in the budget photo below, dries delicates and towels without taking floor space. Choose one that folds flat against the wall, and hang it high enough that wet items do not drip onto the counter below.

Tall Cabinet Ideas

9. Give the broom and vacuum their own tall cabinet

A tall cabinet at the end of the laundry wall swallows the awkward items that never fit anywhere else: broom, mop, bucket and a stick vacuum. Wall clips keep the handles upright, and narrow shelves down one side hold sprays and cloths (our shelf spacing calculator sets the gaps). Painting just this one cabinet in a warm color, such as terracotta, turns it into a feature.

10. Hang the ironing board on the inside of the door

Mount a pair of ironing board hooks on the inside of a tall cabinet door so the board is out of sight but ready in seconds. Use a solid door with sturdy hinges and screw the hooks into the frame of the door, not just the thin center panel. If no door is wide enough, a pull-out ironing board that folds into a drawer does the same job.

11. Make one high cabinet the locked supply cabinet

If young children or pets visit your laundry room, choose one upper cabinet, or the top section of a tall cabinet, for detergent, pods, bleach and stain removers and fit it with a cabinet lock. This follows the AAP advice above and is one of the most important cabinet decisions in a family home. Keep each product in its original, labeled container.

Small Laundry Room Cabinet Ideas

Small laundry room cabinet ideas focus on depth and height rather than width. When there is no room for a full wall, these four tricks still add useful storage.

12. Pair base cabinets with open shelves instead of uppers

In a narrow room, a row of upper cabinets can feel heavy. Base cabinets with a counter, plus two open shelves above, give you useful storage that feels lighter. Keep the shelves tidy with matching baskets, and see our guide to DIY floating shelves if you want to build them yourself.

13. Add a hanging rod under the shelves

A short rod mounted under a shelf or upper cabinet lets you hang shirts straight from the dryer, so they do not wrinkle while waiting to be put away. It only needs about the length of a few hangers to be useful.

14. Fit a shallow cabinet above a stacked pair

A stacked washer and dryer is about 78 in tall, so in a room with an 8 ft (96 in) ceiling there is only a short space left above it. A shallow wall cabinet fills that gap with storage for spare towels, dryer sheets and refills. Hang it a few inches above the stack, and remember you will need a small step stool to reach it.

15. Slide a slim pull-out into the gap beside the machines

If you have a few spare inches between the machines and a wall, a slim rolling cart or pull-out cabinet made for that gap can hold sprays and stain removers. Leave the side clearance your machines need, and keep anything harmful higher up if children are around.

Budget Laundry Room Cabinet Ideas

16. Use flat-pack kitchen base cabinets

Ready-to-assemble kitchen base cabinets are one of the cheapest ways to add closed storage. Line them up along one wall, top them with a butcher block counter and add a simple drop-in or farmhouse sink if you have the plumbing. Seal a wood counter well, because a laundry room sees water and steam.

17. Paint the cabinets you already have

If the boxes are solid, a new color and new hardware can change the whole room. Sage green, warm cream and soft terracotta all suit a laundry room. Clean and sand the doors, use a primer, then a durable cabinet paint, and finish with brass or black pulls.

18. Look for secondhand cabinets

Kitchen remodels often leave good cabinets behind. Habitat for Humanity ReStores, local marketplaces and salvage yards can be a source of base and wall cabinets for a fraction of the new price. Measure carefully before you buy, and check that the boxes are not swollen from water.

Bonus Ideas: Hidden Machines and a Mini Mudroom

Hide the washer and dryer behind cabinet doors

Paneled or bi-fold doors turn the washer and dryer into one more cabinet, which is ideal when the laundry is part of a kitchen or hallway. Size the opening from the machine sizes above plus the side and back gaps, check that the cabinet doors open fully past the machine doors, and leave room for the hoses and dryer vent. Heat and moisture need a way out, so check your dryer’s install guide for closet airflow, and consider louvered doors or a gap above them. Our European laundry ideas show more machines tucked behind doors.

Turn one end of the laundry wall into a mini mudroom

If the laundry room is also the way in from the garage or backyard, give one end a bench, a row of hooks and open cubbies for shoes and bags, with a tall cabinet for coats. A wipeable floor and a basket for each person keep it tidy. Our mudroom ideas cover benches, hooks and boot storage in more detail.

Laundry Cabinet Colors and Styles

Two-tone cabinets are an easy way to make a plain laundry room look designed: navy, deep green or wood base cabinets with white or cream uppers keep the top of the room light. In a small room, light colors are the safer choice; dark cabinets work best when the walls, counter and floor stay light.

Shaker doors suit a classic or farmhouse look, while flat slab doors look more modern and are easier to wipe down. Brass or black pulls finish either style. For paint colors, wall art and finishing touches, see our laundry room decor ideas.

Laundry Cabinet Mistakes to Avoid

  • Putting a counter over a top-loader. The lid needs about 2 ft of clear space to open.
  • Using a standard 36 in counter height over front-loaders. Most are about 39 in tall, so the counter has to sit higher.
  • Resting the counter on the machines. Support it on cabinets and wall cleats instead, and leave a small gap.
  • Forgetting the depth of the machines. Front-loaders are deeper than standard base cabinets, and they need space behind for hoses and the vent.
  • Storing pods and detergent low. Keep them in a high, locked cabinet, not on the machines or in drawers under them.
  • Choosing finishes that hate moisture. Unsealed wood and raw particleboard edges can swell in a damp room.

FAQ

What kind of cabinets are best for a laundry room?

Most laundry rooms use kitchen-style base, wall and tall cabinets. Pick boxes with sealed edges and a wipeable finish, solid doors for cleaning products and glass doors only for neat items. Flat-pack cabinets suit a budget; semi-custom or custom cabinets suit rooms with awkward sizes.

How deep should laundry room cabinets be?

Standard wall cabinets are about 12 in (30 cm) deep and standard base cabinets about 24 in (61 cm) deep. Remember that front-loaders are deeper than a standard base cabinet, so plan for the machines to sit forward of the cabinet line or use deeper custom cabinets around them.

How high should cabinets be above a washer and dryer?

Above a counter, wall cabinets usually start about 15–18 in (38–46 cm) higher, as in a kitchen. Above a top-loader, keep about 2 ft (61 cm) clear for the lid. Above a stacked pair, which is about 78 in tall, there is usually only room for a shallow cabinet.

Can I use kitchen cabinets in a laundry room?

Yes. Kitchen base, wall and tall cabinets work well in a laundry room and are easy to find. Just check the depth around your machines, use a moisture-resistant counter and keep one high, lockable cabinet for detergent.

How much does it cost to add cabinets to a laundry room?

Cabinets alone run from about $250 to $2,000, with countertops another $300 to $1,400, according to Bob Vila’s guide (updated 2023). A fuller project for an 8 x 10 ft room, with installation, plumbing and extras such as a sink, is about $2,500 to $6,000 in RTA Cabinet Store’s sample budget.

Can you put a washer and dryer in a closet with cabinets?

Yes, if the closet fits the machines plus their gaps: about 1 in at each side and 6 in behind. A stacked front-load pair is about 27 in wide and 78 in tall, so a shallow cabinet can go above it. Check the dryer’s install guide for closet airflow and venting before you close it in.

What is the best layout for laundry room cabinets?

For most rooms, a single wall works best: machines side by side under a counter, a tall cabinet at one end and wall cabinets above. Leave about 36 in of clear floor in front, and keep detergent in a high, locked cabinet. In a narrow room, stack the machines and use the freed wall for base cabinets.

Final Thoughts

The best laundry room cabinet ideas start with the machines: measure them, decide where the counter and tall storage go, and choose one high, locked cabinet for anything harmful. After that, pick the ideas that fit your room and budget, from a full built-in wall to a few painted flat-pack base cabinets.
