Small Bathroom Storage Ideas: 25 Ways to Save Space (Even in Very Small Bathrooms)

ByMohamed Elber September 26, 2026October 19, 2026

Most small bathrooms don’t run out of space. They run out of planned space. The counter overflows while the wall above the toilet, the back of the door and the gap beside the vanity sit completely empty.

This guide gives you 25 small bathroom storage ideas, but more importantly, it shows you which ones fit your bathroom: how much space the ideas need, whether they need drilling, and roughly what they cost. Start with the quick-pick table, measure your four storage zones, then jump to the ideas that match.

Key Takeaways

  • Quick answer: The best small bathroom storage ideas use the walls, the door and the space above the toilet before adding floor furniture. Measure your four storage zones first, and choose no-drill options like an over-the-toilet étagère or tension pole caddy if you rent.
  • Keep daily items within easy reach and move backup stock high, low or behind a door.
  • Measure first so every organizer actually fits your bathroom.
  • Many of these ideas need no drilling, so they work in rentals too.

Time: 1 weekend (about 3–5 hours of work) · Cost: $20–150 · Difficulty: Easy

Quick Picks: The Best Idea for Your Situation

Step 1: Measure Your Four Storage Zones

Buying organizers before measuring is one of the most common mistakes. Every small bathroom has the same four hidden storage zones. Grab a tape measure and write down these numbers first:

Then sort what you actually store into two piles: daily use (toothbrush, skincare, hair tools) and backup stock (extra toilet paper, refills, cleaning supplies). Daily items get the easy-to-reach spots. Backups go high, low or behind a door.

Wall and Vertical Storage Ideas

When the floor is full, go up. The wall between the sink and the ceiling is often one of the largest unused storage areas in a small bathroom.

1. Floating Shelves Above the Sink

Two narrow floating shelves above the sink hold daily skincare and a small plant without using any counter space. Keep them shallow, around 4–6 inches deep, so you don’t bump your head when you lean over the sink.

Fits: any bathroom with open wall above the sink · Drilling: yes · Budget: $

2. A Leaning Ladder Shelf

A slim ladder shelf leans against the wall and stores rolled towels, baskets and toilet paper vertically. It doesn’t need to be mounted, which makes it one of the easiest storage ideas for renters; if it comes with an anti-tip strap, use it, especially with kids at home.

Fits: a free wall at least 12–16 inches wide · Drilling: no · Budget: $$

3. A Shelf Above the Door

The strip of wall above the bathroom door is usually empty. One long shelf there is perfect for backup stock you reach for less than once a week, such as extra toilet paper, spare towels and refills.

Fits: a door with wall space above the frame · Drilling: yes · Budget: $

4. A Mirrored Medicine Cabinet

If you have a flat mirror, replacing it with a mirrored medicine cabinet adds hidden storage at eye level while keeping the same look. Surface-mounted models don’t require cutting into the wall.

Fits: any bathroom with a wall mirror · Drilling: yes · Budget: $$–$$$

5. A Magnetic Strip Inside a Cabinet Door

An adhesive magnetic strip inside the medicine cabinet or vanity door holds tweezers, nail clippers and bobby pins, so tiny metal items stop disappearing into drawer corners.

Fits: any cabinet door · Drilling: no · Budget: $

Over-the-Toilet Storage Ideas

The wall above the toilet is prime storage space: it’s within easy reach, yet completely out of your walking path.

6. An Over-the-Toilet Étagère

A freestanding over-the-toilet unit adds three or four shelves with no installation. Before buying, check two measurements: the width between its legs must clear your toilet, and the lowest shelf must sit above the tank lid so you can still open it.

Fits: most standard toilets · Drilling: minimal (no wall mounting, but use an anti-tip strap, which usually needs one small screw) · Budget: $$

Want more options for this spot? See our full guide to over the toilet storage ideas, including a measuring guide.

7. A Wall Cabinet Above the Toilet

If you can drill, a shallow wall cabinet above the toilet hides mismatched bottles behind doors, which makes a small bathroom look calmer immediately.

Fits: bathrooms where you can mount into studs or use proper anchors · Drilling: yes · Budget: $$

8. A Basket or Tray on the Toilet Tank

A low basket on the tank lid holds spare rolls and hand towels. It’s one of the quickest upgrades on this list. Keep it light so you can lift it off when you need to open the tank.

Fits: any toilet with a flat tank lid · Drilling: no · Budget: $

Under-the-Sink Organization Ideas

The cabinet under the sink turns into a pile because nothing has a boundary and the pipes block standard shelves. These ideas work around both problems.

9. A U-Shaped Organizer That Fits Around Pipes

Pipes are the reason most under-sink organizers never fit. A U-shaped or adjustable two-tier organizer wraps around the plumbing and uses space that ordinary shelves can’t reach. Measure the distance between the pipe and each side wall before you buy.

10. Clear Pull-Out Drawers

Stackable clear drawers use the full height of the cabinet and let you pull items forward instead of digging to the back. Clear fronts mean you see what you have and stop buying duplicates.

11. A Tension Rod for Spray Bottles

Fit a small tension rod across the top of the cabinet and hang spray bottles by their triggers. It frees the whole cabinet floor for bins.

12. A Turntable for the Back Corners

A lazy Susan makes hard-to-reach back corners useful. One spin brings everything to the front.

13. Adhesive Bins on the Cabinet Door

Small bins stuck to the inside of the cabinet door hold brushes, a hair dryer or cleaning cloths. Check that they clear the pipes when the door closes.

Ideas 9–13 at a glance: all work in rentals · no drilling · budget $ each. For a full step-by-step method, see how to organize under the bathroom sink.

Door Storage Hacks

The bathroom door is one of the most overlooked storage surfaces in a small bathroom, and it’s ideal for renters because nothing gets drilled.

14. An Over-the-Door Pocket Organizer

A fabric or clear pocket organizer holds toiletries, hair accessories and first-aid basics. In a shared bathroom, give each person their own row of pockets.

15. Towel Hooks Instead of a Towel Bar

A row of over-the-door hooks holds two or three towels in the width one towel bar needs, though towels on hooks dry a little slower than towels spread flat on a bar.

16. An Over-the-Door Hair Tool Holder

Keep a hair dryer and straightener in a door-mounted holder instead of a tangled drawer. Always let hot tools cool before storing them, and choose holders made from heat-resistant material.

Tip: measure your door’s thickness first. Over-the-door hooks that are too narrow won’t sit flat, and thick hooks can stop the door from closing.

Shower and Bathtub Storage Ideas

17. A Tension Pole Corner Caddy

A floor-to-ceiling tension pole caddy adds three or four shelves in the shower corner without drilling into tile. Choose shelves with drainage holes to reduce soap scum and mildew.

18. A Second Tension Rod Inside the Shower

Install a second rod inside the shower and hang small baskets or S-hooks from it for loofahs, razors and bath toys.

19. Adhesive Shower Shelves

Strong adhesive shelves hold bottles at the height you actually use them. Apply them to clean, dry tile and wait the full curing time the manufacturer recommends before loading them.

20. A Bathtub Tray

A tray across the tub adds a surface for soap and a book, then lifts off when you clean.

Freestanding and Multi-Purpose Storage

In very small bathrooms, flexible pieces beat built-ins: you can move them, and they come with you when you move out.

21. A Slim Rolling Cart

A narrow rolling cart slides into the gap between the toilet and the vanity or wall, turning dead space into three shelves. This is the zone 2 measurement from Step 1: most slim carts need a gap of about 5–7 inches.

22. A Storage Ottoman or Bench

A small storage ottoman gives you a seat and a hidden box for towels or backup supplies in one footprint.

23. A Skirt or Shelf Under a Pedestal Sink

Pedestal sinks have no storage at all. A fabric sink skirt hides a basket underneath, or a narrow shelf can wrap around the pedestal.

Countertop and Small-Item Storage

24. A Two-Tier Tray

A two-tier tray doubles the counter space for daily products and makes cleaning a ten-second job: lift the tray, wipe, put it back.

25. Drawer Dividers and Clear Labeled Containers

Dividers stop small items from mixing into chaos, and labels mean everyone in the household knows where things go back. This is the idea that keeps all the others working.

A Simple 1-Weekend Plan

You don’t need all 25 ideas. For most small bathrooms, this order gives the biggest result for the least effort:

  1. Saturday morning: empty the under-sink cabinet, toss expired products, and measure your four zones.
  2. Saturday afternoon: buy only what fits your measurements, usually one under-sink organizer, one door organizer and one over-the-toilet solution.
  3. Sunday: install, move backup stock up high, keep only daily items on the counter, and label the bins.

Common Small Bathroom Storage Mistakes

  • Buying before measuring. Organizers that don’t fit become clutter themselves.
  • Keeping backup stock in prime spots. The cabinet you open every day should hold only what you use every day.
  • Ignoring the door and the space above eye level. These are often the most overlooked zones.
  • Using opaque bins for small items. If you can’t see it, you’ll buy it again.
  • Overloading adhesive products. Check the weight rating and respect the curing time, especially in a humid shower.

Small Bathroom Storage FAQ

How can I add storage to a very small bathroom without drilling?

Combine an over-the-toilet étagère, a tension pole shower caddy, over-the-door organizers, a leaning ladder shelf and a slim rolling cart. Together they can add a lot of storage with little or no damage to walls or tile, which makes them ideal for renters.

What is the best storage for a small bathroom with no cabinets?

Use vertical and freestanding pieces: shelves or a ladder shelf on the wall, an over-the-toilet unit, and a rolling cart or storage ottoman on the floor. Baskets on open shelves keep everything looking tidy.

How do I organize under a pedestal sink?

Add a fabric sink skirt with a basket hidden underneath, or use a narrow shelf that wraps around the pedestal. A small caddy on the floor beside the sink also works for daily items.

Where should I store extra toilet paper in a small bathroom?

Keep one or two rolls within reach, in a basket on the tank or a slim holder beside the toilet, and store the rest high up: on a shelf above the door or on the top shelf of an over-the-toilet unit.

Final Thoughts

A small bathroom doesn’t need a renovation to feel organized. Measure your four zones, pick the ideas that fit them, and give every item a home. Start with the under-sink cabinet this weekend, then move on to the door and the wall above the toilet. For a full system, see our bathroom organization ideas.
